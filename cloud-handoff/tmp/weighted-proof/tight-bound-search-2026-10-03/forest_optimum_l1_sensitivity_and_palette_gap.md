# Capped forest recourse: L1 sensitivity and the remaining palette step

**Status (2026-10-03): research lemma/candidate under the five-hour protocol.** The finite-palette conclusion is unresolved. No change to the manuscript or Lean files is implied.

## A sensitivity lemma for a fixed forest profit

Let `T` be a finite bipartite forest, `C_e>=0` fixed edge caps, `r_v>=0` variable vertex capacities, and `q_e` fixed profits. Define `Opt_q(r)` as the maximum of `sum_e q_e f_e` over `0<=f_e<=C_e`, `sum_(e at v)f_e<=r_v`. There is a generic perturbation `q'` of `q` such that the `q'` optimum is unique for every `r`, and for two capacity vectors differing only at one vertex `v` by `delta`, the corresponding optimal edge flows satisfy

`||f(r)-f(r')||_1 <= diam(T_v) delta`,

where `T_v` is the tree component containing `v`. For the finite set of actual heavy-state capacity vectors, `q'` can be chosen so close to `q` that its optimum is also `q`-optimal at every one of those states. (If `q` has ties, first select a generic tie-break direction and then a sufficiently small perturbation.)

Here is a direct alternating-path proof, supplied in the parent audit. Let `f,g` be the unique generic-profit optima before and after raising `r_v` by `delta`, and put `z=g−f`. At each tree vertex, pair positive and negative incident portions of `z`; the leftover portions are path endpoints with the sign of the vertex load change. Since the support is a forest, this conformally decomposes `z` into alternating signed simple paths. A path avoiding `v` at its endpoints can be added in a small amount to `f` and removed from `g`: box caps are respected because its signs conform to `g−f`, interior vertex loads do not change, and at either endpoint the old/new load difference supplies the needed slack under their common vertex cap. Optimality of both flows then forces the path's signed profit to be zero, excluded by a generic perturbation against all alternating simple-path signed costs. A path with `v` only in its interior has the same contradiction. A path ending at `v` with a **negative** load change also has the same contradiction because raising its capacity cannot obstruct that move. Thus every difference path starts or ends at `v` with positive load change. If `v` had positive slack in `f`, a small amount of such a path could again be added to `f` and removed from `g`, contradicting genericity. Hence `f` saturates `r_v` whenever the flow changes, and the sum of path masses is `deg_g(v)−deg_f(v)<=delta`. Each path has at most `diam(T_v)` edges, proving the bound. The same path argument proves uniqueness at **every** `r` after excluding finitely many signed path-cost hyperplanes; zero-capacity coordinates can be omitted.

For a matched heavy pair, one categorical state change alters the residual capacity of at most its two vertices, by at most one each. Applying the lemma twice gives `L1` change at most `2 diam<=4R` in the depth-residue-cut forest components of `bounded_degree_capped_localization_audit.md`, whose diameters are at most `2R`. A pair meets at most two components per forest. Average over forest decomposition and cut residues: the same `O(R)` global `L1` sensitivity holds for the averaged flow policy.

The profit-independent component policy can be chosen within this stable class. For each profit vector `q`, the generic-profit optimum selector just described is stable and has exactly the capped activation theorem's optimal support value at every heavy-state configuration. The expected feasible-flow set is a Minkowski sum of finite-state polytopes; exposed extreme mean vectors are obtained by per-state profit-optimal selectors. Thus its point meeting all coordinate lower bounds can be written as a convex combination of finitely many stable profit-optimal selectors. Pointwise averaging makes one feasible, profit-independent, `O(R)`-Lipschitz policy. This uses finite-state separation and needs a precise statement of the perturbation/vertex representation, but it avoids an arbitrary unstable selector.

## Why vertex totals may be enough

Use constant-length blocks of main colors. At each heavy pair and block, choose an independent random permutation of exact approximate bucket counts; the unplaced heavy identities use the usual `O(M/L)` heavy-only reserve. At a fixed color, pair states are independent with the desired rounded-down marginals. The stable capped policy gives per-color tiny flow `Z_e(c)<=C_e=O(w_e/M)` and expected total flow slightly above each physical tiny weight `w_e` after slack inflation.

If there are `M/L` independent blocks, an edge's total `S_e=sum_c Z_e(c)` has `Var(S_e)=O(L w_e^2/M)`, regardless of graph order. Therefore `E(w_e-S_e)_+=O(w_e sqrt(L/M))`, and summing at a vertex gives expected uncovered tiny **weight** `O(sqrt(LM))` when its total tiny stock is `O(M)`. Exact empirical coverage of every tiny edge is unnecessary if one can find a single block schedule with small uncovered weight at **every vertex**. Clip each edge's total fractional stock to at most `w_e`; this only lowers per-color loads. For physical edge `e`, give color `c` probability `x_(e,c)=Z_e(c)/max(w_e,S_e)` and give an uncolored option the remaining probability. A colored option contributes `w_e<=tau` to the two endpoint/color capacity rows; the uncolored option contributes `w_e<=tau` to the two endpoint residual-stock rows. **Every option meets at most two capacity rows**, so the manuscript's `lem:two` applies directly, even for a nonbipartite tiny support: it assigns one whole option per edge and increases each row by at most `2tau`. The reserved `3tau` capacity slack absorbs this. The residual stock degree after rounding is at most `U_v+2tau`, so the all-tiny cleanup lemma uses `o(M)` extra colors whenever every `U_v=o(M)`.

## The precise unresolved gap

The uncovered-weight row `U_v=sum_(e at v)(w_e-S_e)_+` is **nonlinear** in the independent heavy-pair block permutations. Forest `L1` sensitivity implies changing one block source changes `U_v` by `O(LR)` and the sum of its changes over all vertex rows by `O(LR)`. It also gives individual McDiarmid concentration for each `v`. But the manuscript's Harris–Srinivasan proposition controls **additive nonnegative rows** of independent sources, and a union bound over arbitrarily many vertices loses graph-order uniformity. A valid dimension-free simultaneous-selection theorem for these nonlinear bounded-difference rows, or an additive conditional-progress surrogate analogous to the manuscript's `Q_v`, is still required. Normalizing every edge separately does not help: a source can touch arbitrarily many tiny edges, so the column sum counts edges rather than total weight.

This gap must be closed before deriving an unrestricted physical finite-palette bound from the stable forest policy. The earlier explicit radius-one rule plus weighted defective types remains a separate conditional route.
