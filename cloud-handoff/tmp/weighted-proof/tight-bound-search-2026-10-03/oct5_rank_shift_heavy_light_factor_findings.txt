Structured rank-shift heavy/light factor test
5 October 2026. Scratch only; no new lower bound.

Attempted geometry. Use four vertices on each shore. The heavy types
form an 8-cycle: U_i--V_i and U_i--V_(i-1), with indices mod 4. All
sixteen U_i--V_j small types form K4,4; the edges with offset j-i in
{0,1} have weight .16 and the others weight .20. Thus each vertex has
two small edges of each size, and their total weight is .72. The heavy
cycle has weight sequence (.64,.60,.56,.64,.60,.56,.60,.56) in the
alternating edge order emitted by the script. Every vertex has two
heavy types. In each local bin packing, assign the two .16 edges to
the heavier heavy type and the two .20 edges to the lighter one:
both resulting bins weigh at most one. Since every heavy copy weighs
more than 1/2, the m-fold blowup has exact local bound beta=2m.
The .64 heavy types occur on opposite parity positions of the heavy
cycle, so a naive two-phase assignment would require the low-small
factor to track conflicting heavy phases.

The accompanying oct5_rank_shift_heavy_light_factor_probe.py solves
the nonnegative-profit polar for the canonical local point x_e=1/2.
It uses a mixed-integer pricing oracle with every type allowed its
full per-color integer multiplicity floor(1/w_e), and adds the found
global count patterns to a linear polar master. It terminated after
96 cuts at numerical polar value exactly 1. A prior sequence with
.64 types on the same heavy matching parity also terminated at 1
after 81 cuts. These are numerical diagnostics, not exact certificates
of gamma/beta=1 or of a general theorem.

The clear structural repair is the all-small one-copy pattern: each
vertex sees total small weight .72<1. There are also small 2-factors
that can accompany heavy matchings in many colors. Thus a merely
frustrated assignment of local heavy/small pairs does not remove enough
global one-color value; the full repeated-count oracle found no gap.
Any next candidate of this type should make the all-small pattern
infeasible while preserving a two-bin local packing. With two heavy
edges occupying both bins, however, the total small weight at each
vertex is at most the sum of their two residual capacities, strictly
less than one. Therefore that escape is impossible in this entire
two-heavy/two-full-bin architecture. One must add a third local bin,
use nonuniform profits/targets that exploit more than this canonical
half-point, or change the heavy support so local bins are not all
filled by heavy edges. The last sentence is a design constraint, not
an upper bound on arbitrary profits or arbitrary target points.
