# Why one-edge five-color repair does not tensor automatically

**Interim research note, no multi-fluid transfer theorem.** The one-fluid-edge `4/5` theorem in `two_matching_midpoint_single_fluid_edge_five_color_lemma.md` repairs a two-matching heavy segment by recoloring one copy of `M_0⊎M_1` into three matchings. For several disjoint fluid edges, the same recoloring must serve all pairs. The points below isolate the remaining compatibility problem.

Let the heavy segment be `x=p1_{M_0}+(1−p)1_{M_1}` where the symmetric difference of the two matchings is one alternating path or even cycle. For a fluid pair `uv`, put endpoint residuals `A_i,B_i` in full state `M_i`. Its two-state Jensen gap is `C_p=min(p d,(1−p)e)` when `A_0−B_0=d>0` and `A_1−B_1=−e<0`, with state labels/signs swapped as needed.

If neither endpoint is an endpoint of the alternating H path, each vertex is either covered in both full states or has a state-constant H state. If both are covered, every residual is at most `1/2`, so `d,e<=1/2` and `C_p<=1/4`. If one residual is state-constant, `d+e<=1`, again `C_p<=1/4`. An alternating cycle therefore has no pair with gap over `1/4`. An alternating path has at most two potentially hard fluid pairs because the fluid support is a matching and only its two path endpoints can cause larger gap.

For an **even-length** H path, its two endpoints are uncovered in opposite full states. A hard pair through the left endpoint requires `p>1/2`, while a hard pair through the right endpoint requires `p<1/2`, since in the covered state the endpoint-to-internal residual difference has magnitude at most `1/2`. Thus at a fixed `p` at most one pair is hard. For an **odd-length** H path, both endpoints are uncovered in the same full state, so two hard pairs can coexist.

A hard pair joining H-path endpoint `s` to an internal vertex `v` seeks an assortative three-color pairing: the H edge at `s` and the **heavier** of the two H edges at `v` should have the same color. The heavier internal edge must belong to the opposite full matching from the endpoint H edge, because otherwise the two-state min has no positive Jensen gap. The selected H edges are nonincident when the fluid chord is not itself a H edge.

One can add all fluid-pair edges as dummy edges to the multiset `M_0⊎M_1` and properly three-edge-color the resulting bipartite graph of maximum degree three. Ignoring the dummy edges afterward ensures that, at each fluid pair, one of the three colors is H-free at **both** endpoints. In the other two colors the original endpoint residual values are paired either as in `M_0,M_1` or swapped. This protects pairs whose endpoints are H-covered in both full states: the original pairing has the full-plus-empty slack `1−4C_p>=0`; the swapped pairing has gain `G>=d+e−1/2`, while `4C_p<=d+e`, so `1+G−4C_p>=1/2` in the opposing-sign case. If the endpoint differences have the same sign, `C_p=0` and the swapped gain is at least `−1/2`. The stronger `1/2` margin is **only for the swapped pairing**, not arbitrary pairings.

The remaining graph-coloring demand is to realize the assortative swap at every hard pair in one proper three-edge-coloring of `M_0⊎M_1` plus the dummy fluid matching. Two hard chords *alone* on an odd path were colorable in exhaustive path lengths `5,7,9,11,13`, but additional easy chords can block this overstrong requirement. For example, on H path `0--1--...--7`, take fluid matching chords `(0,5),(2,7),(4,1),(6,3)`. The endpoint chords can simultaneously be hard, yet no proper three-edge-coloring of the full augmented graph gives the two needed endpoint/internal H-edge equalities. The exhaustive check is a graph-coloring diagnostic; it does **not** show failure of the `4/5` mixed hull, because easy pairs may tolerate a color with no shared H-free slot. The parent agent also identified a shorter obstruction with H path length four and a direct H/fluid parallel pair, which is quantitatively benign.

Likewise, recoloring H alone arbitrarily can harm a pair whose initial full-plus-empty proof is tight. Endpoint residual lists `A=(0,1/2)` and `B=(1/2,0)` have `F_0=F_1=0`, `F_D=1/4`, and exactly tight empty-state compensation at `p=1/2`. Pairing augmented three-row lists `(0,1/2,1)` and `(1/2,0,1)` by permutation `(0,2,1)` gives only `1/2` total three-row recourse, whereas preserving a common free color gives at least one. Any multi-fluid proof must account for easy-pair slack when a hard-pair phase switch changes their color alignment.
