PHYSICAL HEAVY + TINY WEIGHTED EDGE COLORING: INDEPENDENT AUDIT
5 October 2026

STATEMENT (loopless multigraph; rational weights for polynomial-time wording)

Partition physical edges into precolored edges and tiny edges. The extension
lemma itself permits precolored weights anywhere in (0,1], provided their
K-coloring is proper; the intended heavy application has weights in
(1/2,1]. Tiny weights lie in (0,tau], with 0<=tau<1/4. Let H be maximum
HEAVY edge-count degree, L(v) the weighted heavy load at v, T(v) the
weighted tiny load, and d(v)=L(v)+T(v). Fix any proper K-edge-coloring
of the heavy submultigraph, with K>=ceil(3H/2) sufficient by Shannon.
Put c=1-4tau>0. If for every tiny edge uv,

  c^2 K >= T(u)+c[L(u)+L(v)]
  c^2 K >= T(v)+c[L(u)+L(v)],                         (A)

then the heavy coloring extends to a physical K-coloring of every tiny
edge with total weight at most one in each vertex-color row. In
particular, with N>=max_v d(v) and Lmax=max_v L(v), it suffices to take

  K >= max{ceil(3H/2), ceil((N+c Lmax)/c^2)}.       (B)

The original proposed weaker condition K>=(N+Lmax)/(1-8tau) for
tau<1/8 is also sufficient. Condition (A) is the sharper local form;
it equals c^2 K>=d(u)-4tau L(u)+c L(v) and its u/v swap.
If no tiny edges exist, only proper heavy coloring is needed.

FULL CHECK OF THE FRACTIONAL ASSIGNMENT

In color k, let h(v,k) be the weight of the unique heavy edge at v,
or zero. Properness guarantees uniqueness. The physical residual is
R(v,k)=1-h(v,k), and the reserved residual is
R'(v,k)=max(0,R(v,k)-4tau)=(c-h(v,k))_+.
For any a,b>=0, pointwise

  (c-a)_+(c-b)_+ >= c^2-c(a+b).                     (1)

If a,b<=c, the difference is ab>=0. If either exceeds c, the
right-hand side is nonpositive. Averaging (1) over the K colors gives

  M'(uv):=(1/K) sum_k R'(u,k)R'(v,k)
       >= c^2-c[L(u)+L(v)]/K.

Condition (A) gives M'(uv)>=T(u)/K and >=T(v)/K.
Because a positive tiny edge contributes to both T(u),T(v),
M'(uv)>0. Thus no zero denominator is possible for any tiny edge.

For tiny edge e=uv define its fractional load in color k by

  f(e,k)=(w_e/K) R'(u,k)R'(v,k)/M'(uv).

It is nonnegative and sum_k f(e,k)=w_e exactly. At vertex u and
color k,

  sum_{e=uv} f(e,k)
    =R'(u,k) sum_{e=uv} (w_e/K)R'(v,k)/M'(uv)
    <=R'(u,k) sum_{e=uv} w_e/T(u)
    =R'(u,k),

using R'(v,k)<=1. If T(u)=0 there are no incident positive tiny
edges and the row is vacuous. Set z(e,k)=f(e,k)/w_e. It obeys
sum_k z(e,k)=1, 0<=z<=1 and sum_{e∋v}w_e z(e,k)<=R'(v,k).
Only pairs with R'(u,k)R'(v,k)>0 are allowed in the rounding LP.

ITERATIVE ROUNDING AND ZERO SUPPORT

Repeatedly choose a basic feasible solution of the remaining LP.
Fix every z=0 permanently to zero and every z=1 permanently to one,
updating all remaining row capacities and edge equations. If no
fractional variable remains, stop. Let n be the number of currently
fractional variables and m the number of remaining edge equalities.
Each such equality has at least two fractional variables, because
its right-hand side is zero or one after fixing integral variables;
therefore m<=n/2. If t vertex-color rows are tight and independent
at the BFS, rank gives n<=m+t, so t>=n/2. Every fractional
variable occurs in exactly two vertex-color rows (its two endpoints),
giving at most 2n row incidences among tight rows. Some tight row
therefore contains at most 4 fractional variables. Drop that row and
repeat. (It is also enough to count all currently enforced rows; the
rank argument selects a tight one.) Fixing all zeros is essential:
otherwise new positive variables could appear in a dropped row and
invalidate the four-variable bound.

When a row is dropped, its fixed load plus current fractional load
obeys its residual capacity. Every remaining variable touching it
has weight <=tau, and there are at most four, so any later integral
assignment adds at most 4tau beyond the fixed load. Its final tiny
load is at most R'(v,k)+4tau. If R'(v,k)>0 then
R'(v,k)=R(v,k)-4tau, so the final tiny load is at most R(v,k).
If R'(v,k)=0, all incident z(e,k) were zero in the initial product
support, so that color receives no tiny edge at v. Thus every
physical row has heavy-plus-tiny load at most one. Each tiny edge
ends with exactly one assigned color. Rational LP/BFS steps and a
constructive Shannon coloring give polynomial time for rational data
when the chosen K is polynomially bounded. If the ceiling in (B) is
larger than the total edge count, simply give every physical edge a
different color; this uses fewer colors than (B) and is polynomial.

ORIGINAL COARSER CONSTANT AND SMALL IMPROVEMENT

The proposed estimate R'_u R'_v >= R_u R_v-8tau is valid, since each
residual drops by at most 4tau and all factors lie in [0,1]. Also
R_u R_v>=R_u+R_v-1, giving
M'>=1-[L(u)+L(v)]/K-8tau. This yields
(1-8tau)K>=d(u)+L(v) and its swap, or the global
K>=(N+Lmax)/(1-8tau). A direct bound on the maximum product loss
improves 8tau to 8tau-16tau^2, because
ab-(a-4tau)_+(b-4tau)_+ increases in both a,b in [0,1] and is
maximal at (1,1). The stronger inequality (1) further improves the
heavy-load coefficient, leading to (A).

ASYMPTOTIC READING AND LIMITS

Let A=Lmax/N, assuming N>0. Bound (B) is

  K <= max{ceil(3H/2), ceil([1+cA]N/c^2)}

when K is chosen minimally. If A<=1/2, then every heavy weight >1/2
implies H<2Lmax<=N, so this gives
K<=[(1+c/2)/c^2]N+O(1)
 = (3/2+10tau+O(tau^2))N+O(1).
The coefficient 10tau follows from c=1-4tau:
(1+c/2)/c^2=(3/2-2tau)/(1-8tau+16tau^2).
This is a physical coloring result for arbitrary tiny-edge topology
and arbitrary heavy multigraph, provided there are no medium-weight
edges. It does not imply the same factor for unrestricted weights.
For bipartite heavy support, K>=H suffices in place of Shannon's
ceil(3H/2); the fractional and rounding arguments are unchanged.

LITERATURE SCOPE CHECK (not a novelty certification)

A targeted search found the related weighted-edge-coloring literature,
including Debarsho Sannyasi, "Improved Approximation Algorithms for
Weighted Edge Coloring of Graphs" (2020),
https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.15056. Its abstract studies the same
per-vertex/color capacity-one definition but reports different online
and graph-specific offline results; it does not state this specific
heavy-plus-tiny extension. Constructive polynomial Shannon coloring
is documented by "Edge-Coloring Algorithms for Bounded Degree
Multigraphs" (2023), https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06579.
This search is insufficient to claim the present result is new or
absent from other literature.
