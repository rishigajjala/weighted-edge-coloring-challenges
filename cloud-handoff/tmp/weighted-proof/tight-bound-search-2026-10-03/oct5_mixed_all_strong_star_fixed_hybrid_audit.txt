ALL-STRONG MIXED STAR PREFIXES UNDER A UNIVERSAL FIXED HYBRID
5 October 2026

STATUS

Proof candidate independently derived and algebraically checked here;
the k=1 base case is independently audited in
oct5_mixed_one_strong_group_fixed_hybrid_audit.txt. For every profit
prefix of one divisible-fluid star whose positive leaf guard groups
are ALL strong (K Y_i>=1), this note proves expected unit-profit
recourse at least alpha times total prefix mass under one fixed heavy
law: 3/4 independent categorical heavy sampling plus 1/4 one common
occupancy threshold, always at alpha=2/3. All occupied physical
weights are in [1/2,1]. The center guard is distinct from the
external leaf groups; a center-paired leaf needs a separate add-on.
Weak leaf groups are not handled. This alone is not a full star forest
law, a branching theorem, or a physical palette theorem.

ENDPOINT AND LOCAL REDUCTIONS

The arbitrary-graph concavity argument in
oct5_endpoint_reduction_universal_mixed_law.txt lowers recourse when
each occupied categorical residual is replaced by a mean-preserving
spread to {0,1/2}. Do this only for proof; the physical heavy law
keeps exact original identity marginals.

Let the prefix have k strong guard groups, masses Y_i>0, total
Y=sum_i Y_i<=1. At group i its largest leaf has fraction
t_i in [1/2,1] of Y_i, and its endpoint half residual supplies
z_i=1/(2t_i) in [1/2,1] units to a fully free center. Let
A=alpha(1-Y), B_i=alpha(1-t_iY_i). As in the one-group audit, the
adverse center case takes whole-star load S=Y and all center/leaf
weighted loads saturated, using only additions of unit-type presence
or conversions of half type to unit. These operations stochastically
lower pair residuals conditional on every common uniform threshold.
An actually unguarded center or leaf can receive a proof-only virtual
guard pair to a dummy vertex under that same threshold; the virtual
residual is at most the physical residual one. This comparison never
alters the actual heavy identity marginals.

Write p for center occupancy probability, r_i for group i occupancy
probability, h_i for its conditional half-type fraction, and h_c for
the center fraction. Then

  p h_c=2(p-A), p in [A,min(alpha,2A)];
  r_i h_i=2(r_i-B_i), r_i in [B_i,min(alpha,2B_i)].

Put r=min_i r_i and Rprod=prod_i r_i. Conditional on all leaf pairs
being occupied, group i carries 0 if unit type and z_i if half type.
If any pair is absent, that group alone carries one unit, so the
truncated leaf aggregate is one. Let

  P0=prod_i(1-h_i),
  Q0=sum_i h_i(1-z_i)prod_{j!=i}(1-h_j),
  L=P0+Q0.

Indeed the aggregate's only deficit occurs when every pair is unit
(deficit one) or exactly one pair is half and all others unit
(deficit 1-z_i); two half groups already supply at least one.
Thus L is its conditional deficit when all pairs are occupied.

INDEPENDENT AND COMMON MODE FORMULAS

In independent mode, the leaf aggregate has expected rank
1-Rprod L. When the center is half residual it carries 1/2 iff at
least one group is not unit; this has probability 1-Rprod P0.
Center half-state probability is p h_c=2(p-A), and center free-state
probability is 1-p. Hence independent recourse is

  I=(1-A)(1-Rprod P0)-(1-p)Rprod Q0.           (1)

Its derivative in p is Rprod Q0>=0. In common mode all leaf groups
are occupied below r, and above r at least one group is absent, so
leaf rank is one. If p<=r, exact common recourse is

  C=1-A-(r-A)P0-(r-p)Q0,                     (2)

whose derivative in p is Q0>=0. Therefore the hybrid H=(3I+C)/4
is worst at p=A in this region, where the center is a unit guard:

  H=1-A-L[(3/4)(1-A)Rprod+(1/4)(r-A)].     (3)

Since 1-A-alpha Y=1/3, it suffices to show the bracketed failure
term times L is at most 1/3.

For p>=r, the common event U>p makes center and at least one leaf
group fully free, so C>=1-p. In all cases C>=1/3 from the interval
U>max(p,r_1,...,r_k).

TWO LEAF-DEFICIT BOUNDS

Let Z_i be group i's rank in independent mode: 0 for unit, z_i for
half, and one for absent. Pointwise

  1-min(1,sum_i Z_i) <= prod_i(1-Z_i).

The groups are independent, and

  E(1-Z_i)=r_i(1-z_i h_i)
           =B_i+(1-1/t_i)(r_i-B_i)<=B_i.

Thus Rprod L<=prod_i B_i. More generally I is at least
(1-A)(1-prod_i B_i) because min(R_center,leaf_rank) is at least
R_center times leaf_rank and the independent center mean is 1-A.

Choose an index i with r_i=r. Conditional on all pairs occupied,
the leaf aggregate rank is at least group i's rank Z_i, hence

  L<=1-z_i h_i<=1-h_i/2=B_i/r.

Also B_i>=A because t_iY_i<=Y; thus r>=A.

MERGING PRODUCT FACTORS

Each B_j<=alpha(1-Y_j/2). For masses a,b>=0 with a+b<=1,

  alpha^2(1-a/2)(1-b/2)
       <=alpha(1-(a+b)/2).

After division by alpha, the difference is
(2-a-b-ab)/6>=0. Repeated merging gives

  prod_i B_i<=alpha(1-Y/2),

and, for k>=2 with chosen group i of mass y,

  prod_{j!=i} B_j<=alpha(1-(Y-y)/2).

BASELINE: Y<=1/2

The product bound gives I>=(1-A)[1-alpha(1-Y/2)]
=(1+2Y)(1+Y)/9. Combined with C>=1/3, the hybrid minus
alpha Y is at least (2Y-1)(Y-2)/12>=0 for Y<=1/2.

CASE p>=r, Y>1/2

Now p<=2A=4(1-Y)/3, so C>=1-p>=1-2A=(4Y-1)/3.
The same independent baseline gives hybrid gap at least
Y(2Y-1)/12>=0.

CASE p<=r, Y>1/2, k>=2

Use (3), Rprod L<=prod B_j, and L<=B_i/r:

  (3/4)(1-A)Rprod L+(1/4)(r-A)L
    <=(3/4)(1-A)prod B_j
      +(1/4)B_i(1-A/r).

As r<=alpha and A=alpha(1-Y), the last parenthesis is at most Y.
Use the product merge over j!=i and B_i<=alpha(1-y/2). The failure
term is at most

  g(Y,y)=(2/3)(1-y/2)
       *{(1+2Y)/6*[1-(Y-y)/2]+Y/4}.

Direct differentiation gives

  partial g/partial y=[Y(Y-1)-y(2Y+1)]/18<=0

for 0<=y<=Y<=1. Hence g(Y,y)<=g(Y,0), and

  1/3-g(Y,0)=(Y-2)(Y-1)/9>=0.

Thus H>=alpha Y. The proof even works for all Y in this branch,
though the preceding baseline already handles Y<=1/2.

CASE k=1

The product merge over other groups is unavailable. The exact
one-group hybrid proof is in
oct5_mixed_one_strong_group_fixed_hybrid_audit.txt and covers all
Y,t, including zero-denominator endpoints by the baseline.

WHAT REMAINS

To make this a full star theorem, weak leaf groups (K Y_i<1) must be
absorbed while preserving the fixed-hybrid bound; the center's own
guard partner as a prefix leaf must also be handled. After that,
star additivity and support-function separation would produce one
fluid convex-hull law for unequal vertex-disjoint stars, even when
guard pairs link distinct stars. Arbitrary branching fluid support
and a physical palette realization would still be separate.
