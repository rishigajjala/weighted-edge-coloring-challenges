AUDIT: ONE STRONG MIXED LEAF GROUP UNDER UNIVERSAL FIXED HYBRID
5 October 2026

STATUS AND SCOPE

The proposed one-group argument passes independent algebraic audit. It
proves the 2/3 unit-profit prefix recourse inequality for ONE star
prefix whose leaves lie in exactly one heavy guard pair distinct from
the center guard pair, with weight in [1/2,1], categorical types,
target mass Y, and cap sum strong enough that K Y>=1. The heavy law
is the universal 3/4 independent + 1/4 common occupancy-threshold law
at alpha=2/3, with categorical types independent of the occupancy
uniform conditional on selection. It is a fixed law, so it would glue
across unequal stars IF the multi-group prefix inequality were proved.
This note does not prove that multi-group inequality, a star forest
theorem, arbitrary branching, or a physical palette transfer.

PRELIMINARY REDUCTIONS

The endpoint-reduction lemma in
oct5_endpoint_reduction_universal_mixed_law.txt permits replacing
occupied residuals in [0,1/2] by endpoint values 0 or 1/2 for a
lower bound on expected recourse. Thus write each pair's original
unit-type presence a and half-type presence b. Let p=alpha(a_c+b_c)
for the center pair and r=alpha(a_l+b_l) for the leaf pair. Let
h_c=b_c/(a_c+b_c), h_l=b_l/(a_l+b_l), with zero-presence cases by
continuity. Then p,r<=alpha and residuals lie in {0,1/2,1}.

The center whole-star load S is at least prefix mass Y. Greater S
only tightens its guard allowance, so the adverse center case has
S=Y. It is legitimate to saturate each pair's weighted-load local
constraint. To do so without increasing recourse, add unit-type
presence while the pair's total presence is below one. If a weighted
load gap remains when total presence reaches one, convert half-type
presence to unit type at fixed total presence. Adding unit presence
stochastically lowers the pair residual at every common-mode uniform
value U: the old occupied interval gets more unit type and the new
occupied interval replaces free by occupied. Converting half to unit
also lowers the residual pointwise in stochastic order conditional
on U. Independent mode is likewise monotone. The procedure reaches
the allowed weighted load because its maximal possible load is one.

Put A=alpha(1-Y), B=alpha(1-tY), where t in [1/2,1] is the largest
leaf's target fraction. After saturation,

  p(1-h_c/2)=A,       r(1-h_l/2)=B,
  p in [A,min(alpha,2A)],  r in [B,min(alpha,2B)].

The group can carry Z=min(1,R_l/t) into a free center; for an endpoint
half residual it carries z=1/(2t) in [1/2,1]. The exact lower-model
payoff table is in the endpoint note. The independent-mode recourse I
is at least E[R_c]E[Z]>=(1-A)(1-B), because
min(R_c,Z)>=R_c Z and E[Z]>=1-B. In common mode the interval where
both pairs are free has length at least 1/3, so C>=1/3. Write
H=(3/4)I+(1/4)C. We seek H>=alpha Y.

CASE 1: Y<=1/2

Since t>=1/2,

  H >= [(1+2Y)(1+Y)+1]/12.

The difference from 2Y/3 is

  (2-5Y+2Y^2)/12=(2Y-1)(Y-2)/12>=0.

CASE 2: Y>1/2 AND p>=r

Here p<=2A, since 2A<alpha. Whenever U>p, both pairs are fully
free, so C>=1-p>=1-2A=(4Y-1)/3. With the independent baseline and
t>=1/2, direct subtraction gives

  H-alpha Y >= Y(2Y-1)/12>=0.

CASE 3: Y>1/2 AND p<=r

The endpoint payoff table gives

  I=(1-p)(1-r+z r h_l)+(p h_c/2)(1-r+r h_l),
  C=1-r+z(r-p)h_l+(p h_c h_l/2).

Substitute p h_c=2(p-A), r h_l=2(r-B). Both I and C are
nondecreasing in p in this region: their p slopes are respectively
(2-1/t)(r-B) and (2-1/t)(r-B)/r, both nonnegative. Since A<=p
and A<=B<=r, replacing p by A is adverse. Then h_c=0, and
H as a function of r has the form

  H(r)=constant+L r+Q/r,
  L=(1/t-1)(1-3A/4),   Q=AB/(4t).

The derivative H'(r)=L-Q/r^2 is increasing in r>0. Its sign at r=B
is the sign of

  D=2(1-t)(1-tY)(1+Y)-(1-Y).

The coarse baseline above already proves the target if
t>=t0=(3Y-1)/[Y(1+2Y)]. In the remaining region t<t0,
D decreases with t, so D(t)>=D(t0). Exact simplification gives

  D(t0)=[8Y^3-Y^2-7Y+4]/[Y(1+2Y)^2].

For Y>=1/2, the numerator is at least
3Y^2-7Y+4=(1-Y)(4-3Y)>=0, since the difference is
4Y^2(2Y-1)>=0. Therefore H'(r)>=0 throughout r>=B.
Its minimum is at r=B (pure unit leaf guard). At p=A,r=B,

  H=(1+2tY)(1+Y)/6
   >=(1+Y)^2/6
   =2Y/3+(1-Y)^2/6.

This proves the one-strong-group inequality in all cases. The only
potential division by zero is B=r=0, which requires Y=t=1; that
endpoint is already covered by the coarse baseline, so it is never
used in the derivative branch.

UNRESOLVED ASSEMBLY

The unit-weight star proof combines an independent-mode product
estimate with a common-mode contribution from one strongest group.
For mixed weights the common contribution can be below the unit
activation bound, as shown by the legal center-half/leaf-unit example
in oct5_unequal_star_common_law_audit.txt. This one-group theorem
budgets the independent and common modes together, but adding other
groups changes both terms. No valid absorption or telescoping rule
has yet been shown to turn this theorem into a multi-group prefix
bound. That is the next required step for unequal star forests.
