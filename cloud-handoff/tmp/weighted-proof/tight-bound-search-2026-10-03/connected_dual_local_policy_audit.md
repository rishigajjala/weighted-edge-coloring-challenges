# Connected dual witnesses and a tentative local capped policy

**Status (2026-10-03): exploratory, no integer theorem.** This note isolates two possible ways around the graph-order dependence in the finite-palette step and records a failure of a naive local policy under heavy-pair correlation.

## Connected profit witnesses

Fix an exact heavy schedule `H` and a nonnegative tiny-edge profit vector `q`. Delete every tiny edge with `q_e=0`. If the resulting positive-profit graph has vertex-disjoint connected components `G_i`, then the capped recourse in every color splits: `F_q(R^c(H),C)=sum_i F_(q|G_i)(R^c(H),C|G_i)`. The target `q·w` splits likewise. Therefore a strict palette support deficit for `q` implies a strict deficit for at least one connected positive-profit component. This is exact but does not bound the component size.

For normalized `q·w=1`, let `a_P=sum_{e incident to P}q_e w_e` for heavy pair `P`. Then `sum_P a_P<=2`, and minimal caps give pair sensitivity `B_P<=12a_P/N` in one color. Exact-injection concentration for any fixed `q` consequently has tail `exp[-c t^2 N/sum_P a_P^2]`. The effective support `1/sum_P a_P^2` improves the exponent for diffuse profit. However the family of connected positive-profit witnesses can still have unbounded cardinality and size. A union bound over witnesses, even after aggregating parallel tiny edges, is not graph-order independent. A local resampling/discrepancy argument must exploit the total tiny stock at each vertex, not merely graph degree.

A useful source-size split is available: at a vertex `v`, at most `1/delta` neighboring heavy pairs contribute more than `delta N` total tiny stock each. A remaining pair contributes at most `12delta` to the sum of minimal edge caps at `v` in a color. This identifies finitely many influential neighbors per vertex at a fixed scale, but a direct Harris--Srinivasan concentration estimate for the remaining sources loses `sqrt(delta log N)`, so fixed `delta` does not by itself give vanishing local error. This is a multiscale lead, not a proof.

## A restricted case with no simultaneous-witness gap

If the *simple tiny support* is a matching, parallel physical tiny edges on one pair can be aggregated into a bundle of total stock `T_e`; different bundles use disjoint vertices. Fix the one-color categorical heavy law with each physical heavy identity occurring with probability `1/M`. For every matched heavy pair, list its `M` actual states (each heavy identity once, plus empty states) and sort by the pseudo-residual capacity in the same global color order. The empirical marginal at each heavy pair is exact. For a tiny bundle joining different heavy pairs, its maximum per-color capped load is `min{C_e,R_u,R_v}`, with `C_e=12T_e/N` up to palette scaling. This is a supermodular function of the two scalar residuals, so the comonotone coupling maximizes its expectation among all couplings with the given marginals. Its palette sum therefore dominates the product-law expected sum from the capped one-color theorem. For a tiny bundle internal to one heavy pair, both endpoints have the same residual and no cross-pair coupling is needed. Since bundles are vertex-disjoint, allocate each bundle's stock independently across its feasible colors, then split its load among parallel physical edges in proportion to their weights. A physical edge's per-color fraction remains `O(1/N)` and the finite source table has exact stocks. The existing `tau`-slack physical rounding yields a candidate integer `(4/3+O(tau))N` bound in this matching-tiny-support case, uniformly in the number of heavy pairs. The argument does not extend to a tiny vertex incident with several bundles: comonotone residuals can align all demands in too few colors.

## A radius-one policy to investigate

For independent binary vertex activation on a bipartite graph, take a feasible target `y`, degree bounds `D`, and `p_v=1/4+3D_v/4`. Put

`b_e=min{(4/3)y_e/(p_up_v),12y_e}` for `e=uv`.

When the activation bits are `A_v`, set `L_v=sum_{e=uv} b_e A_u A_v` and

`z_e(A)=b_e A_u A_v / max{1,L_u,L_v}`.

This is always feasible at every vertex and obeys `z_e<=12y_e`. Unlike the optimal recourse policy, each `z_e` depends only on activation bits at distance at most one from its endpoints. It remains **unproved** that `E z_e >=(3/4)y_e` for all feasible `(D,y)`. Numerical scans of 60,000 random 3-by-3 and 30,000 random 4-by-4 bipartite instances found no ratio below `0.75`; the observed minima were within `10^-6` above `0.75`. These probes do not establish a theorem, and values with extremely tiny `y_e` are floating-point sensitive. The coefficient `4/3` matters: using coefficient `1` fails on a two-spoke star with ratio about `0.731`, and coefficient `4` fails on tested small graphs.

The inequality is sharp on an isolated edge `y_e=D_u=D_v=1/3`: both endpoints activate with probability `1/2`, and the policy sends one unit when both activate, giving `E z_e/y_e=3/4`. There is a nonisolated equality family. Keep `y_e=1/3`, add a spoke of target `b` at `u` to an always-active leaf `x` (`D_x=1`), and set `D_u=1/3+b`, `D_v=1/3`. For small positive `b`, `p_u=1/2+3b/4`, `p_v=1/2`, and the two tentative flows are `t_e=(8/9)/p_u`, `t_f=(4b/3)/p_u`. Whenever `e` is active it receives `t_e/(t_e+t_f)=1/(1+3b/2)`, so `E z_e=(p_u/2)/(1+3b/2)=1/4` exactly. If instead `D_x=b`, high-precision enumeration gives `E z_e/y_e=3/4+(81/16)b^2+O(b^3)` at `y_e=1/3`; the first-order gain and loss cancel. Script: `local_star_critical_probe.py`.

A tempting simpler Jensen proof for coefficient `K=2` is false although the policy itself has not been disproved. It would require `E[max(1,L_u,L_v)|A_u=A_v=1] <= (4/3)min(2,12p_up_v)`. With an infinitesimal probe edge between two always-active centers, one center having three equal spokes of target `1/3` and the other four equal spokes of target `1/4`, all leaf capacities minimal, the corresponding normalized maximum satisfies `E max(1/2,S_3,S_4)=1.3337182998657227>4/3`, where `S_3=(2/3)Bin(3,1/2)` and `S_4=(4/7)Bin(4,7/16)`. Thus the Jensen sufficient condition misses the reciprocal expectation by a small but decisive amount. Script: `k2_jensen_probe.py` tests random finite instances, while this exact family was identified in the parent audit.

For a positive-profit external tiny edge `e=u_Lv_R`, conditional on `A_u=A_v=1`, the two incident tentative loads depend on disjoint other-shore activation sets and are independent under fully independent activations. When activation bits within a *third* matched pair `(a_L,b_R)` are changed from independent to comonotone, `a` can enter only `L_v`, and `b` only `L_u`. The function `(x,y) -> 1/max(1,x,y)=min{1/max(1,x),1/max(1,y)}` is supermodular because `min` of two monotone same-direction scalar functions has nonnegative rectangular cross difference. Thus comonotone correlation of a third pair cannot lower `E z_e`. If the matched pair contains endpoint `u` or `v`, its partner does not enter `z_e` **provided no tiny edge internal to that pair is included**. This suggests a coordinatewise lift to paired binary activations for external edges, conditional on the missing independent-activation estimate.

## Why internal tiny edges need reservation

The same radius-one policy can fail the `3/4` marginal target after matched-pair correlation if it treats internal tiny edges as ordinary competitors. Let a star have center `u_L`, matched partner `h_R`, and external leaf `v_R`. Put internal target `a≈0.3110434783` on `uh`, external target `b≈0.0724456621` on `uv`, and capacities `D_u=a+b`, `D_h=a`, `D_v=b`. Correlate the activation bits of `u,h` comonotonically and leave `v` independent. Direct enumeration gives `E z_(uv)/b≈0.682909`, although the internal edge's ratio is about `1.426`. Script: `/private/tmp/local_pair_internal_probe.py`. This is a counterexample to transferring this **fixed local policy** coordinatewise; it does not contradict opposite-side supermodularity of optimal capped recourse.

There is a clean candidate reservation. At heavy pair `P`, the endpoints have the same heavy-state residual `R_P`, mean `p=1/4+(3/4)D`, and the same total target `I` on internal tiny edges. In each state reserve `(3/4)y_e R_P/p` for each internal edge `e`. This has mean `(3/4)y_e`, edge cap at most `3y_e<=12y_e`, and total reservation at most `R_P` because `I<=D<=p/(3/4)`. The remaining common residual is `R'_P=(1-(3/4)I/p)R_P`, with mean `p'=1/4+(3/4)(D-I)`. Every external target degree is at most `D-I` at either endpoint. One can reduce real residuals constructively to paired Bernoulli capacities: for realized `R'`, sample each pair's binary activation with success probability `R'_P`, apply any feasible binary policy, then average its flow over the sampled binary activations conditional on `R'`. The averaged flow has load at most `R'_P` at both endpoints, obeys edge caps, and retains the binary policy's unconditional mean. If the binary policy is local, this interpolation is local as well. The unresolved items are the independent-activation inequality for the policy and a graph-order-independent finite-palette concentration/rounding argument.

If the local-policy inequality is established, minimal caps yield a useful per-edge injection tail. Write `Y_(u,P)=sum_{f=ux, x in P}y_f`. For external tiny `e=uv`, changing a matched pair `P` in one color changes `z_e/w_e` by at most `(12y_e/w_e)[1_{P is an endpoint pair}+min(1,12Y_(u,P))+min(1,12Y_(v,P))]`. This follows from the cap `b_e<=12y_e` and the 1-Lipschitz function `r(t)=1/max(1,t)`. Summing the bracket over `P` is at most `2+12(D_u+D_v)<=26`, so its squared sum is bounded by a numerical constant independent of graph degree. A transposition in one pair's exact injection changes two colors; Azuma across `M=Theta(N)` slot exposures gives an `exp(-c delta^2 N)` failure bound for the total coverage of each **fixed** physical tiny edge. Simultaneous coverage over arbitrarily many edges is still open: a union bound again introduces graph order, while the exact local dependence has unbounded degree.
