# A conditional graph-order-independent palette bridge via weighted defective types

**Status (2026-10-03): proof candidate, contingent on an unproved local activation inequality; several technical estimates below still require independent audit.** This is scratch exploration within the requested five-hour period and is not an unrestricted theorem claim. The candidate target is the matching-heavy, bipartite-combined-support case with arbitrary tiny support, parallel multiplicity, and graph order. The missing analytic input is the radius-one `3/4` coordinatewise policy of `connected_dual_local_policy_audit.md`.

## Conditional analytic input and fixed parameters

Assume that for every bipartite target `y` with `sum_(e at v)y_e<=D_v<=1`, independent binary activations with `p_v=1/4+3D_v/4`, and `b_e=min{(4/3)y_e/(p_up_v),12y_e}`, the pointwise feasible capped policy

`z_e(A)=b_e A_uA_v/max{1,L_u,L_v}`, `L_v=sum_(f at v)b_f A_(endpoints f)`,

has `E z_e>=(3/4)y_e` for every edge. Aggregate parallel tiny edges into endpoint bundles before evaluating the policy, splitting bundle flow afterward in proportion to physical weights. Reserve internal tiny bundles of a matched heavy pair as in `connected_dual_local_policy_audit.md`; their required marginal is obtained locally, and the remaining external residual is shared by the pair. Correlation of opposite-shore pair bits cannot lower each external edge's policy expectation after internal reservation, because a third pair affects the two competitor loads in the supermodular reciprocal-max direction and a pair containing a probe endpoint has no competitor effect. Conditional Bernoulli interpolation maps the binary policy to an actual real-residual policy with the same mean, cap, and radius-one dependency.

Fix an arbitrarily small target coefficient loss `epsilon>0`. Choose constants in this order: a tiny approximation tolerance `zeta<<epsilon`; a polynomial approximation degree `t=t(zeta)`; a strong/weak threshold `gamma>0` so small that `sqrt(gamma)` and `sqrt(gamma[t log(1/gamma)+log(1/gamma)])` are much smaller than `zeta`; a number `Q` of weighted-defect sublabels large relative to `gamma^-3/epsilon`; and then a prime power `q` large enough for the final number of types, heavy-bucket count, and inverse desired heavy-leftover fraction. These are fixed constants independent of `N` and graph order. Let `r=t+2`, `L=q^r`, and take `N>>L`. The enormous constants are acceptable for an existence bound.

Use the pseudo-heavy slack parameters of `bounded_heavy_pairs_integer_transfer_candidate.md`: heavy weights rounded upward to `K=O(1/epsilon)` buckets, `s=1-3tau`, main palette size `M` a multiple of `L` satisfying `M=(1+rho)N/[(3/4)s]+O(L)`, and normalized tiny target `y'_e=(1+delta)w_e/[(3/4)sM]` with positive `delta` absorbed by `rho`. The one-color law for any rounded-down heavy bucket probabilities below has physical tiny mean at least `(1+delta)w_e/M` and per-color physical cap `C_e^act=12(1+delta)w_e/[(3/4)M]`; scaling by `s` leaves at least `3tau` physical vertex/color slack.

## Strong interaction graph and weighted defective type refinement

Contract each matched heavy pair to one supervertex; retain each bare vertex as a deterministic supervertex. An external tiny bundle `e=uv` joining supervertices `P,Q` has normalized target `y'_e`. Mark it strong when `y'_e>gamma`, weak otherwise. The strong supergraph has maximum degree `k<=2/gamma`, since the two physical endpoints of a supervertex each have total normalized target at most one. Color its third power properly with `T=O(k^3)` base types. For a supervertex `P`, write `S_P={P} union N_strong(P)`. Every member of `S_P` has a distinct base type. For a strong edge `P-Q`, every member of `S_P union S_Q` has a distinct base type. A weak edge can have repeated base types across `S_P,S_Q`.

Build a weighted *conflict graph* on the supervertices. For every weak bundle `e=P-Q` of physical total weight `T_e`, and every cross pair of **distinct** vertices `A in S_P`, `B in S_Q` that shares a base type, add `T_e` to the conflict edge `AB` (multiple contributions add). If the same vertex lies in both `S_P` and `S_Q`, it is only one local source and creates no conflict self-loop. There are no within-`S_P` or within-`S_Q` duplicates. A fixed supervertex `A` belongs to `S_P union S_Q` only if the bundle meets `A` or one of its at most `k` strong neighbors. Each such possible endpoint supervertex has total physical tiny stock at most `2N`, and each bundle creates at most `O(k)` conflict edges incident to `A`. Thus the weighted conflict degree of `A` is at most `C k^2 N` for a universal `C`.

Give conflict-graph vertices `Q` sublabels by globally minimizing the total weight of monochromatic conflict edges. At a minimum, changing one vertex to a uniformly random sublabel cannot improve the objective, so its monochromatic incident weight is at most its full conflict degree divided by `Q`. Give each supervertex the final type `(base type,sublabel)`. Declare a weak tiny bundle **bad** if its local set `S_P union S_Q` has two members of the same final type. Every such duplicate is a monochromatic conflict edge touching some `A in S_P`. Hence the total physical stock of bad weak bundles at each supervertex `P` is at most

`sum_(A in S_P) mono_conflict_degree(A) <= C k^3 N/Q`.

Choose `Q` so that this is at most `epsilon N/100`. Set all bad weak bundles aside. The all-tiny weighted coloring lemma gives them at most `ceil[(epsilon N/100)/(1-14tau)]` extra colors, uniformly in graph order and arbitrarily small individual weights. Every remaining external bundle, strong or good weak, has **distinct final types throughout its full strong local set**. The final number of types `TQ` depends only on `epsilon,gamma`, not on the graph order.

## Constant blocks and heavy counts

Take `q>=TQ` and assign distinct Vandermonde linear forms `ell_j:F_q^r->F_q` to the final types, chosen so any at most `r` forms are independent. In each block of `L=q^r` main colors, index colors by `x in F_q^r`. A supervertex pair `P` chooses an independent shift `s_P in F_q` for that block and reads field symbol `ell_type(P)(x)+s_P`. Map the `q` field symbols to its heavy bucket states: bucket `b` receives exactly `m_(P,b)=floor(q n_(P,b)/M)` symbols, where `n_(P,b)` is its physical heavy count; all other symbols mean empty. Each bucket therefore occupies exactly `M m_(P,b)/q<=n_(P,b)` main colors across all blocks, regardless of shifts. Assign distinct physical heavy identities to those slots. The heavy identities left over at a pair number less than `K M/q`; because heavy simple support is a matching, they fit in `ceil(K M/q)` extra heavy-only colors. For the main one-color law, the rounded-down bucket probabilities `m_(P,b)/q` only increase residual capacity relative to the full physical heavy counts, so the normalized tiny target remains locally feasible.

## Balancing weak aggregate loads within every block

For a remaining tiny bundle `f` let `b_f<=12y'_f` be its tentative coefficient. For a physical vertex `v` and weak neighboring supervertex `P`, put `B_(v,P)=sum_(f=vx,x in P and weak)b_f<=12gamma`. In an actual heavy state, let `R'_P(c)` be the real residual after any internal-bundle reservation. The conditional mean of the auxiliary binary *weak competitor* load is the additive row

`m_v(c)=sum_(P weak neighbor of v) B_(v,P) R'_P(c)`.

For one block, the pair shifts are independent source variables before selection. Each source contribution to a row `(v,c)` is between zero and `12gamma`; its expectation is independent of `c`. Add complementary rows using `B_(v,P)(1-R'_P(c))` so upper bounds imply both upper and lower bounds on `m_v(c)`. After scaling by `12gamma`, every row has expected sum `O(1/gamma)`, while a single pair shift contributes at most `O(L/gamma)` over **all** vertex/color rows in the block (its two physical endpoints have total normalized tiny target at most two). The Harris--Srinivasan proposition quoted as `prop:HS` in the manuscript therefore chooses all pair shifts in this block so that, simultaneously for every `v,c`,

`|m_v(c)-bar m_v| <= E_gamma := O(sqrt(gamma log(L/gamma))+gamma log(L/gamma))`,

where `bar m_v` is the uniform-shift mean. Choose shifts separately for each block; each block has the same bound. This use of the proposition is graph-order independent because `L` is a fixed parameter. A single global pair schedule source would instead have `D=O(M/gamma)` and an unwanted `log M`.

For a probe bundle `e=P-Q`, let `T_e=S_P union S_Q`. First **retain** every weak competitor whose pair ID lies in `T_e` as a local binary variable; a pair can be weak-adjacent to one endpoint and strong-adjacent to the other. Conditional on all these local bits and the real residual state, the remaining weak binary competitor load at `v` has variance at most `sum_(A outside T_e) B_(v,A)^2=O(gamma)`. Its conditional mean is `m_v(c)-sum_(A in T_e and weak at v) B_(v,A)R'_A(c)`. Replace `m_v(c)` by `bar m_v` using the HS bound and keep the finite local correction `sum_local B_(v,A)(A_A-R'_A(c))` inside the strong-variable function. This avoids an unjustified conditional-independence assumption. The reciprocal-max function is 1-Lipschitz, so the two replacements cost `O(sqrt(gamma)+E_gamma)b_e` in each probe flow. For a weak probe itself, remove its own tentative coefficient from the weak competitor sum and keep its endpoint activation contribution explicitly. Constants in `B_(v,P)=O(gamma)` allow up to the bounded number of parallel endpoint orientations between two contracted heavy pairs.

## Limited independence for the remaining local function

After weak-load replacement, an edge's binary local flow is `b_e A_uA_v g(X_u,X_v)`, with `g(x,y)=1/max(1,x,y)` and `X_u,X_v` equal to a constant plus sums of strong-neighbor coefficients and the retained finite weak corrections on `T_e`. All `X` values lie in a fixed bounded interval, e.g. `[0,12]`, before the mean substitution; enlarge the approximation interval by the fixed HS error if needed. Choose a bivariate polynomial `P_t(x,y)` uniformly within `zeta` of `g` on that interval. Substitution makes `A_uA_v P_t(X_u,X_v)` a polynomial of total degree at most `t+2` in the local auxiliary binary activations and real residuals. Taking conditional expectation over the binary activations keeps total degree at most `r=t+2` in the real residual variables (reduce binary powers using `A^j=A`). Every pair ID in `T_e` has a distinct final type for a good edge. The Vandermonde construction therefore makes the block average of this polynomial **exactly equal** to its expectation under independent pair residual marginals, regardless of chosen shifts. Uniform approximation contributes at most `O(zeta)b_e` per color.

Together with the weak variance and HS errors, the empirical physical palette coverage of every remaining tiny edge is at least its product-law mean `(1+delta)w_e` minus

`O(zeta+sqrt(gamma)+E_gamma) * M*b_e^act`,

where `M*b_e^act=O(w_e)` uniformly in the physical weight and graph order. Choose fixed parameters so this is at most `delta w_e`. Thus every remaining physical tiny edge is covered at least once in the main palette. Scale its color fractions down individually to exact stock, preserving all per-color constraints. Finally use the bipartite source-preserving iterative rounding lemma with `3tau` slack to assign whole physical tiny edges. The bad weak tiny bundles and leftover heavy identities use the extra color sets above. With all fixed losses chosen inside `epsilon`, this would yield the conditional `(4/3+epsilon)N+O_epsilon(1)` integer bound uniformly in graph order.

## Items requiring a separate full audit

1. The radius-one independent-activation inequality is unproved and numerically sharp. This is the central missing lemma.
2. In the weak-load replacement, a weak probe's own endpoint-pair contribution must be subtracted exactly before conditioning on its endpoint activation; the remaining local function still has degree at most `t+2`.
3. The conflict graph should be built from aggregate physical stock `T_e`, while the strong graph uses normalized target `y'_e`; these are proportional for all tiny bundles, so a supervertex's total stock is bounded by `2N`.
4. The `prop:HS` application is one-sided; complementary rows are needed for the lower bound. Its source-column bound is `O(L/gamma)` only when each block's pair shift is treated as a separate source, not the entire palette permutation.
5. The color count contains a fixed block-rounding overhead `O_epsilon(L)`, an `O(KM/q)` heavy reserve, and an `O(epsilon N)` all-tiny bad-edge reserve. The constants must be chosen in a noncircular order and then `N` large enough.
