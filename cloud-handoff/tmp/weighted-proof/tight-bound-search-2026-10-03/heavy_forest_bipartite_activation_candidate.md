# Heavy-forest bipartite fluid extension: interim activation candidates

**Research status, 2026-10-03.** These are intermediate arguments under audit, not a claim of a full heavy-forest theorem. The intended model has bipartite combined support; categorical heavy edges of weight greater than `1/2`; arbitrary divisible fluid support and no per-fluid-edge supply cap. The previously established heavy-*matching*-support transfer is separate. The first nonmatching heavy component, a star, is analyzed below. A general heavy forest remains unresolved.

## Signs of matching-value second differences

Let `f(S)` be the maximum weight matching value in the induced subgraph of a bipartite fluid graph, with nonnegative edge profits. For distinct vertices `u,v`, fix all other available vertices. If `u,v` lie on the **same** bipartition side, then

`f(11)+f(00)-f(10)-f(01) <= 0`.

If they lie on **opposite** sides, the inequality reverses. For the same-side assertion, take optimum matchings for states `11` (red) and `00` (blue). If either added vertex is unmatched in the red matching, monotonicity suffices. Otherwise each is an endpoint of a red-starting alternating path in the red/blue symmetric difference. They cannot be endpoints of the same path: red at both ends forces an odd path length, but a path between same-side vertices has even length. Swap one endpoint's alternating component to obtain matchings for states `10` and `01` with the same total weight. For the opposite-side assertion, start with optimum matchings for states `10` and `01` and swap a component as in the separate [bipartite activation note](bipartite_independent_activation_reduction.md). Common edges are retained in both matchings. These sign statements hold for arbitrary nonnegative edge weights and multiedges.

## Candidate star-component domination

Consider one heavy star with center `b` on the right side and leaves `a_i` on the left. First reduce any selected heavy weight to a binary unit blocker by the coordinatewise concavity/chord argument. Since at most one heavy edge can be selected at `b`, the binary blocker law is categorical: edge `ba_i` blocks `b,a_i` with probability `s_i`, with `sum_i s_i<=1`; otherwise nobody in the star is blocked. The vertex availability marginals are `p_b=1-sum_i s_i` and `p_{a_i}=1-s_i`.

Compare this categorical law with fully independent vertex activations at the same marginals, conditional on all vertices outside the star.

1. Keep `b` independent and change the independent leaf *blocker* indicators to a categorical one-hot blocker with probabilities `s_i`, using a dummy no-block outcome of probability `1-sum_i s_i`. For each fixed center state, the matching value as a function of leaf activations is submodular; complementing all leaf variables preserves submodularity. The multilinear extension has nonpositive mixed partials, so randomized pipage rounding along `x_i+x_j=constant` has nondecreasing expected value. Repeatedly round the blocker marginals, including the dummy, to a one-hot vector. The unique final law is the desired categorical leaf-blocker law, and expected matching value has not decreased.
2. Now set `b` active exactly in the dummy/no-block outcome. This preserves its activation marginal. Its matching-value increment `f(b=1)-f(b=0)` is nondecreasing in each left-side leaf activation, by the opposite-side second-difference sign. The all-leaves-active dummy outcome has probability `p_b` and maximizes this increment. Rearranging the center activation to that outcome therefore cannot decrease expected value.

The resulting joint vertex-availability law is precisely the categorical heavy-star blocker law. Thus, **conditional on the two sign lemmas and the pipage step**, its expected binary matching recourse dominates independent vertex activation with the same marginals. Independent sampling across disjoint heavy-star components would allow this comparison one component at a time. Combined with the proved boosted-independent-activation forest induction, this suggests a `3/4` local-hull inclusion for heavy-*star-forest* support and arbitrary bipartite divisible-fluid support. It has not been promoted here to a final theorem because the requested broader heavy-forest case is still under study.

## Why a universal disjoint-edge overlap rule is unavailable

Even in a heavy path of three edges, two nonadjacent heavy edges can be coupled with variable overlap while preserving their marginals. Different tiny objectives prefer opposite overlap choices. Let their endpoint pairs be `{a(L),b(R)}` and `{c(L),d(R)}`. A tiny edge `a-d` has matching values `(2 free, only first free, only second free, neither free)=(1,0,0,0)`, so placing the two heavy blockers together increases expected recourse at fixed blocker marginals. Conversely, add outside vertices `z(R),w(L)` and tiny edges `a-z,c-z,b-w,d-w`, all with unit profit. The corresponding matching values are `(2,2,2,0)`, so blocker overlap has second difference `0+2-2-2=-2` and *decreases* expected recourse. Both fluid graphs are bipartite. A heavy-forest proof therefore cannot assume that one fixed maximal-overlap matching schedule is best for every profit direction.

## Arbitrary heavy support with unit binary blockers: edge-preserving pipage candidate

For a bipartite heavy graph `H=(L,R;E)` and a fractional matching vector `z` on its edges, let `s_v=sum_{e incident v}z_e` and transform the vertex availability bits by `sigma_u=A_u` for `u in L` and `sigma_v=1-A_v` for `v in R`. When blockers form a matching `M`, the one-set of `sigma` is `(L\L(M)) union R(M)`, a basis of the transversal matroid defined by `H`. The two sign lemmas above imply that every weighted tiny matching recourse function becomes submodular in these transformed bits.

There is an edge-marginal-preserving pipage route that appears to avoid restricting `H` to a forest. Start at fractional `z`. In its fractional-edge support, choose an even cycle or a maximal alternating path. Increase/decrease its edge coordinates with alternating signs until an edge reaches `0` or `1` (or a vertex degree boundary). Internal vertex degrees stay fixed. Along a path, the two endpoint `sigma` marginal coordinates change in opposite directions regardless of endpoint shores; along a cycle, all transformed marginals stay fixed. The multilinear extension of a submodular function is convex along an opposite-coordinate swap, so randomizing between the two feasible step endpoints while preserving the mean of every edge coordinate does not decrease its expected value. Iterating to an integral matching would give a blocker matching law with **each edge marginal `z_e`** and expected tiny matching recourse at least that under fully independent vertex activation with marginals `1-s_v`. This is an interim proof candidate; path/cycle termination and the common distribution for all submodular objectives need a complete write-up before promotion.

For a weighted categorical heavy edge of type `h`, the desired blocker marginal is `z_h=alpha w_h x_h`, whereas its actual presence marginal is `a_h=alpha x_h`. A blocker law with edge marginals `z` cannot simply be declared the actual heavy law. One can partially extend it: if `alpha=3/4`, then the extra soft-presence target `c=a-z` has vertex degree at most `alpha-s_v=(1-s_v)-1/4`. If the blocker law dominates independent vertex activation, the boosted independent-activation theorem and support-function separation give a conditional matching of soft edges on blocker-free vertices with exact edge marginals `c`. Thus an inclusion coupling `M_block subset M_presence` exists. But the resulting binary blocker need not be conditionally Bernoulli with probability `w_h` given the **full** presence matching/type state. Without that martingale property, concavity does not compare the actual residual `1-w_h` to the binary blocker residual. This is the weighted-lift gap, not an edge-marginal gap.

## Common-heavy-weight bipartite transfer: audited interim argument

Suppose all heavy edge types have one common weight `w in (1/2,1]`, though their simple support and parallel multiplicities are arbitrary. Let `alpha=3/4`. Let the local fractional data satisfy `sum_{h incident v}x_h<=1` and `w sum_{h incident v}x_h+sum_{e incident v}y_e<=1`. The combined heavy/fluid multigraph is bipartite. Fluid edges are divisible and have no supply caps, or they have caps `C_e` with `y_e<=C_e/12` for every `e`. Then the following argument gives an interim `alpha` local-hull inclusion; it does **not** cover heterogeneous heavy weights or physical indivisible tiny edges.

Write `t_h=alpha x_h`, a fractional heavy matching, `d_v(t)=sum_{h incident v}t_h`, and `p_v(t)=1-w d_v(t)`. For a fluid profit vector `q`, define `f_q(S)` as the optimal fluid matching/flow value when exactly vertices in `S` are active, with the fixed edge caps if present. Let `G_q(t)=E[f_q(A)]`, where the activation indicators are independent with probabilities `p_v(t)`. Flip the activation coordinates on the right shore: `sigma_u=A_u` on the left and `sigma_v=1-A_v` on the right. The same-shore and opposite-shore second-difference signs proved above say that `f_q`, expressed in `sigma`, is submodular. The statement remains true with fixed fluid-edge caps: for two optimal fractional bipartite flows, orient their edgewise difference, decompose into directed paths/cycles, and exchange all paths starting at the relevant activated endpoint. Every exchanged edge keeps the sum of its two flow values fixed, so each color still respects its own edge cap. The alternating flow difference proves the same two second-difference inequalities.

Round `t` on its bipartite fractional-edge support by alternating-cycle/path pipage. If there is a cycle, it has even length; perturb its edges with alternating signs. Every vertex degree, hence every `p_v`, stays fixed. Otherwise choose a path between two leaves of a fractional-support tree and perturb successive edges alternately. Its internal degrees remain fixed. At its endpoints, the transformed `sigma` probabilities move by equal amounts in opposite directions: for same-shore endpoints the original degree changes have opposite signs; for opposite-shore endpoints they have the same sign and the right-shore flip reverses one. The common `w` makes the two probability changes equal in magnitude. Since the multilinear extension of a submodular function is convex along a two-coordinate swap, `G_q` is convex in the perturbation parameter. Move to either feasible endpoint of the perturbation interval with probabilities chosen to preserve its mean zero. This preserves **each heavy-type edge marginal** and cannot decrease expected `G_q`, for every `q` simultaneously. At least one fractional edge becomes integral per step. The support leaves have degree in `(0,1)` and no incident integral edge, so both directions are initially feasible. The procedure terminates with a random heavy matching `M` satisfying `P(h in M)=alpha x_h` and `E_M G_q(M)>=G_q(alpha x)` for every `q`.

At the start, `p_v(alpha x)=1-alpha w sum_h x_h=1/4+alpha D_v`, where `D_v=1-w sum_h x_h`. The independent-activation theorem gives `G_q(alpha x)>=alpha q·y`, with the `y_e<=C_e/12` condition in the capped case. At an integral heavy matching `M`, actual residual vertex capacity is `r_v(M)=1-w 1_{v covered by M}=p_v(M)`. Joint concavity of the optimum fractional fluid value in its vertex-capacity vector yields `F_q(r(M),C)>=G_q(M)`, since independent binary vertex activations have mean `r(M)`. Thus `E_M F_q(r(M),C)>=alpha q·y` for all `q`. The pipage law of `M` is independent of `q`; support-function separation against the convex expected feasible-flow set supplies conditional fluid flows with edge means at least `alpha y`, and downward closure trims them to equality. The resulting one-color law has each heavy edge type with probability `alpha x_h` and each fluid edge expected load `alpha y_e`.

The path rounding uses equal weight at both endpoints **and across all heavy edges**: if weights differ, an internal vertex's heavy *degree* stays fixed but its weighted used capacity changes. The needed convexity then becomes a many-coordinate assertion and is not supplied by transformed submodularity. This is the unresolved weighted-lift issue.

## Why weighted-load-preserving pipage does not immediately repair heterogeneity

One can perturb `z_h=w_h t_h`, rather than `t_h`, with alternating `+epsilon,-epsilon` on a fractional heavy path. This keeps the **weighted** load at every internal vertex fixed and moves only its endpoint activation coordinates in opposite transformed directions, regardless of the edge weights. However, feasibility also requires `sum_{h incident v}t_h<=1`, and this unweighted matching-degree constraint can become tight before any edge is fixed.

An explicit two-edge star already exhibits the stall. Let its edge weights be `w_1=0.51,w_2=1` and start with `t_1=0.25,t_2=0.50`; its center degree is `0.75`. In the weighted-load-preserving direction increasing the first edge, put `delta z_1=epsilon, delta z_2=-epsilon`. Its center matching degree becomes `0.75+epsilon(1/0.51-1)`, so at `epsilon=0.25/(1/0.51-1)≈0.2602` it reaches `1`, while `t_1≈0.7602` and `t_2≈0.2398` are both still fractional. The opposite branch can hit an edge boundary, so an unbiased step is possible, but the first branch cannot continue by the same weighted-load-preserving direction. This is a barrier to a simple iteration proof, not a counterexample to the desired `3/4` transfer: the heavy-star domination argument handles this instance by a different coupling.

The *unweighted*-degree-preserving alternation also lacks the necessary convexity. On a heavy path `a(L)-b(R)-c(L)` with weights `w_1<w_2`, set `delta t_1=epsilon, delta t_2=-epsilon`. For the one-edge fluid recourse `a-b` under independent binary vertex activation, `G(t)=p_a(t)p_b(t)`, and its second derivative along this line is `2 w_1(w_1-w_2)<0`. Thus Jensen's inequality goes the wrong way. This does not refute the desired recourse inequality: at an integral heavy state, the selected heavy edge creates *positively correlated* binary blockers at its two endpoints, and joint concavity of fractional recourse retains that gain. The independent-vertex potential discards it too early.

## A stronger stochastic-order target and its slack limit

A possible heterogeneous route is to choose an actual heavy matching `M` with marginals `t_h=alpha x_h`, then independently mark each selected edge `h` as a binary blocker with probability `w_h`. Its binary blocker subset `B` is a matching and has marginals `w_h t_h`. Ask that the transformed vertex availability law of `B` dominate the independent Bernoulli law with the same vertex marginals for **every submodular set function**. This would imply the desired fluid-recourse statement after the conditional concavity chord, but it is stronger than needed.

The finite LP [weighted_heavy_path_submod_order.py](weighted_heavy_path_submod_order.py) expresses this domination as a nonnegative combination of elementary submodular-square moves in the law difference. On a heavy path of 4--6 edges with alternating weights `(0.51,1,...)` and equal edge marginals `t_e=3/8` (internal matching degree `3/4`), it found feasible laws. This is diagnostic only. At `t_e=9/20`, already a four-edge path makes the stronger order **impossible**, and the obstruction can be certified by one pairwise covariance inequality.

For that path, label edges `1,2,3,4`, and let `T_13,T_14,T_24` be probabilities of its three two-edge matchings. Every matching law with all four edge marginals `d=9/20` has `T_13+T_14+T_24>=4d-1=4/5`, since a matching has size at most two, and `T_13+T_14<=d`. With weights `(51/100,1,51/100,1)`, the two right-shore internal vertices are both binary-blocked with probability

`J=(51/100)^2 T_13+(51/100)T_14+T_24 >= (51/100)^2 d+(3d-1)=0.467045`.

Their individual blocker probabilities are both `(151/100)d=0.6795`, whose product is `0.46172025`. Hence the two transformed right-shore bits have positive covariance under every possible thinned matching law; an elementary submodular function detects failure of the strong order. This occurs at internal heavy degree `0.9`, outside the target `alpha=3/4` region. It is **not** a counterexample to the actual fractional-fluid recourse bound, where fractional capacities may exceed their binary-chord expectation. The targeted strong-order statement remains open at degree at most `3/4`.

The maximum-entropy matching distribution is not a universal choice even at target degree `3/4`. On the four-edge path with `t_e=3/8` and weights `(0.51,1,0.51,1)`, its independently thinned binary blockers give positive transformed covariance between vertices 1 and 3 (about `0.0087834`), contradicting all-submodular domination. The finite LP nevertheless finds another matching distribution that satisfies the stronger order. A proof, if true, must tune overlap among disjoint heavy edges according to their weights.

Nor does independent `3/4`-thinning of an arbitrary decomposition of the *unscaled* fractional matching suffice. On heavy `K_{2,2}`, let every original edge have marginal `x_e=1/2`, realized by choosing one of the two perfect matchings uniformly. Independently retain selected edges with probability `3/4`, and use heavy weights `1` on one perfect matching and `0.51` on the other. After the independent binary blocker coins, each of the two left-side vertices has blocker probability `(0.75+0.75·0.51)/2`, but their blocker indicators have positive covariance `[(0.75-0.75·0.51)^2]/4`. Their transformed availability indicators have the same positive covariance, contrary to all-submodular domination. This refutes that particular rounding law, not the existence of a better partial-matching law or the target fluid theorem.

For one **stronger, independent-thinning** lift, let `P(M)` be the presence distribution on heavy matchings and let `Q(B)` be the distribution after each selected edge `e` is independently kept as a blocker with probability `w_e`. Their generating polynomials obey

`Q(z)=sum_M P(M) product_{e in M}[(1-w_e)+w_e z_e]`.

This triangular map is invertible when every `w_e>0`. It defines a tractable sufficient condition, but **not** the exact heterogeneous problem: the joint-concavity chord only needs `Pr(e in B | M)=w_e` for each `e in M`. The blocker indicators of *different* selected edges may be correlated conditional on `M`. The exact martingale transport asks for a joint law on `B subseteq M` with those conditional per-edge probabilities, prescribed `Pr(e in M)=t_e`, and a blocker law dominating independent vertex activation (or enough fluid recourse). Arbitrary pipage rounding of `w_e t_e` supplies a promising blocker marginal law but does not automatically supply this inclusion martingale. The independent-thinning LP above tests one strictly stronger route.

## Exact q-specific recourse program and exchange condition

The intended fluid hull does **not** require one heavy law to dominate every transformed-submodular function. Fix a nonnegative fluid-profit direction `q`, let `a_h=alpha x_h`, and define `H_q(M)=F_q(r(M),C)` with `r_v(M)=1-sum_{h in M: h incident v}w_h`. The exact heavy-law recourse support function is

`W_q(a)=max { sum_M P_M H_q(M): P_M>=0, sum_M P_M=1, sum_{M contains h}P_M=a_h for every h }`.

Its LP dual is

`W_q(a)=min { lambda_0+sum_h a_h lambda_h: lambda_0+sum_{h in M}lambda_h>=H_q(M) for every matching M }`,

where all dual variables are free. Consequently, the desired `alpha` inclusion follows if, for each `q`, every affine majorant of `H_q` on heavy matchings evaluates at `a` above `alpha q·y`. An optimizing heavy law may depend on `q`; support-function separation then recovers a single final joint heavy/fluid law with all target coordinates.

There is an exact local exchange necessary condition for an optimal law. If two matchings `M,N` both have positive probability, and `K` is one alternating path or cycle component of `M triangle N`, let `M',N'` exchange that component. They are still matchings and `1_M+1_N=1_{M'}+1_{N'}` edgewise. Therefore optimality gives

`H_q(M)+H_q(N) >= H_q(M')+H_q(N')`.

The arbitrary bipartite heterogeneous target asks whether these affine/exchange constraints, together with local feasibility of `(x,y)`, force `W_q(alpha x)>=alpha q·y` at `alpha=3/4`. This remains open. In particular, the displayed exchange inequality is only a **necessary** condition at an optimum and has not been turned into the desired bound.

## Exact binary matching dilation for uncapped fluid recourse

There is an exact combinatorial representation that removes the need to choose any specific endpoint-blocker coupling. Let `G_F` be bipartite, `r in [0,1]^V`, and `F_q(r)` the optimal **uncapped** divisible-fluid matching value under vertex capacities `r`. Then

`F_q(r)=max { E[m_q(G_F[A])]: A in {0,1}^V random with E A_v=r_v for every v }`.  (2)

Joint concavity of `F_q` gives the upper bound in (2). For the reverse bound, take an optimal fractional flow `f` with `deg_f(v)<=r_v`. Bipartite matching-polytope integrality decomposes `f` into a random ordinary matching `N` with edge marginals `f_e`; a vertex is covered by `N` with probability `deg_f(v)`. Set `A_v=1` whenever `N` covers `v`. For every uncovered vertex, add activation with conditional probability `(r_v-deg_f(v))/(1-deg_f(v))` (arbitrary when the denominator is zero). Then `E A_v=r_v`, `N` is always a matching inside `G_F[A]`, and `E m_q(G_F[A])>=q·f=F_q(r)`. This proves (2) for every profit vector `q`.

Condition on each heavy matching `M` and apply (2) with its physical residual vector `r(M)`. It follows that an exact target fluid vector `alpha y` can be realized if and only if there exists a joint law of a heavy matching `M` and a fluid matching `N` such that `Pr(h in M)=alpha x_h`, `Pr(e in N)=alpha y_e`, and, for every heavy matching state `M` and vertex `v`,

`E[1_{v covered by N} | M] <= r_v(M)`.

The forward direction decomposes each conditional fractional fluid flow into matchings. For the reverse direction, take conditional means of the fluid-matching incidence vector. This is an **exact** two-matching coupling LP for uncapped divisible fluid; no independence of blocker coins or universal submodular order is required. It is not asserted for small per-fluid-edge caps, where an ordinary matching edge of load one can violate an edge cap even though its mean is below the cap. The open question is whether the local heavy categorical and weighted-degree constraints ensure this coupling at `alpha=3/4` for arbitrary bipartite heavy support.

## A false unscaled additive-gap shortcut

For fixed fluid profits `q`, let `C_q(a)=W_q(a)` denote the concave closure of the conditional recourse over heavy matching marginals `a`. It is concave in `a`: mixing two heavy laws mixes both their marginals and their objective values. Since `C_q(0)=F_q(1)`,

`C_q(alpha x) >= alpha C_q(x)+(1-alpha)F_q(1)`.

Therefore a uniform unscaled bound

`C_q(x) >= F_q(D) - (1/3)F_q(1)`  (3)

would imply `C_q((3/4)x)>=(3/4)F_q(D)` and hence the uncapped divisible-fluid `3/4` transfer. **But (3) is false**, as the following exact bipartite family shows. Let the heavy graph be a cycle `C_{2n}` with `x_e=1/2` on every edge. Give all edges in one alternating perfect matching weight `h in (1/2,1)` and all edges in the other weight `1`. A full-marginal matching law must choose the two perfect matchings with probability `1/2` each: its expected cardinality is `n`, forcing every state to be perfect, and the even cycle has precisely those two perfect matchings. Add a fluid star from a new right-shore center `c` to every left-shore cycle vertex, with unit profit. Each left leaf has residual local capacity `D=(1-h)/2` and `D_c=1`. Choose `n(1-h)/2>=1`, for example `n=5,h=3/5`. Then `F_q(D)=F_q(1)=1`. In the low-weight perfect-matching state all leaves retain capacity `1-h`, so the star recourse is `1`; in the unit-weight perfect-matching state all leaves have zero capacity, so it is `0`. Consequently `C_q(x)=1/2`, violating the right side `2/3` of (3). This does **not** refute the target scaled claim: at `alpha=3/4`, partial heavy matchings become available, and the same instance admits a law with fluid-star recourse one in every state (for `n=5,h=3/5`, take the low-weight perfect matching with probability `3/8` and otherwise a uniformly chosen three-edge submatching of the disjoint unit-weight perfect matching). The displayed law has every heavy edge marginal `3/8`.

## Unit-profit fluid stars with at least four leaves: a scoped exact result

Let the combined graph be bipartite. The fluid support is a single star with center `c` and a set `S` of `n>=4` leaves on the opposite shore; every fluid spoke has the same nonnegative profit `q`. The center may itself be incident to heavy edges, and the heavy support and heavy weights may be arbitrary. Let `t_h=(3/4)x_h` be the target heavy edge marginals. The local heavy categorical inequality implies `deg_t(v)<=3/4` at each vertex. Therefore `sum_{h incident S} t_h<=3n/4<=n-1`.

The polytope of bipartite heavy matching incidence vectors `m` satisfying `sum_{h incident S}m_h<=n-1` is integral. To see this directly, put an arc of capacity `n-1` from the source to a group node for `S`, unit-capacity arcs from that group node to every left-shore leaf in `S`, and unit-capacity arcs directly from the source to the other left vertices. Continue through heavy edges to right-shore vertices, each of which has unit capacity to the sink. Integral flows correspond exactly to heavy matchings using at most `n-1` edges incident to `S`. The given fractional vector `t` is a feasible flow, so it decomposes into such integral matchings while preserving every individual heavy edge marginal.

In every resulting heavy state `M`, at least one leaf `s in S` is not covered by a heavy edge and has full residual capacity `r_s(M)=1`. Thus the fluid star can place `r_c(M)` units on spoke `cs`; its unit-profit recourse is `F_q(r(M))=q r_c(M)` (the reverse inequality is the center capacity). Taking expectation gives `q[1-(3/4)sum_{h incident c}w_hx_h]=q[1/4+(3/4)D_c] >= (3/4)q sum_{e incident c}y_e`, because the local fluid target has total at `c` at most `D_c`. This is a q-specific support-function bound for a **single equal-profit star**. It does not preserve each fluid-spoke target marginal; it does not establish a general star result with unequal profits or simultaneous forest components. If one needs only the equal-profit support function, it handles the large-star cycle family above for all heterogeneous weights.

## Arbitrary-profit single fluid star with an unheavy center: audited interim proof

The following stronger q-specific argument handles every profit vector and every number of spokes, provided the fluid-star center `c` has **no incident heavy edge**. The bipartite heavy graph may have arbitrary support, parallel types, and heterogeneous weights in `(1/2,1]`. This is an interim scoped result under the five-hour proof-audit instruction, not a claim for a fluid forest or a center that itself receives heavy load.

Fix nonnegative spoke profits and order the leaves `v_1,v_2,...` by decreasing `q_i` (ties arbitrary). First choose **any** heavy matching law with edge marginals `x_h`; it exists because `x` lies in the bipartite matching polytope. Independently choose a uniform phase `T in {1,2,3,4}`. Delete from the chosen full matching every heavy edge incident to leaf `v_i` for which `i≡T (mod 4)`. Every heavy edge touches at most one star leaf, since all leaves occupy the same bipartition shore. Delete each selected heavy edge not incident to any star leaf with probability `1/4` by any further randomization. The remaining set is a matching and each heavy edge `h` has exact marginal `(3/4)x_h`.

For a prefix `P_k={v_1,...,v_k}`, put `L_k=sum_{i<=k}sum_{h incident v_i}w_hx_h`, so `sum_{i<=k}D_i=k-L_k`. Let `R_i` be the physical residual capacity after deletion. The prefix fluid-star recourse is `f_k(R)=min(1,sum_{i<=k}R_i)` because the center has capacity one. If `k>=4`, in every state either one of the first four leaves was originally unmatched or phase `T` deletes a heavy edge from one of them. Hence `f_k(R)=1>= (3/4)min(1,k-L_k)`. If `k=3`, the same argument succeeds for at least three of four phases, so `E f_3(R)>=3/4>= (3/4)min(1,3-L_3)`. If `k=1`, `E f_1(R)=E R_1=1-(3/4)L_1=1/4+(3/4)D_1 >=(3/4)D_1`.

For `k=2`, conditional on a full heavy matching, if either leaf is unmatched then `f_2=1` for every phase. If both are matched by edges of weights `w_1,w_2`, the two leaf indices have distinct residues modulo four; exactly two phases delete one edge and give `f_2=1`, while the other two retain both edges and give `f_2=1-(w_1+w_2-1)_+`. Since `(W-1)_+<=W/2` for `W in[0,2]`, averaging over phases and the full matching gives `E f_2 >=1-L_2/4`. For `L_2<=1`, this is at least `3/4`; for `L_2>=1`, `1-L_2/4 >=(3/4)(2-L_2)`. Thus in either case `E f_2 >=(3/4)min(1,2-L_2)`.

For an uncapped star with center capacity one, the fractional-knapsack layer-cake identity is exact:

`F_q(R)=integral_{lambda>=0} min(1,sum_{i:q_i>=lambda}R_i) d lambda`.

The sets in this integral are precisely the ordered prefixes. Integrating the four prefix bounds gives `E F_q(R)>=(3/4)F_q(D)>=(3/4)q·y` for any locally feasible fluid target `y`. The heavy law may be fixed independently of `q`; only the phase deletion rule depends on the ordering by `q`, which is allowed in a support-function argument. Support-function separation therefore proves the `(3/4)` local-hull inclusion for **one uncapped divisible fluid star with an unheavy center**, even when heavy support is arbitrary and weights are heterogeneous. This proof does not apply verbatim when the center is heavy, because the center capacity enters every prefix minimum and may correlate with the deletion phase. Nor does it provide one deletion phase simultaneously adapted to multiple fluid-star centers.

### Heavy-center extension for long prefixes

The same four-phase construction settles every profit prefix of length at least three even when the star center `c` has heavy edges. Give any heavy edge at `c` that is not incident to a star leaf phase `0`, and delete it when `T=0`; an edge from `c` to a leaf retains that leaf's phase assignment. Other heavy edges may be given any one phase. Every heavy edge still survives with probability `3/4`, and deleting edges preserves the matching property. Let `r_c^0(M)` be the center residual in the **original** full heavy matching, and `r_c(M,T)>=r_c^0(M)` its residual after deletion. For `k>=4`, every phase leaves a prefix leaf fully free, so the prefix recourse equals `r_c(M,T)` in every state. Its expectation is `1/4+(3/4)D_c >=(3/4)min(D_c,sum_{i<=k}D_i)`. For `k=3`, if a prefix leaf was originally free, the recourse equals the new center residual in all phases. If all three were originally covered, phases corresponding to their three distinct indices each delete one leaf edge; on those three phases the recourse is the new center residual, at least `r_c^0(M)`. Hence conditional on **any** original matching, the four-phase mean recourse is at least `(3/4)r_c^0(M)`. Averaging gives `(3/4)D_c >=(3/4)min(D_c,sum_{i<=3}D_i)`. The one- and two-leaf prefixes remain unresolved by this argument; a fixed arbitrary full matching law can fail at one leaf (two disjoint unit-heavy edges chosen anticorrelated with original marginals `1/2` each yield `1/4` fluid availability after phase thinning, below the target `3/8`). An optimized full heavy matching law may avoid that failure. This is a reduction of the single-heavy-center star problem to its first two profit leaves, not a complete heavy-center theorem.
