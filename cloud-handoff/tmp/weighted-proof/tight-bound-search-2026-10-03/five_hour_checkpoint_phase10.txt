1. MAIN NEW TOOL: DIRECT PHYSICAL PALETTE COMPLETION
Read oct5_arbitrary_heavy_mixed_tiny_physical_palette_audit.txt first, then
its independent check oct5_physical_heavy_tiny_product_rounding_audit.txt.
The first note has the strongest scope: the precolored core need not be
properly colored, only feasibly weighted-colored. The second note initially
states the proper-core special case, and its proof verifies the same steps.

Let G=J union T be a finite loopless multigraph, with a given feasible
K-coloring of J. Every remaining edge has weight at most tau<1/4. Put
c=1-4tau, W(v)=total core weight at v, and T(v)=total remaining weight.
If every remaining edge uv obeys

 c^2 K >= max(T(u),T(v)) + c(W(u)+W(v)),

then the given core coloring extends to all physical edges in the SAME
K colors. This is a finite theorem, uniform in graph order, multiplicity,
and minimum positive tiny weight. It has a polynomial-time construction
for rational input and polynomially many supplied color slots.

Proof mechanism: reserve 4tau at each vertex-color slot, leaving
r(v,k)=(1-4tau-coreload(v,k))_+. The inequality
(c-a)_+(c-b)_+ >= c^2-c(a+b) makes
S(uv)=sum_k r(u,k)r(v,k) >= max(T(u),T(v))>0.
Assign each tiny physical edge fraction r(u,k)r(v,k)/S(uv) to color k.
Every edge's fractions sum to one; vertex-color loads fit the reserved
capacities. Iterative LP rounding drops a tight row with at most four
fractional variables: basicness gives n<=m+t, item equalities give
m<=n/2, and two endpoints give at most2n row incidences. Fix all zero
variables permanently, so a dropped row gains at most4tau. Positive
reserved rows fit the original residual; zero rows are never used.
No divisible-to-physical gap or sampling argument remains in this lemma.

COROLLARY. If edges are heavy (>1/2) or tiny (<=tau), let H be maximum
heavy edge-count degree, N>=Delta_w, and Lmax maximum heavy weighted
load over tiny-edge endpoints. A sufficient palette is

 max{3ceil(H/2), ceil((N+c Lmax)/c^2)}

in general; replace the first term by H for bipartite heavy support.
The general proof includes an elementary polynomial proper heavy-coloring
algorithm: even-regular augmentation, Euler orientation, bipartite perfect
matchings, then three colors per resulting cycle factor.
For N=bp, H<=N. If Lmax<=N/2, this is (3/2+O(tau))N+O(1), with arbitrary
heavy and tiny supports. In bipartite graphs Lmax<=N/3 similarly gives
(4/3+O(tau))N+O(1). Choose tau depending on desired accuracy; fixed tau
does NOT make O(tau) vanish as bp grows. The computable bound can use
N=Delta_w and retain H separately; no exact bp computation is needed.
The bipartite load-threshold corollary does not improve the existing harmonic
charge bound in this heavy/tiny regime: H_v-W_v<=W_v for heavy weights>1/2.
The substantial new scoped improvement is on general graphs. The medium-weight
range is still missing. No unrestricted improvement or literature-novelty
claim is justified by this result alone.
