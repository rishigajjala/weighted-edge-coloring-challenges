# Physical tiny caps and a candidate route to a bipartite transfer

**Status (2026-10-03): candidate fractional theorem; integer palette remains open.** The capped activation theorem below removes the arbitrary-support cap obstacle. It has not yet been checked by an independent full proof audit. No unrestricted integer theorem is claimed.

## Absolute slack by inflating heavy weights

Let every physical tiny edge have weight at most `tau`, and let a feasible local one-bin vector consist of heavy categorical marginals `x_h` and tiny load vector `y_e`, with `sum_h w_h x_h + sum_e y_e <= 1` at each vertex. Fix `s=1-k tau>0`, with `k` a small integer, and define `w'_h=min(1,w_h/s)`, `x'_h=s x_h`, and `y'_e=y_e`. Then `w'_h x'_h<=w_h x_h`, so `(x',y')` is locally feasible for the pseudo-heavy weights. Applying the established bipartite independent-heavy fluid theorem at factor `alpha=3/4` gives pseudo-heavy marginals `alpha s x_h` and normalized fluid loads `alpha y_e`. Multiplying fluid loads by `s` yields actual heavy marginals and tiny loads both scaled by `alpha s`.

In each resulting state, normalized fluid at vertex `v` is at most `1-w'_h` (or `1` in the empty state). The actual tiny load is therefore at most `s(1-w'_h)=(s-w_h)_+` when heavy `h` is present, and at most `s` when no heavy is present. If `w_h<=s`, this is exactly `k tau` below actual residual `1-w_h`; if `w_h>s`, the pseudo-fluid is zero at that vertex. Thus every positive pseudo-fluid use has `k tau` absolute vertex slack. This removes the sub-tiny-residual obstruction to local physical rounding. It does **not** enforce each physical edge's one-copy cap: pseudo-fluid on one edge can exceed that edge's weight.

The capped activation theorem below supplies a conditional pseudo-fluid vector with the stronger bound `0<=z_e<=12w_e/N` when the local target is the physical one `y_e=w_e/N` and `N>=12`: set the recourse cap at its **minimum allowed value** `C_e=12y_e`. Apply dependent rounding to the per-edge inclusion fractions `s z_e/w_e`, each at most `12s/N`. Since the tiny support is bipartite, every vertex weighted-load row can be maintained until it has at most **one** fractional incident edge. While the fractional support has an even cycle (parallel 2-cycles included), alternating perturbations of size inversely proportional to the edge weights preserve all weighted degrees. Once it is a forest with `m` fractional edges, the number of vertices having fractional degree at least two is at most `m-1`, so a nonzero nullspace direction preserves all still-active rows. Randomly choose the two boundary points of each nullspace segment with probabilities preserving the current vector. A released row rises by at most one tiny edge weight, hence at most `tau`. Taking `k=1`, or `s=1-tau`, makes each rounded physical subset feasible and preserves every edge marginal. If the cap theorem and the heavy-pair transfer below are accepted, this proves a **fractional** coloring factor `4/[3(1-tau)]` for bipartite combined support with matching heavy support and arbitrary tiny support, `N>=12` and `tau<1`.

For a finite palette of colors, an analogous source-preserving iterative rounding has each physical edge's color-choice equation plus two vertex/color load rows per variable. On **bipartite** tiny support, keep vertex/color rows while they have at least four live variables. With `m` fractional variables, unfinished edge-choice equations number at most `m/2`, and active load rows at most `m/2`. If either bound is strict, a nullspace move exists. In the equality case, every source row has exactly two live variables and every variable meets both endpoint load rows; in each color having a live variable, the sum of its active left load rows equals the sum of its active right load rows, giving a row dependency. Hence a nullspace move still exists. A released load row gains at most `3 tau`, so `k>=3` supplies enough slack **provided** a finite fractional palette assignment with all edge-choice sums exactly one and edge boxes exists. A one-color distribution alone does not provide that finite assignment or an exact heavy schedule.

## Capped forest activation lemma

For independent binary vertex activations with `p_v=(1+3D_v)/4`, consider a bipartite forest with edge profits `q_e>=0`, edge caps `C_e>=0`, and a feasible target `y` satisfying `y_e<=C_e/12`. Then

`E max{q·z: 0<=z_e<=C_e, sum_(e incident v) z_e<=1_(v active)} >= (3/4) q·y`.

The proof is the established leaf induction with one adjustment. Delete a leaf `v` and edge `e=uv`, and reduce `D_u` by `y_e`. Conditional on other activations, let `f(t)` be the reduced graph's capped-flow optimum with `u` capacity `t in [0,1]`; `f` is concave and nondecreasing. Put `A=f(0)`, `B=f(1)-f(0)`, and `c_e=min(1,C_e)`. If `u,v` are active, sending `c_e` on the leaf edge and leaving `1-c_e` capacity at `u` gives value at least `f(1-c_e)+q_e c_e >= A+B+c_e(q_e-B)`. Hence adding the leaf changes the expected value by at least

`(3/4)y_e B + p_u p_v c_e (q_e-B)_+`, where `c_e=min(1,C_e)`.

This is at least `(3/4)y_e q_e` if `p_u p_v c_e >= (3/4)y_e`. For `C_e<1`, the cap ratio gives `(3/4)y_e<=C_e/16`, while `p_up_v>=1/16`. For `C_e>=1`, `D_u,D_v>=y_e` and `(1/4+3y_e/4)^2>=3y_e/4` by the square identity. This proves the induction.

For physical local demand `y_e=w_e/N`, the cap ratio holds when `N>=12`.

## Arbitrary-support capped activation theorem

The forest condition can be removed by decomposing the **target and its cap together**. Fix the original `D`, and let `P_D={z>=0: sum_(e incident v)z_e<=D_v}` on a bipartite graph. Every extreme point has positive support free of even cycles, hence a forest (parallel 2-cycles included). Write any feasible target as a convex combination `y=sum_k lambda_k y^k` of such extreme points. Define temporary cap vectors `C^k=12y^k`. They need not be bounded by the original cap `C`; their **average** satisfies `sum_k lambda_k C^k=12y<=C` coordinatewise.

For a fixed activation state `A`, the capped-flow value `F_q(A,C)` is nondecreasing and concave in the cap vector: combine feasible flows for cap vectors `C^k`, and their average is feasible under the average cap. Thus

`F_q(A,C) >= F_q(A,12y) >= sum_k lambda_k F_q(A,C^k)`.

Apply the capped forest lemma to each `(y^k,C^k)` with the **same** capacity vector `D` and therefore the same vertex-activation probabilities. Average over `A` and `k` to obtain

`E_A F_q(A,C) >= (3/4) q·y`.

This is an exact theorem for arbitrary bipartite support, nonnegative profits, heterogeneous `D`, and real edge caps satisfying `C_e>=12y_e`. The fixed-cap forest-mixture obstruction does not apply: e.g. the `K_(n,n)` example above cannot decompose `y` into forest-supported vectors that each obey the *same* caps, but it can decompose `y` while the temporary caps vary and average back to `12y`.

## Capped heavy-pair transfer

The independent heavy-pair reduction remains valid with edge caps. The recourse value is concave in vertex capacities, so an independent common real residual at each heavy pair can be replaced by a common Bernoulli residual of the same mean without increasing expected recourse. Opposite-side supermodularity of capped bipartite flow then says splitting each common Bernoulli pair into independent endpoint activations also cannot increase expected recourse.

For completeness, let `u` be on the left and `v` on the right. Take optimal capped flows `f` in the state with `u` alone activated and `g` in the state with `v` alone activated, with all other vertex states fixed. Remove the common edgewise flow `min(f,g)`. Decompose the residual red (`f`) and blue (`g`) quantities into alternating weighted trails, pairing opposite-color mass at each intermediate vertex and leaving only one color of unpaired mass there. A trail that starts blue at `v` cannot end red at `u`: that alternating trail has even length, whereas a path from right to left has odd length. Swap red and blue on all trails incident to `v`. The two new flows belong to the both-active and neither-active states, respectively. At every edge each new flow lies between the old two, so its cap is respected; at intermediate vertices the two color loads are preserved, and at trail endpoints the changed loads lie between the original loads, so vertex capacities are respected. Total profit is unchanged. This gives `F(S+u+v,C)+F(S,C)>=F(S+u,C)+F(S+v,C)`.

Consequently the capped independent-activation theorem transfers to matching heavy support in a bipartite combined graph. For every nonnegative profit vector, the expected capped recourse in independently sampled categorical heavy-pair states supports at least `(3/4)y`. As in the uncapped proof, downward closure and support-function separation give a conditional capped-flow policy with **coordinatewise exact** mean `(3/4)y` and heavy-edge marginals `(3/4)x`. The cap restriction is `z_e<=C_e` in every one-color state.

The **independent-vertex** capped activation theorem itself also holds for a general loopless multigraph. Take its bipartite double cover: each original `uv` becomes `u_L v_R` and `v_L u_R`, each with cap `C_uv` and profit `q_uv`; duplicate `D` and `y`. With independent activations of all clones, the bipartite theorem gives expected recourse at least `2(3/4)q·y`. Correlate each twin pair `u_L,u_R` (opposite shores) to a common Bernoulli activation; opposite-shore supermodularity can only increase expectation. For each resulting shared activation/capacity vector `r`, double-cover recourse is exactly twice original capped recourse: duplicate an original optimum for one direction, while averaging the two oriented flows of any double-cover feasible solution gives an original feasible flow with edge cap `C` and vertex capacity `r`. Divide by two. This general-graph statement does **not** transfer categorical original heavy edges whose endpoints may interact on the same side or on a nonbipartite cycle; the independent-heavy-pair reduction used above still needs combined bipartiteness.

## Direct edge induction obstacle

For a general edge `uv`, removing it leaves both endpoints nonleaf. Let the reduced value with binary capacities at `u,v` be `f_00,f_10,f_01,f_11`; opposite-shore supermodularity allows a positive cross term `k=f_11+f_00-f_10-f_01`. Lowering both activation probabilities by `t=(3/4)y_e` changes expected reduced value by `t(d_u+d_v)+[t(p_u+p_v)-t^2]k`, where `d_u=f_10-f_00`, `d_v=f_01-f_00`. A crude insertion estimate charges the new edge against `f_11-f_00=d_u+d_v+k`. At low activation probabilities, the coefficient on `k` is about `t/2`, insufficient to pay `tk`. Thus the capped leaf proof does not extend edge-by-edge without a sharper use of the cross term.

The direct induction failure does not affect the convex-decomposition proof above. The finite-palette integer conversion remains separate: the one-color probability law with exact marginals gives a fractional coloring, but it does not itself choose each physical edge exactly once across `M` shared colors while maintaining the needed per-color slack. The older unrestricted weighted-edge-coloring goal therefore remains open.

## A physical quantifier-gap example for a tighter palette

The implication “a mixture of exact-heavy schedules has enough capped tiny recourse in every profit direction, therefore one exact-heavy schedule serves all tiny stocks” is false without further structure or slack. Here is a bipartite physical example at `N=M=4`. It does **not** contradict the prospective `M≈4N/3` theorem, whose extra palette colors may remove this obstruction.

Put two disjoint heavy pairs `P=p_Lp_R` and `Q=q_Lq_R`, each with two parallel unit-weight heavy physical edges. Add a left-side hub `c_L` and three tiny bundles: `e_0=p_Lq_R` of total physical weight `1.1`, `e_1=c_Lp_R` of total weight `1.3`, and `e_2=c_Lq_R` of total weight `0.8`. Realize each total by edges of weight `tau=0.1/k` for an integer `k>=1`, so `tau` can tend to zero and every displayed decimal is an integer multiple of `tau`. The local bin count is four: the two unit heavies use two bins at each pair endpoint, while the incident tiny totals are `1.1,1.3,1.9,0` at `p_L,p_R,q_R,q_L`; the hub total is `2.1`.

In four colors each heavy pair has exactly two heavy-free colors. Let `k` be the overlap of the two free sets. If `k<=1`, the `e_0` bundle has at most one usable color and can carry at most one unit, short of `1.1`. If `k=2`, the free sets coincide, so the hub's `e_1+e_2` total `2.1` must fit in only two colors. Thus no exact four-color physical assignment exists.

Five colors suffice: put all four heavy physical edges in colors `1,2` (one from each pair in each color). In free colors `3,4,5`, use bundle loads respectively `(e_0,e_1,e_2)=(1,1,0)`, `(0.1,0.3,0.7)`, and `(0,0,0.1)`. These are sums of actual `tau`-edges and satisfy every vertex/color capacity. Hence this example has `bp=4`, `chi'_w=5`, while the fractional tables below give `chi'_(f,w)<=4`.

Nevertheless a 50/50 mixture of two **four-color flow tables** covers all three tiny bundle stocks in expectation while every individual color is feasible and obeys each tiny physical edge's one-color cap. In the `k=2` table, the two common free colors carry bundle loads `(e_0,e_1,e_2)=(1,1,0)` and `(1,0.6,0)`. In the `k=1` table, take `P` free in colors `0,1` and `Q` free in `1,2`; color `0` carries `(0,1,0)`, color `1` carries `(0.2,0,0.8)`, and color `2` carries `(0,0,0.8)`. Their average aggregate is exactly `(1.1,1.3,0.8)`. By symmetrizing within each tiny bundle, the per-color physical-edge marginals can be made equal across its edges. The tables reuse some physical tiny edges across colors in one table; that is legal for a fractional-coloring mixture but illegal for one integer coloring. This is the precise failure of replacing an average capped recourse inequality by one schedule's source-preserving feasibility.

The manuscript's `thm:round` and `lem:bp-mult-round` do not directly repair this gap: their graph-size-independent partial-coloring theorem assumes `x_(e,c)=O(1/N)` and simple or bounded-multiplicity support. The heavy parallel bundles here may have multiplicity proportional to `N`. If one uses the loose physical cap `C_e=w_e`, conditional tiny inclusion can be near one; the minimal cap `C_e=12w_e/N` above fixes this smallness issue. It does not give deterministic palette fractions satisfying each physical edge's source sum exactly one, and the conditional flow policy may depend on heavy states throughout the graph. Exact heavy injections plus a fresh finite-palette tiny-flow existence theorem are still required.
