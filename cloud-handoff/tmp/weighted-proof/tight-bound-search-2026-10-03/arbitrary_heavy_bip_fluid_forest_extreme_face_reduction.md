# Extreme-face reduction for arbitrary bipartite heavy support with forest fluid

**Status (2026-10-03, within the required five-hour research interval):** verified linear-algebra reduction, not a mixed-hull inclusion. Heavy simple support may be arbitrary bipartite; parallel heterogeneous heavy types are allowed. Fluid is uncapped/divisible. This note isolates the missing rounding problem to low-dimensional faces of the heavy matching polytope when a local extreme target has few positive-fluid components.

Let `P_H={x≥0: Σ_{h∋v}x_h≤1 for every vertex}`. Because the heavy support is bipartite, `P_H` is the ordinary bipartite matching polytope, including parallel physical heavy types, and every vertex is an actual heavy matching. Add nonnegative fluid variables `y_e` and weighted vertex-load inequalities

`Σ_{h∋v}w_hx_h+Σ_{e∋v}y_e≤1`, with `0≤w_h≤1`.

Fix an extreme point `(x,y)` of this mixed local polytope. Let the positive-fluid graph `F={e:y_e>0}` have `c` nonempty connected components. The support `F` must be a forest: every even cycle gives an alternating-sign perturbation of its positive fluid variables while leaving all vertex loads and `x` fixed. Since the combined graph is bipartite, all cycles in `F` are even. Parallel positive fluid edges give the same two-column dependence.

**Claim.** The minimal face of `P_H` containing `x` has affine dimension at most `c`. Consequently, if `c=1`, `x` is an integral heavy matching or a point on one edge of `P_H`, so it is a convex combination of at most two integral heavy matchings. More generally it can be expressed as a convex combination of at most `c+1` heavy matchings by Carathéodory within its face.

**Proof.** Let `T` be the direction space of the minimal face of `P_H` containing `x`, with dimension `d`. A sufficiently small perturbation `x±εz` stays in `P_H` for any `z∈T`. At a vertex with no positive fluid edge, the weighted-load inequality cannot add an independent active constraint: `Σw_hx_h≤Σx_h≤1`; equality in the weighted row forces categorical degree one and positive support only on weight-one heavy edges, where the weighted row is the same as the degree row along the face. Thus only load rows at positive-fluid components matter.

Consider a positive-fluid tree component `C` with `n` vertices and `n−1` positive edges. Its unsigned incidence matrix `A_C` has rank `n−1`, and its sole left-null vector has nonzero alternating signs on the two shores. Let `R_C` be the subset of vertex load rows active at `(x,y)`. If `R_C` is a proper subset of all `n` rows, the restricted incidence matrix `A_C[R_C,*]` has full row rank, so every heavy perturbation `z∈T` can be offset by a perturbation of the positive fluid variables to preserve these active rows. If all `n` load rows are active, there is exactly **one** linear compatibility condition on `z`: the weighted heavy-load change must be orthogonal to the alternating left-null vector. Hence each component imposes at most one homogeneous linear restriction on `T`.

If `d>c`, choose a nonzero `z∈T` satisfying all these at-most-`c` restrictions. In each fluid component choose a corresponding fluid perturbation that preserves its active load rows. All positive fluid coordinates and all inactive inequalities have positive slack, so sufficiently small perturbations in both signs are feasible. This contradicts extremality of `(x,y)`. Therefore `d≤c`, as claimed.

**Interpretation and limitation.** The one-tree case reduces the heavy marginal vector to a two-matching segment. Endpoints of one edge of a bipartite matching polytope differ on a single connected alternating path or even cycle: if their symmetric difference had two disjoint alternating components, flipping either component alone would give two other feasible matchings whose average lies on the same segment, contradicting adjacency. Thus the heterogeneous rounding problem on one fluid tree reduces to one connected alternating heavy structure. Its weighted endpoint residuals can still be very different. The reduction alone does not show that a `3/4` heavy-marginal law yields enough fluid recourse, and multiple positive-fluid components can produce a face dimension growing with graph size. The known four-edge alternating-heavy-path plus one fluid chord diagnostic has exactly this two-matching shape at full marginals, while scaling allows extra partial heavy matchings that repair its deficit.

## Boundary-only compatibility and an exact special case

The compatibility equation for a fluid tree component `C` is more explicit. Give its two shores signs `+1` and `−1`. For a heavy-face direction `z`, the equation is

`Σ_{v in C} sign(v) Σ_{h incident v} w_h z_h = 0`.

Every heavy edge with both endpoints in `C` cancels term by term, irrespective of its weight. Only heavy edges with exactly one endpoint in `C` can contribute. In the one-component case, if `x` lies in the interior of a one-dimensional heavy face, extremality further requires this boundary functional to be nonzero on the unique face direction; otherwise the heavy direction can be offset by fluid perturbations. Thus the alternating path/cycle by which the two endpoint heavy matchings differ must have a nonzero **weighted boundary effect**. Merely having internal heterogeneous heavy edges cannot create a fractional local vertex.

An exact hull consequence is worth recording. Suppose every heavy edge lies wholly inside one positive-fluid component or wholly outside all such components; in particular, no heavy edge crosses any positive-fluid component boundary. Then every component compatibility functional vanishes identically. The dimension argument above forces `d=0` at every mixed local extreme. Hence each extreme has an integral heavy matching. Conditional on that matching, its fluid vector satisfies the actual residual vertex capacities, so the extreme itself is a feasible one-color mixed state (fluid is divisible). By convexity, the entire local mixed polytope equals the one-color convex hull, with no scaling loss. This is a **support-dependent** statement about positive-fluid support at every extreme under consideration; it is not a theorem for arbitrary mixed instances, because a different extreme can have boundary-crossing heavy guards.
