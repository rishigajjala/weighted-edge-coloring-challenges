# Bounded-degree finite palette from localized capped recourse

**Status (2026-10-03): research candidate under the five-hour protocol, not an unrestricted bound.** This strengthens `bounded_tiny_degree_palette_conditional.md`: the explicit radius-one policy conjecture is unnecessary when the simple tiny-support degree is fixed. The argument still needs a full independent audit, especially the finite-palette and rounding interfaces.

## Localize the existing capped theorem

Let `y` be any feasible normalized tiny target, with degree bounds `D_v<=1`, and physical edge caps at least `12y_e`. Decompose `y=sum_k lambda_k y^k` into extreme points of the *uncapped* fractional degree polytope `P_D={z>=0: sum_(e at v)z_e<=D_v}`. Each positive support `y^k` is a forest (also for a bipartite multigraph, where two parallel edges form a cycle). Give it temporary edge caps `C^k_e=12y^k_e`. Their average is `12y_e`, so per-state convex averaging of the resulting flows stays within the physical cap.

Root each forest. Choose a residue `j` uniformly modulo an integer `R`; delete edges whose child depths equal `j` modulo `R`. Every surviving connected component has diameter at most `2R`, and each original forest edge survives with probability `1-1/R`. The restriction of `y^k` to each component remains feasible under the *original* local degree bounds `D_v`. Do not decrease `D_v` when components are cut: the heavy-state activation probabilities are fixed by the original local vector.

Apply the capped heavy-pair activation theorem separately to each component, with target its restricted `y^k`, caps `12y^k`, and the marginal law of heavy states on the matched pairs meeting that component. A pair with only one endpoint in the component contributes its ordinary marginal. A pair with both endpoints contributes the correct shared categorical heavy state. The opposite-shore supermodularity transfer applies within the component. By support-function separation, there is a **single profit-independent conditional feasible flow policy** for the component whose expected flow on each surviving edge is at least `(3/4)y^k_e`. This component policy need not be the restriction of an arbitrary global policy; choosing it directly under the paired law avoids any unsupported assertion that an independent-state policy improves under correlation. Average over policies, residues and forest terms, then condition away their auxiliary randomness. The resulting per-state flow `Z_e` obeys

`0<=Z_e<=12y_e`, `sum_(e at v)Z_e<=R_v` and `E Z_e>=(3/4)(1-1/R)y_e`.

For each edge `e`, its flow in a given component depends only on the heavy states of matched pairs meeting that component. A component has diameter at most `2R` in the tiny-support graph; after contracting matched heavy pairs, the relevant pair IDs lie in an interaction-graph ball of radius at most `2R+1` around `e`. Averaging over the fixed forest decomposition does not expand this radius. Auxiliary Bernoulli interpolation for a real pair residual is applied within the component; it preserves pointwise capacity and locality.

## Finite palette when simple tiny degree is bounded

If the simple tiny support has maximum degree `d`, the contracted pair interaction graph has degree at most `2d`, independent of heavy parallel multiplicity and tiny parallel multiplicity. The radius `2R+1` neighborhood of any edge then contains at most `L(d,R)` pair IDs. Color a sufficiently high power of this graph using `T(d,R)` labels so these IDs have distinct labels. The exact-heavy-stock construction and constant-dimensional permutation box discrepancy in `bounded_tiny_degree_palette_conditional.md` now apply to **any** bounded local policy, not just the conjectured explicit one. For sufficiently large `N`, the empirical local state law approximates the product state law on every edge simultaneously with error `o(1)y_e`, independent of graph order; the temporary cap `12y_e` makes the absolute discrepancy proportional to the physical edge weight. The localization loss `1/R` is absorbed by the slack in the normalized target; then source-preserving tiny rounding uses the reserved physical `3tau` vertex/color slack.

The resulting research candidate is a `(4/3+epsilon)N+O_(d,epsilon)(1)` physical integer palette for the matching-heavy bipartite regime with bounded **simple** tiny degree, all parallel multiplicities unrestricted, provided the cited capped theorem and the palette/rounding interfaces survive full verification.

## Why this does not yet settle unbounded tiny degree

The local radius is fixed, but its neighborhood cardinality grows with simple degree. The component policy chosen by separation may depend on all heavy states in a large star, and no Lipschitz or weighted-sum representation of that dependence is known. The weighted-defective finite-type bridge in `weighted_defective_palette_bridge_conditional.md` uses the explicit reciprocal-max policy to reduce many weak neighbors to a balanced additive load; the localized abstract policy does not automatically have that property. A new stability or weak-neighbor averaging lemma is needed before that bridge applies to arbitrary simple tiny support.
