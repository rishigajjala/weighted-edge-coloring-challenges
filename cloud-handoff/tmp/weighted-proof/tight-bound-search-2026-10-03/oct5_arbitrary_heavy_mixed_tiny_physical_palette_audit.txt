5 October 2026 — audit of arbitrary-heavy mixed/tiny physical palette

STATUS. This proof was proposed by the root agent and independently checked
here. It is a nonasymptotic physical coloring theorem for a genuine mixed
heavy regime, with arbitrary heavy topology, arbitrary tiny topology and
parallel multiplicity. The palette-completion part actually allows a
precolored core of arbitrary weights and topology; the heavy/tiny result
is its immediate corollary. No manuscript or Lean source was changed.

GENERAL PALETTE-COMPLETION LEMMA. Let G=J∪T be a finite loopless
weighted multigraph, every edge in T having weight at most τ∈(0,1/4).
Suppose J has any feasible weighted K-coloring. Write W_v for the
total weight of J-edges at v, T_v for the total weight of T-edges at
v, and c=1-4τ. If every tiny edge uv satisfies

  c² K >= max(T_u,T_v)+c(W_u+W_v),                 (LOCAL)

then the given J-coloring extends to a feasible K-coloring of all G.
No properness of J's color classes is needed: several core edges may
share one color at a vertex as long as their total weight is at most
one. The criterion is local to tiny-edge endpoints.

If T is empty, completion is immediate. Otherwise, if
N>=max_v(W_v+T_v) and A=max_{v incident to T} W_v/N, then the
simpler sufficient condition

  K >= N(1+cA)/c²                                  (GLOBAL)

implies (LOCAL). For uv∈T, set W_hi=max(W_u,W_v) and
W_lo=min(W_u,W_v). Since T_v<=N-W_v,

  max(T_u,T_v)+c(W_u+W_v)
    <=N-W_lo+c(W_hi+W_lo)
    =N+cW_hi-(1-c)W_lo<=N+cAN.

This completion lemma may be applied after a medium-edge or packet
precoloring, but does not itself construct that precoloring.

THEOREM. Let G be a finite loopless weighted multigraph. Suppose each edge
has weight either strictly above 1/2 (heavy) or at most τ (tiny), where
0<τ<1/4. Let N=bp(G)>=1, let H be the maximum number of heavy physical
edges incident to a vertex, and put

  A=max_{v incident to a tiny edge}(1/N)Σ_{h∋v}w_h,
  c=1-4τ.

For any integer K satisfying

  K >= 3ceil(H/2),
  K >= N(1+cA)/c²,

the whole graph has a feasible weighted K-coloring. In particular, since
H<=N, one may take K=max{3ceil(H/2),ceil(N(1+cA)/c²)}.
As N tends to infinity, this is at most

  max(3/2, (1+cA)/c²)N+O(1).

For bipartite G, replace the first threshold by K>=H; then the same
proof gives K=ceil{N(1+cA)/c²}. At A<=1/2 these are respectively
(3/2+O(τ))N and (3/2+O(τ))N, while the theorem permits A up to one.

PROOF. Each heavy physical edge needs its own local bin, so H<=N.
Also Σ_{e∋v}w_e<=N for every vertex. The self-contained proper
heavy-coloring algorithm below uses 3ceil(H/2) colors; add unused
colors to obtain K. In the bipartite case use Kőnig instead. From
here onward the proof works for any feasible precolored core J.

Let h_{v,c} be the weight of J-edges of color c incident to v,
R_{v,c}=1-h_{v,c}, and

  R'_{v,c}=max(0,R_{v,c}-4τ)=max(0,c-h_{v,c}),
  S_{uv}=Σ_d R'_{u,d}R'_{v,d}.

For a,b∈[0,1] one has

  (c-a)_+(c-b)_+ >= c²-c(a+b):

if a+b>=c, the right side is nonpositive; otherwise both positive
parts open and the difference is ab>=0. Since Σ_d h_{v,d}=W_v,

  S_{uv}>=c²K-c(W_u+W_v)>=max(T_u,T_v)>0

for every tiny edge uv, by (LOCAL). Give that physical edge the
fractional color assignment

  z_{e,c}=R'_{u,c}R'_{v,c}/S_{uv}.

These fractions are nonnegative, at most one, and sum to one. At
vertex v in color c, use S_{uv}>=T_v and R'_{u,c}<=1 to get

  Σ_{e=uv tiny} w_e z_{e,c}
    = R'_{v,c} Σ_{e=uv tiny} w_e R'_{u,c}/S_{uv}
    <=R'_{v,c} Σ_{e∋v tiny}w_e/T_v
    =R'_{v,c}.

Thus z is a feasible fractional assignment of every physical tiny
edge to the K actual core-color slots, with 4τ held back at each slot.

Round z by iterative relaxation of the assignment LP. Maintain one
equation Σ_c z_{e,c}=1 for each unassigned tiny physical edge and one
weighted-load inequality per active vertex-color row. Fix 0/1
variables, remove their edge equations, and update row capacities.
Take a basic feasible solution. If fractional variables remain, let n
be the number of positive fractional variables, m the number of
unassigned edges, and t the number of tight active vertex-color rows.
Every unassigned edge has at least two fractional choices, so n>=2m.
Basicness gives n<=m+t, hence t>=n/2. Each variable participates in
at most two active rows, one at each endpoint, so total incidences
over tight rows are at most 2n. Some tight row consequently contains
at most four positive fractional variables: if all contained at least
five, then 5t<=2n, whereas t>=n/2 would give 5n/2<=2n.
Drop that row and repeat.

At the moment a row is dropped its fractional load is within its
current capacity. Only the at most four still-fractional variables in
that row can subsequently increase its load; each corresponds to a
tiny edge of weight at most τ. Its final tiny load is therefore at
most R'_{v,c}+4τ. If R'_{v,c}=0, every incident z_{e,c}=0 initially,
and zero variables remain fixed, so its final tiny load is zero.
If R'_{v,c}>0, then R'_{v,c}+4τ<=R_{v,c}. Thus every final color is
physically feasible. Every tiny physical edge is assigned exactly once,
and the heavy edges were already assigned exactly once.

SELF-CONTAINED PROPER HEAVY COLORING. Put r=ceil(H/2). Take two
disjoint copies of the heavy multigraph. For each original vertex v,
join its two copies by 2r-deg_H(v) new parallel dummy edges. The
result is a loopless 2r-regular multigraph on twice as many vertices.
Orient an Euler tour in each connected component, giving every
vertex indegree and outdegree r. Make a bipartite r-regular graph
whose left and right shores are copies of this vertex set; represent
each directed edge u→v by a bipartite edge from left u to right v.
Repeatedly find a perfect matching and delete it. Hall's condition
holds in every regular bipartite residual graph, so this decomposes
the directed edges into r sets having indegree and outdegree one at
each vertex. Each set is a vertex-disjoint union of directed cycles,
including possible two-cycles from parallel edges, and admits a
proper edge coloring with at most three colors. Use separate three-
color palettes for the r sets, then restrict to the original copy
of the heavy graph. This constructs a proper heavy coloring with
exactly at most 3r=3ceil(H/2) colors in polynomial time. For H=0,
the empty heavy coloring uses zero colors; the completion palette
still has K colors from (GLOBAL).

DETAILS/QUANTIFIERS. The denominator S_{uv}>0 was checked above.
The z variables are automatically <=1 because each numerator is one
nonnegative term in the denominator; no additional stock constraint
is needed. The iterative rounding works for arbitrary parallel
physical edges since each edge has its own assignment equation and
touches exactly its two endpoint-color rows. The proof is finite and
nonasymptotic. Fix every initially zero z variable permanently,
including every row with R'=0; otherwise a later LP could introduce
new choices in a dropped row and invalidate the four-edge count.
The process is polynomial in the number of physical edges and K.
If a sufficient K exceeds the physical edge count, assigning one
distinct color to each edge is trivially feasible and keeps the
algorithm polynomial in input size. Computing exact bp for the stated
ratio is a separate algorithmic issue.

SCOPE. This improves the existing harmonic-charge treatment on mixed
heavy (>1/2) plus tiny (<=τ) instances when A is not too close to
one. For unit-heavy plus tiny edges the manuscript already has
stronger coefficients, as recorded in oct5_unit_heavy_scope_audit.md.
The new contribution is retention of residual capacity when heavy
weights are strictly below one. The general completion lemma accepts
medium edges inside its already colored core, but does not construct
that core coloring. Thus it does not improve the unrestricted constants
by itself.

MEDIUM-CORE APPLICATION. The same self-contained proper coloring
works for any designated core J whose edges have weight at least
ρ>0, without a >1/2 condition. Let N=bp(G), let A=max_v W_v/N,
and suppose all remaining edges weigh at most τ<1/4. Since every
core edge contributes at least ρ to weighted degree, its maximum
ordinary degree H satisfies H<=AN/ρ. Color J with 3ceil(H/2)
colors and then apply (GLOBAL). This gives the physical bound

  χ'_w(G) <= max{3ceil(AN/(2ρ)), ceil[N(1+cA)/c²]}

in a general multigraph; in a bipartite graph replace the first
term by ceil(AN/ρ). Using actual H in place of AN/ρ is sharper.
For example, when ρ=49/100, A<=1/2, and τ tends to zero, the
general coefficient is at most 75/49≈1.5307 and the bipartite
coefficient at most 3/2. This includes physical medium edges below
one half, arbitrary topology, and arbitrary parallel copies.

LIMIT OF REPLACING THE MANUSCRIPT'S 1.999 FINISH. To fill tiny edges
inside a κN-color precolored core palette using (LOCAL), one needs
for every tiny uv

  max(T_u,T_v)/N+c(W_u+W_v)/N <= c²κ.

For κ=1999/1000 and τ tending to zero, the global sufficient
condition (GLOBAL) is available when A<=κ−1=999/1000,
with an O(τ) deduction from that threshold for positive τ.
The current 1.999 proof does not guarantee this: a vertex can have
core weighted degree arbitrarily close to N while still incident
to a tiny edge. For a concrete load pattern, put q unit core edges
at each of two endpoints u,v and one tiny edge uv; then bp=q+1,
W_u=W_v=q and T_u=T_v is tiny. At κ(q+1) colors, the bound (LOCAL)
fails for all sufficiently large q at every c∈(0,1), because
cκ<2. The actual color overlap might still be ample, but the local
weighted-load data do not certify it. The manuscript's residual
insertion also manages medium residual objects and block endpoint
labels; this completion lemma handles only genuinely tiny physical
edges after a feasible core coloring has been established. Thus it
can replace that bridge only in a subclass where (LOCAL) is proved
for the constructed core palette, not in the unrestricted 1.999
proof on the present evidence. Adding the missing roughly 0.001N
colors in this saturated example would reach coefficient two and
erase the strict sub-two conclusion.

ADDITIONAL SCOPE CHECK BY ROOT. The new general-graph load-sensitive bound
can improve the existing harmonic-plus-Shannon estimate. The bipartite
heavy/tiny corollary should not be presented as a better constant than
existing harmonic charging: at a vertex H_v-W_v<=W_v because all heavy
weights exceed1/2, so harmonic charge H_v+T_v is already at most
N+W_v up to the tiny rounding loss. The generic completion lemma may
still simplify a proof or apply to a supplied medium core; that is a
separate benefit from improving a bipartite coefficient.
