# Bounded heavy components: the reusable capped transfer input

**Research note, 2026-10-03; candidate mathematics under audit.** This isolates what the sparsify–localize–finite-label physical bridge actually needs. A matching of heavy pairs is one way to provide the input, but the palette argument itself only needs bounded independent heavy state components. No unrestricted bipartite or general-graph bound is claimed here.

## Abstract input and physical consequence

Partition the heavy simple support into vertex-disjoint connected components `P`, each with at most a fixed `h` physical vertices; bare vertices are singleton components. A one-color heavy state at `P` is a matching of heavy edge **classes** (endpoints and rounded weight bucket), so all states are feasible and the state menu has a size bounded solely by `h` and the fixed bucket count. Components choose states independently. A state specifies a residual capacity vector `R^P in [0,1]^P`, not necessarily a common scalar.

The precise missing/usable one-color input, at coefficient `alpha`, is this: for every feasible heavy fractional matching vector `x` and tiny target `y` with `sum_(heavy e at v) w_e x_e + sum_(tiny e at v)y_e<=1`, there is a product law `mu=product_P mu_P` on heavy-component matching states such that

1. each heavy class `e` has marginal `alpha x_e`, with the law independent of tiny profit vector `q`;
2. for every nonnegative tiny profit `q` and edge caps `C_e>=K y_e`,
   `E_(H~mu) F_q(R(H),C) >= alpha q·y`;
3. the same statement holds when the tiny graph is restricted to any local component, using the unchanged heavy marginals and local degree upper bounds.

Here `F_q` is maximum capped fractional tiny-flow profit in one color. By support-function separation and downward closure, condition 2 yields a **single q-independent** conditional flow policy with coordinatewise mean at least `alpha y` and cap `K y_e`. Condition 3 permits forest/pseudoforest localization. The matching-pair theorem in `physical_tiny_cap_rounding_route.md` gives this input for `alpha=3/4,K=12` on bipartite combined support.

Under this input and bounded `h`, the physical bridge in `sparsify_localize_type_palette_bridge_candidate.md` extends almost verbatim. Contract heavy components instead of pairs. The sampled pair-graph row bound `2/a` becomes `h/a`; a cut tiny component still has bounded physical size and hence touches only boundedly many heavy component IDs. The finite-label lemma gives graph-order-independent types; a Vandermonde block makes their arbitrary local categorical state functions exactly product-distributed. The state menu per heavy component is bounded by `2^{O(K_b h²)}` after fixed heavy bucketing, so one field size and one block length suffice for all graph orders.

For pruning compatibility there is a particularly clean variant that avoids Bernoulli augmentation. Start with heavy target `x`, local free bound `D_v=1-sum_heavy w_e x_e`, and sampled/pruned tiny target `Y^pr`. Scale **both** to `x^0=x/(1+eta)` and `y^0=Y^pr/(1+eta)`. Then

`D^0_v=1-sum_heavy w_e x^0_e=(D_v+eta)/(1+eta)`, and `sum_(tiny at v)y^0_e<=D^0_v`.

Apply the one-color input to `(x^0,y^0)`. This uses heavy marginals `alpha x/(1+eta)` and gives tiny mean `alpha y^0` before forest cuts. Relative to a main palette designed for `alpha x`, an `O(eta)` fraction of heavy identities moves to a separate heavy-only palette. That is harmless for an `alpha^{-1}+epsilon` physical bound and works for a vector residual state of arbitrary fixed dimension `h`. It also avoids needing an `A|R` coordinatewise Bernoulli coupling inside a heavy component.

To make the main heavy counts exact, floor **each nonempty matching-state probability** to a multiple of `1/q` and transfer its removed mass to the empty state. This couples the rounded law to the ideal one by deleting some selected heavy edges, so every residual coordinate increases pointwise. A local flow policy for the ideal law therefore transfers to the rounded law by conditioning on the coupled ideal state; its marginal and cap do not decrease, and its state dependence remains inside the same heavy component. Each heavy edge class has at most `eta n_e+O_{h,K_b}(M/q)` unscheduled physical identities across `M` main colors. At a physical vertex the leftover heavy degree is `O(eta N+M/q)`, and the leftover multigraph can be colored separately in `O(eta N+M/q)` colors (bipartite: its maximum degree; general: the elementary `2Delta-1` greedy bound). Thus bounded **component size**, rather than heavy matching support, is sufficient for the finite-palette part once the one-color input is known.

The exact new bottleneck is condition 2: constructing a single q-independent product law of feasible matchings inside each heavy component whose residual vector is good for **all** capped tiny recourse profits. Bounded `h` makes the problem finite-state but does not prove the stochastic recourse domination. In particular, pairwise covariance signs alone do not control every nonlinear capped flow value.

## A new one-color input for common-weight bipartite components

There is a plausible extension of the capped `3/4` one-color input to *arbitrary-sized* bipartite heavy components whose heavy edges all have one common pseudo-weight `w_P` within each component (weights may differ between components). The following pipage argument was checked independently as a candidate; its same-shore exchange step merits a written full proof audit.

Let `z=alpha x`, `alpha=3/4`, be a fractional heavy matching vector. For each physical vertex let `p_v=1-w_P sum_(heavy e at v) z_e=1/4+3D_v/4`. Define

`Phi_q(p)=E_{A_v independent Bernoulli(p_v)} F_q(A,C)`.

The capped bipartite independent-activation theorem gives `Phi_q(p)>=alpha q·y` when `C>=12y`. For two vertices on opposite shores, capped recourse has nonnegative cross-difference; for two on the same shore it has nonpositive cross-difference. The opposite-shore exchange proof is recorded in `five_hour_capped_activation_audit.md`. The same-shore direction follows by taking optimal flows for both and neither vertices active and alternately exchanging their red/blue difference trails: a trail beginning with a red excess at one vertex cannot terminate in a red excess at the other same-shore vertex, because such an alternating trail would have odd length while same-shore paths have even length. The exchange redistributes the two flows to the two one-active states without changing total profit and preserves edge caps.

Pipage-round `z` within each heavy component. In its fractional support choose an even cycle, or a path between two leaves of a forest. Alternate `+t,-t` on its edges. Internal matching degrees stay fixed. Since all these heavy edges have the same weight `w_P`, their **weighted** occupancies and hence their `p_v` also stay fixed. On a cycle all `p_v` stay fixed and `Phi_q` is constant. On a path only its two endpoints change. If the path has odd length, its endpoints are on opposite shores and their `p` derivatives have the same sign; if it has even length, they are on the same shore and their derivatives have opposite signs. In both cases the cross-difference sign makes the quadratic `Phi_q(p(t))` convex. The path endpoints are slack because they are leaves of fractional support, so the feasible interval contains both negative and positive `t`. Randomly choose its boundary points with probabilities preserving the current `z`. This preserves every heavy edge marginal, and Jensen increases `E Phi_q` for **every** nonnegative `q` simultaneously. The sequence of paths/cycles and the boundary probabilities can be selected from `z` alone, not from `q`; the final matching law is q-independent. Independent rounding in separate heavy components gives the required product law.

For an integral heavy matching state `H`, the actual residual vector `R(H)` has mean `p`; capped recourse is jointly concave in vertex capacities, so pointwise `F_q(R(H),C)>=E_{B_v|R_v independent Bernoulli(R_v)} F_q(B,C)`. Hence `E_H F_q(R(H),C)>=E_H Phi_q(R(H))>=Phi_q(p)>=alpha q·y`. (The second inequality is the pipage result, applied to the multilinear extension.) This supplies the abstract input for componentwise-common heavy weight on bipartite combined support, provided the same-shore exchange audit passes.

Combining this one-color input with the bounded-component physical bridge would give a scoped `(4/3+epsilon)N+O_{epsilon,h}(1)` integer coloring theorem for bipartite combined support with heavy components of at most `h` vertices and one common heavy weight per component, tiny physical edges, and arbitrary parallel multiplicity. The finite-palette argument is not restricted to h=2. It still does **not** address arbitrary heavy weights within a component or unbounded heavy components.

## Why this does not yet settle the unrestricted problem

If heavy weights differ along an alternating path, internal vertices keep their matching **count** degree but not their weighted heavy load; several `p_v` change at once and the shore cross-difference signs no longer force `Phi_q` to be convex along the path. A special matching law may still work, but this argument gives none. Even with common weights, an unbounded connected heavy component has an unbounded matching-state menu, so the finite-field block size from the current physical bridge would depend on graph order. A new local exact-heavy schedule, rather than merely bounded-component contraction, is needed there.

For a nonbipartite combined graph, the independent-vertex capped `3/4` theorem survives via a double cover, and an extreme tiny target has tree or odd-unicyclic support. Random depth/residue cutting localizes the trees and long odd cycles while keeping short odd cycles as bounded components, so the *physical* sparse-label machinery plausibly extends. The obstruction is again the heavy-state transfer: the shore-alternating recourse cross-differences used above are unavailable on an odd cycle, and even independent heavy pairs can fail to dominate independent vertex activation. Bounded heavy components alone do not remove that obstruction.

Numerical context only: random capped-flow LP tests found no same-shore submodularity violation in 5,000 instances, and no matching-law versus product-activation violation in 6,000 unit-heavy 3-vertex-path, 2,500 four-vertex, or 1,700 five-vertex random instances. These tests do not prove the exchange or transfer lemmas. Arbitrary matching laws can have unfavorable residual correlations; the pipage/martingale law above is the specific proposed construction.
