ENDPOINT REDUCTION FOR MIXED GUARD RECOURSE; THREE-LEVEL LAW
5 October 2026

EXACT REDUCTION LEMMA

Fix any finite fluid multigraph (stars, branching, and parallel fluid
identities are all allowed), fixed nonnegative profit vector q, per-edge
caps C, and a random selection of guard PAIRS forming a matching. The
matching-selection law may have arbitrary dependence between pairs but
is chosen without looking at the categorical guard weights realized
afterwards. At each selected pair i, independently of all other pair
types conditional on the selected matching, choose a physical type h
from a fixed distribution p_ih, with weight w_ih in [1/2,1]. The same
pair residual R_i=1-w_ih is used at both endpoints. Unselected pairs
have residual one. For any residual vector R, let

  F_q(R)=max{sum_e q_e f_e: 0<=f_e<=C_e,
            sum_{e incident v} f_e<=R_{P(v)} for every v}.

At unguarded vertices take residual one. The function F_q is jointly
concave and nondecreasing in pair residuals. Indeed, convex-combining
feasible flows at two residual vectors gives a feasible flow at the
combined residual vector; repeating one pair residual at its two
endpoints is an affine restriction.

For a selected pair whose conditional mean weight is mu_i, replace its
residual 1-w by an endpoint residual Z_i in {0,1/2} with

  Pr(Z_i=1/2)=2(1-mu_i),
  Pr(Z_i=0)=2mu_i-1.

This has the same conditional mean residual. For every r in [0,1/2],
concavity gives

  F_q(...,r,...) >= (1-2r)F_q(...,0,...)
                    +2r F_q(...,1/2,...).

Condition first on the selected matching and on all other pair types,
then average and replace pairs one at a time. Consequently the actual
expected optimal recourse is at least the expected optimal recourse of
the endpoint surrogate. This is a lower-bound comparison only: actual
physical categorical type marginals remain those of the original law.
It works for ANY fluid support, not just stars. It also works when the
matching law is a fixed hybrid independent/common occupancy law,
because categorical types are independent of the occupancy uniform
conditional on selection. The surrogate preserves each pair's total
presence x_i and its weighted original load a_i=x_i mu_i, so all local
weighted-load constraints survive exactly. The lemma does not by
itself prove a 2/3 fluid transfer or a physical palette.

ENDPOINT PARAMETERIZATION

At pair i write a_i for original presence of unit-weight types and
b_i for original presence of half-weight types (aggregating physical
identities). Then x_i=a_i+b_i<=1 and weighted load is a_i+b_i/2.
Under alpha=2/3 sampling, the residual has three levels:

  R_i=0 with marginal alpha a_i,
  R_i=1/2 with marginal alpha b_i,
  R_i=1 with marginal 1-alpha(a_i+b_i).

In the fixed hybrid's independent mode these levels are independent
across pairs. In its common occupancy mode a single uniform U first
sets pair occupancy by U<=alpha(a_i+b_i), and conditional on occupancy
the unit/half type probabilities are a_i/x_i and b_i/x_i,
independently across pairs. Thus all quantities for a finite graph
are piecewise polynomial/rational in the a_i,b_i, with breakpoints
only at alpha x_i. This reduces the continuous-weight search to two
weights per guard pair without changing pair presences or local rows.

For one star prefix with a center guard c and one distinct strong leaf
guard group l, let Y be the group's target mass and t in [1/2,1] the
largest leaf's fraction. Its group throughput is at least
min(1,R_l/t). Put z=1/(2t) in [1/2,1]. At endpoint residuals, the
recourse lower-model payoff table is:

             leaf 0   leaf 1/2   leaf 1
 center 0       0         0          0
 center 1/2     0        1/2        1/2
 center 1       0         z          1.

The center local row is a_c+b_c/2+S<=1; the largest leaf row is
a_l+b_l/2+tY<=1. This small table is the exact two-pair object to
optimize for a common-mode correction. It retains the center's
half-unit occupied residual which the binary-chord shortcut loses.

For explicit algebra set p=alpha(a_c+b_c), r=alpha(a_l+b_l), and let
h_c=b_c/(a_c+b_c), h_l=b_l/(a_l+b_l), with zero-presence fractions
defined arbitrarily. The independent-mode lower-model value is

  I=(1-p)(1-r+z*r*h_l)+(p*h_c/2)(1-r+r*h_l).

The common-mode value, with categorical types independent of U, is

  C=1-p+(h_c/2)(p-r+r*h_l)                  if p>=r;
  C=1-r+z(r-p)h_l+(p*h_c*h_l/2)             if p<=r.

The local constraints become p(1-h_c/2)<=alpha(1-S) and
r(1-h_l/2)<=alpha(1-tY), with p,r<=alpha. Differential evolution
over these parameters, using S=Y as the adverse center allowance,
found no violation of (3/4)I+(1/4)C>=alpha Y for one strong group.
This is numerical evidence, not an analytic certificate, and does
not control several interacting groups.

There is an exact dimension reduction before any numerical search:
I and C are nonincreasing in p and r at fixed h_c,h_l,t. This follows
by differentiating the two displayed formulas; for example,
-dI/dp=(1-r+z r h_l)-(h_c/2)(1-r+r h_l)
 >=(1-r)/2, while each derivative of C in p or r is nonpositive
because z>=1/2 and h_c,h_l<=1. The derivative of I in r is also
nonpositive because z h_l<=1 and h_l<=1. Thus the adverse values are

  p=min{alpha, alpha(1-S)/(1-h_c/2)},
  r=min{alpha, alpha(1-tY)/(1-h_l/2)}.

For a fixed prefix mass Y, increasing the whole-star load S only
decreases the admissible center presence, so S=Y is adverse. A
one-group proof can therefore focus on Y,t,h_c,h_l alone, split by
the regions p>=r and p<=r and the two saturation boundaries.

One candidate extremal family emerging from this reduction has t=1/2,
the leaf guard purely unit weight with original presence 1-Y/2,
and center weighted load 1-Y. For Y<=1/2, the center has original
presence one with half-weight conditional fraction 2Y. For Y>=1/2,
it is purely half weight with presence 2(1-Y). Direct substitution
gives the following exact HYBRID SLACK for this family:

  (2-5Y+3Y^2)/12,       0<=Y<=1/2;
  (2-5Y+4Y^2)/24,       1/2<=Y<=2/3;
  (1-Y)^2/6,           2/3<=Y<=1.

All values are nonnegative and the pieces agree at their joins.
These formulas prove feasibility only for this particular family;
the claim that it minimizes over all t,h_c,h_l is unproved.

WHY TWO UNIT-GUARD LAYERS DO NOT IMMEDIATELY SOLVE IT

In a monotone quantile coupling of endpoint types, R_i is the average
of nested unit-free indicators with effective unit presences a_i and
a_i+b_i. Concavity then gives an average of two binary recourse values.
But the original fluid target y need not split into two locally
feasible unit-guard targets whose average is y. Consider a single
fluid edge uv of target 1/2. At u put one half-weight guard of
presence one, so its two layer allowances are 1 and 0. At v put one
unit-weight guard of presence 1/2, so both layer allowances are 1/2.
The original row is tight at both vertices. A two-layer target split
y=(y^(1)+y^(2))/2 would require y^(2)=0 at u and hence y^(1)=1,
but v's first-layer allowance is only 1/2. The decomposition is
impossible even though the fixed hybrid on this one-edge instance
has enough recourse. Thus applying the audited unit-star theorem
separately to the two binary layers is not a valid general proof.

CURRENT GAP

The three-level endpoint reduction removes continuum weight choices,
but the hybrid law still needs a common-mode estimate that pays for
center/leaf complementarity while controlling correlation among
multiple leaf groups. The one-group payoff table is tractable but
does not by itself bound an arbitrarily branching fluid graph. No
one shared heavy law for unequal-load star forests is proved here.
