# Unbounded-degree tiny star forests: candidate physical transfer

**Status (2026-10-03): scratch restricted-case candidate, pending a full independent audit and the five-hour protocol.** This is not an unrestricted weighted edge-coloring bound. It combines the analytic one-star lemma in `one_star_reciprocal_policy_lemma.md` with the weighted-defective finite-palette construction in `weighted_defective_palette_bridge_conditional.md`.

Assume the combined graph is bipartite, the simple support of heavy edges is a matching, and the simple support of physical tiny edges is a star forest (isolated edges included). Tiny parallel multiplicities, star degrees, graph order, and heavy parallel multiplicities are unrestricted. The proposed outcome is an integer coloring with `(4/3+epsilon)N+O_epsilon(1)` colors for sufficiently small maximum physical tiny weight `tau` and sufficiently large local bin count `N`, with thresholds independent of the graph order and smallest positive tiny weight.

Aggregate parallel tiny edges into endpoint bundles, and reserve internal tiny edges of a matched heavy pair proportionally to that pair's residual as in `connected_dual_local_policy_audit.md`. The remaining external tiny support stays a star forest. For each external bundle `e=uv`, orient it so `v` is a leaf. Apply the explicit local policy with `K=4/3`, cap `12y_e`, tentative load at the center `u`, and denominator `max(1,L_u)`; the leaf's only tentative load is its own coefficient and is already included in `L_u`. The one-star note proves its coordinate expectation at least `(3/4)y_e` for both uncapped and cap-active probes.

This expectation survives matching-heavy paired states directly. A center on the left sees competitor neighbors on the right, and every heavy pair has only one right endpoint; thus its competitor activations come from distinct independent heavy pairs. The probe leaf's heavy partner lies on the left and cannot be another neighbor of the center. An internal edge to the center's own heavy partner was reserved separately. After Bernoulli interpolation of each real residual, the policy is pointwise feasible, capped by `12y_e`, and depends only on the center pair and its neighbor pairs. Its one-color mean has the required coordinate lower bound without a two-sided reciprocal-max inequality.

The finite-palette construction in the weighted-defective note applies with the two-sided local function replaced by `1/max(1,L_u)`: strong/weak threshold, third-power base types, weighted conflict sublabels and bad-weak reserve, constant blocks of `r=t+2`-wise independent field symbols, exact heavy bucket counts, and Harris--Srinivasan balancing of weak center loads. For a good edge, retain weak variables whose pair IDs lie in its strong local set, average the other weak Bernoulli load using its `O(gamma)` conditional variance, and approximate the univariate reciprocal by a degree-`t` polynomial. The block average of the remaining local polynomial equals its product-state expectation because its pair IDs have distinct final types. The `3tau` pseudo-heavy slack supports source-preserving integer tiny rounding; bad weak bundles and leftover heavy identities occupy `O_epsilon(epsilon N)+O_epsilon(1)` additional colors.

This candidate avoids the open uncapped two-sided analytic inequality because every external tiny edge has a leaf endpoint. The finite-palette argument still requires a line-by-line proof audit, particularly the physical loss bookkeeping and exact row-balancing application, before any theorem claim.
