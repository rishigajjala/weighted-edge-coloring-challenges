# Shared-heavy free sets and the factorization obstruction

**Interim mechanism analysis.** This is a physical bipartite construction framework and a scoped barrier, not a new lower bound above four-thirds.

Let H consist of two disjoint, oppositely oriented copies of `K_{D-r,D}` with unit-weight heavy edges. Its high vertices have H-degree `D`; its low vertices have H-degree `D-r`. There are `D` low vertices in each bipartition shore. Join the two low shores with a simple bipartite core graph C of medium edge weight `2/5` and degree at most `2r`. At every low vertex, local bin packing requires at most `(D-r)+ceil(2r/2)=D` bins; every high vertex requires `D`. Thus `bp=D` when C is `2r`-regular.

In any hypothetical `D`-coloring, each high vertex uses every color on an H edge. In each H component and each color, the H edges form a matching saturating all `D-r` high vertices, leaving exactly `r` of that component's `D` low vertices free. Each low vertex has `D-r` H edges and `2r` C edges; it is free in exactly `r` colors and must carry exactly two C edges in every free color. Therefore, for each color, the `r+r` free low vertices on the two shores must support a 2-regular C subgraph, and these per-color subgraphs must decompose every C edge exactly once. This is a concrete route to a global obstruction beyond the local bin number.

There is a simple parameter filter: edge-color H in `D` colors, properly edge-color C in `2r` ordinary matchings, and group the C matchings in pairs. This uses at most `D+r` weighted colors. Thus this framework cannot exceed `4/3` whenever `r<=D/3`; any improvement requires `r>D/3` and a quantitative failure to reuse heavy colors.

The dense crown and complete designs are too factorable. The exact physical coloring MILP in `irregular_heavy_crown_coloring_milp.py` found `chi=bp=D` for the crown C=`K_{D,D}` minus a matching at `(D,r)=(5,2)` and `(6,3)`. At `D=6,r=3`, it also found 8-colorings for all four tested 4-regular complements of a 2-factor (cycle partitions `6`, `4+2`, `3+3`, `2+2+2`); these checks do not prove a family theorem.

For the minimal `r=2` case, a very simple way to forbid every free-set 2-factor is to make the 4-regular C graph C4-free: on two free vertices per shore, a simple 2-factor can only be the complete `K_{2,2}`. But a 4-regular C4-free bipartite graph on `D+D` vertices requires `D>=13`. Indeed each of the `D` vertices on one shore contributes `binom(4,2)=6` pairs of neighbors, and no pair on the other shore can occur twice, so `6D<=binom(D,2)`. At `D>=13` the trivial separate-palette coloring uses `D` heavy colors and `r=2` core colors, giving ratio `(D+2)/D<=15/13<4/3`. Thus girth alone cannot push this r=2 framework past four-thirds; a successful construction needs a higher deficit fraction and a failure of the *simultaneous factor decomposition*, not merely the absence of one small factor.
