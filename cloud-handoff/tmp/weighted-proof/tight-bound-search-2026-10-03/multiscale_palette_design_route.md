# Multiscale finite-palette design: a conditional route and its aliasing gap

**Status (2026-10-03): exploratory, not a proof.** This extends the bounded-degree seed construction toward arbitrary tiny support. It isolates exact tools that appear usable and a local-state aliasing issue that remains unresolved. It assumes the unproved radius-one `3/4` activation policy from `connected_dual_local_policy_audit.md`.

## Strong and weak neighboring pairs

Aggregate parallel tiny targets between each vertex `v` and the one opposite-shore endpoint of a heavy pair `P`; write `Y_(v,P)` for that normalized load. Let a neighbor be **strong** at `v` if `Y_(v,P)>gamma`, and weak otherwise. Since the total tiny target at `v` is at most one, it has fewer than `1/gamma` strong neighboring pairs. After contracting each heavy pair to a supervertex, the graph of strong interactions has maximum degree `O(1/gamma)` and its third power can be properly colored with `T=O(gamma^-3)` types.

For the local policy on an external tiny edge `e=uv`, each weak pair `P` contributes at most `B_(v,P)=sum_{f=vx,x in P}b_f<=12Y_(v,P)<=12gamma` to the tentative load at `v`. In the binary auxiliary activation conditional on real residuals, the sum of weak contributions has conditional variance at most `12gamma * sum_P B_(v,P)<=144gamma`. The reciprocal-max function is 1-Lipschitz in each argument, so replacing a weak binary load by its conditional mean changes the flow on `e` by at most `O(sqrt(gamma))b_e`, uniformly in the heavy residual state. The conditional mean is additive in pair residuals: `m_v(c)=sum_(weak P) B_(v,P) R'_P(c)`.

## Blocks, limited independence, and exact heavy stock up to a small reserve

Fix a target approximation `zeta`. Choose a polynomial degree `t=t(zeta)` that approximates the Lipschitz function `(x,y)->1/max(1,x,y)` uniformly on the bounded tentative-load square; elementary bivariate Bernstein polynomials already give a finite `t=poly(1/zeta)`. Choose `gamma` afterward so small that `sqrt(gamma log(L/gamma))` is much less than `zeta`, where `L` is defined next; since `log L=O(t log(1/gamma))`, this is numerically possible for fixed `t` as `gamma log(1/gamma)->0`.

Take a prime-power `q` larger than `T` and the inverse desired final error, let `r=t+2`, and index a block of `L=q^r` colors by `x in F_q^r`. Assign each strong-graph type a distinct Vandermonde linear form `ell_t(x)`, so any at most `r` distinct types have independent uniform field values over the block. Each heavy pair `P` chooses a block-specific additive shift `s_P in F_q` and maps `ell_type(P)(x)+s_P` to bucket-heavy states. The joint category law of at most `r` **distinct types** is exactly product over block colors for every set of shifts. A polynomial of total degree at most `t` in the strong-pair contributions therefore has the same block average as under independent product states, provided the relevant pairs have distinct types (or repeated types are otherwise controlled).

Map each pair's field value to bucket `b` on `m_(P,b)=floor(q n_(P,b)/M)` of the `q` possible values, where `n_(P,b)` is the physical identity count of bucket `b` and `M` is the main palette size. Every block then uses each bucket exactly `m_(P,b)L/q` times, independent of the shift. Across `M/L` blocks, these slots use at most the available physical identities. The leftover heavy identities at any pair number less than `K M/q`; because the heavy simple support is a matching, all leftovers can be colored in at most `K M/q` additional heavy-only colors. Choosing `q` sufficiently large makes this an arbitrarily small coefficient cost, uniformly in graph order. Rounded-down main heavy marginals only increase pseudo-residual capacities, so the capped one-color local target remains feasible.

For each block choose the shifts `s_P` with a Harris--Srinivasan-type nonnegative-row proposition, using independent pair shifts as source variables and rows `(v,c)` for `m_v(c)`. A row contribution is at most `12gamma`, its expected sum is at most `12`, and one source pair's total contribution over all vertex/color rows of a block is at most `24L`. After scaling by `12gamma`, the proposition has `R=O(1/gamma)` and `D=O(L/gamma)` and yields simultaneous additive error

`O(sqrt(gamma log(L/gamma)) + gamma log(L/gamma))`

for every weak-load row, independent of the number of vertices or heavy pairs. Choosing shifts separately block by block keeps `L` fixed even when `M` grows and retains the exact per-block bucket counts. This is the reason to use constant-length blocks instead of one global pair permutation: a global source would have `D=O(M/gamma)` and bring back `log M`.

## The local-state aliasing gap

The strong graph third-power coloring makes all strong neighbors within one strongly adjacent edge's local set distinct types. For a **weak** edge `e=P-Q`, however, `P,Q` and their respective strong neighbors can reuse types. Equal linear forms and shifts then correlate categorical states even over a complete block. This can lower a particular edge's flow: for a two-spoke star whose two same-shore leaves are perfectly correlated, the local-rule ratio can be about `0.5464` when both target edges are about `0.2357`. If the probe target is restricted to at most `gamma`, numerical optimization gave `0.75-3.375gamma+o(gamma)` in that family, suggesting but not proving an absorbable weak-edge penalty. The weak-load row balance controls the aggregate weak competitor contribution, but it does **not** automatically control the correlation of a weak edge's endpoint with a strong competitor.

One possible repair is a random scrambling of each type's `q` field symbols before the bucket quantile map. For any nonzero shift difference, a suitable scrambling can make every pair of shifted interval indicators approximately independent uniformly over all shifts and thresholds (a random permutation plus bounded differences and a union bound over `q^O(1)` tests). Equal shifts produce comonotone residuals, favorable for the two endpoints of an external tiny edge. Yet higher-order repeated-type coincidences can include one endpoint and a strong competitor on the same shore, whose positive correlation is harmful. A quantitative lemma showing these weak-edge alias effects are `O(gamma)` relative to the probe, even in the presence of other strong competitors, is still missing. Without it, the limited-independence block argument cannot be claimed as a full graph-order-independent finite-palette theorem.
