# Arbitrary bipartite heavy support and a fluid matching: a 3/4 transfer

**Scoped result.** Let the combined graph be bipartite. Heavy physical types have weights in `[1/2,1]`, and a one-color heavy set must be a matching; arbitrary parallel types and arbitrary bipartite heavy support are allowed. The remaining edges are uncapped divisible fluid, and their simple support is a matching disjoint in vertices. At a local point, heavy marginals `x_h` obey `sum_{h incident v} x_h<=1`. For each fluid vertex pair `uv`, let `Y_uv` be the sum of its fluid-type targets; the load constraints are `Y_uv+sum_{h incident u} w_h x_h<=1` and the analogous inequality at `v`. Then there is a one-color distribution with heavy marginals `3x_h/4` and fluid marginals `3y_e/4` simultaneously. The statement is about divisible uncapped fluid and fractional one-color hull inclusion; it is not an integer palette or physical small-edge theorem.

## Simultaneous nesting of heavy coverage

Orient every heavy edge from left to right and each marked fluid pair as `u∈L, v∈R`. Put `d_z=sum_{h incident z}x_h`. There is a distribution over heavy matchings with edge marginals `x` such that, at every marked pair, the two heavy-coverage indicators are nested in the direction of their means: `I_u>=I_v` if `d_u>=d_v`, and `I_u<=I_v` otherwise.

To prove this, make a capacitated directed circulation. For every original vertex use a capacity-one vertex gate. Each physical heavy type is an arc `u→v`; source arcs enter left gates, right gates drain to a sink, and a sink-to-source arc closes circulation. Add a return arc `v→u` for each marked pair. If `d_u>=d_v`, prohibit the drain from `v`, permit source inflow at `u`, and give the return arc fractional flow `d_v`; the source inflow at `u` is `d_u−d_v`. If `d_u<d_v`, prohibit source inflow at `u`, permit drain at `v`, and give the return arc flow `d_u`; the drain at `v` is `d_v−d_u`. Unpaired vertices use their ordinary source/drain arcs. Put the prescribed `x_h` on heavy arcs. Conservation holds, the gate capacities hold by `d_z<=1`, and the source/sink total balances. Every arc and gate capacity is integral (zero or one, apart from an unrestricted sink-to-source arc), so integral-circulation decomposition gives a convex combination of integral circulations preserving all physical-heavy arc marginals. In an integral circulation the heavy arcs form a matching. At a marked pair with `d_u>=d_v`, every flow unit entering `v` must use the return arc and then a heavy arc out of `u`, hence `I_v<=I_u`; the reverse case is symmetric.

## Scalar loss and global empty coin

Fix a marked pair and write `U,V∈{0}∪[1/2,1]` for its random heavy loads under the full-marginal law above. Suppose `I_v<=I_u`; the other orientation is symmetric. Set `Z=U−V`. On the event where only `u` is covered, `Z∈[1/2,1]`; where both are covered, `Z∈[-1/2,1/2]`; elsewhere `Z=0`. Consequently, pointwise `Z_+ + 2Z_-<=1`, and therefore

`E max(U,V)−max(EU,EV)=min(EZ_+,EZ_-) <= (EZ_+ + 2EZ_-)/3 <= 1/3`.

The fluid recourse capacity on this pair is `min(1−U,1−V)=1−max(U,V)`. Since the local target satisfies `Y_uv<=1−max(EU,EV)`, the full heavy law gives expected fluid recourse at least `Y_uv−1/3` (a negative right side is harmless). Now use this full-heavy law with probability `3/4`, and with probability `1/4` use the empty heavy matching. In the empty state every fluid pair has capacity one. The expected recourse on each pair is at least `(3/4)(Y_uv−1/3)+1/4=3Y_uv/4`. Because fluid pairs have disjoint endpoints, all their maximum recourses can be routed at once. Thin that total flow to expectation `3Y_uv/4`, and split it among parallel fluid types in fixed proportions `y_e/Y_uv`, to obtain exact coordinate marginals `3y_e/4`. The heavy marginals are exactly `3x_h/4`.

The proof uses a single global heavy law and empty coin, so the result is simultaneous over all marked pairs. It gives no recourse guarantee for two fluid edges sharing a vertex: their maximum flows cannot generally be routed independently.

The nesting construction itself does not extend mechanically to a fluid star. Take heavy edges `c_L a_R` and `c_L b_R`, each with marginal `1/2`, plus an independent heavy edge `v_L t_R` with marginal `1/2`. Put fluid spokes `v_L a_R` and `v_L b_R`. The three vertices `v,a,b` all have heavy-coverage mean `1/2`. Pairwise nesting along both fluid spokes would therefore force `I_v=I_a=I_b` almost surely. Yet `I_a+I_b=1` in every heavy matching with these prescribed marginals, because the first two heavy marginals sum to one at `c`. Thus no heavy law can meet both nesting requirements. This refutes the proposed nesting certificate, not any fluid-star transfer inequality.

The same calculation gives a weight-sensitive variant. If all positive heavy weights lie in `[h,1]` for `1/2<=h<1`, then on the both-covered event `|Z|<=1−h`, and pointwise `Z_+ + Z_-/(1−h)<=1`. Hence the Jensen gap is at most `(1−h)/(2−h)`. Mixing the full law with one empty-heavy state gives scaling `(2−h)/(3−2h)`, which equals `3/4` at `h=1/2` and tends to one as `h→1`. At `h=1` the gap is zero directly.
