UNEQUAL-LOAD MIXED STAR FORESTS: COMMON-LAW AUDIT
5 October 2026

MODEL AND CLAIM LEVEL

Heavy simple support is a matching. Each heavy pair has at most one selected
categorical physical type, with original total presence at most one and each
positive weight in [1/2,1]. Divisible fluid lies on vertex-disjoint stars,
with per-edge caps C_e >= K y_e, K=4/delta, delta in (0,1/2]. Local rows
sum_h w_h x_h + sum_e y_e <= 1. Write alpha=2/3 and
beta=alpha/(1+2delta). This note concerns one-color convex-hull laws only;
there is no physical palette transfer or general branching conclusion.

1. POSITIVE COMMON LAW FOR EQUAL HIGH LOADS

Suppose every star in a guard-connected component has the same whole-star
load S>1/2+delta. Put T=4(1-S)/3 and q=1-T. Draw one Bernoulli bit B with
Pr(B=1)=q. Conditional on B, draw a separate U_P for EVERY heavy pair P,
independently across pairs: uniform on [T,1] for B=1 and on [0,T] for
B=0. Pair P is occupied if U_P <= alpha x_P, with its categorical type
chosen independently conditional on occupancy in proportion to x_h.

Each U_P is marginally uniform, hence each physical heavy identity has
exact marginal alpha x_h. At any star center c, local feasibility and
w_h>=1/2 give x_c<=2(1-S), so alpha x_c<=T; B=1 makes its center pair
free. Within that star, all other leaf guard pairs are independent
conditional on B, even when a pair also supplies a vertex of a DIFFERENT
star. If its center's heavy partner is a leaf of the same star, the
single-star audit handles that leaf separately. If a pair joins two star
centers, it is the center pair for both and remains free on B=1. Thus each
star sees exactly the heavy-law marginal needed by
oct5_mixed_center_single_star_conditional_assembly_audit.md. Its
all-profit support inequality holds, and the disjoint stars' statewise
recourse values add. Separation gives exact alpha fluid marginals under
the one common heavy law; thinning gives exact beta heavy and fluid
marginals. Different guard-connected components can be sampled separately
when each has its own constant high load S.

When ALL fluid-vertex loads are at most 1/2+delta, the low-load common
quantile/product-flow branch of that audit already applies globally;
star loads need not be equal. The equal-high-load statement above is the
new gluing scope. It does not combine an unequal high component.

2. WHY THE EXACT CONDITIONAL CONSTRUCTION CANNOT BE GLUED AT UNEQUAL S

Let stars A and B have T_A<T_B, both in (0,1). Suppose two distinct
heavy guard pairs P,Q each connect one leaf of A to one leaf of B. The
audited construction for star A requires a center-free bit B_A such that
U_P and U_Q, conditional on B_A, are independent uniforms on
[T_A,1] when B_A=1, and B_A={U_P>T_A}={U_Q>T_A} a.s. Star B requires
analogous B_B at T_B, so B_B={U_P>T_B}={U_Q>T_B} a.s. The bits are nested.
Put r=(1-T_B)/(1-T_A), strictly between zero and one. The marginal
conditional law says Pr(B_B=1|B_A=1)=r. Independence of U_P,U_Q
conditional on B_A=1 instead says the common event B_B=1, equal to
{U_P>T_B} intersect {U_Q>T_B}, has probability r^2. This is impossible.
This proves incompatibility of the EXACT per-star conditional uniform and
independence requirements. It does not rule out a different common law or
the desired convex-hull inclusion.

3. LEGAL TWO-STAR FAILURE OF NAIVE NESTED STRATIFICATION

Take delta=1/20 (K=80), so both loads below are high. Star A has center
a and five leaves a_1,...,a_5, each spoke target 3/20. Its load is
S_A=3/4 and threshold T_A=1/3. Star B has center b and five leaves
b_1,...,b_5, each spoke target 23/200. Its load is S_B=23/40 and
threshold T_B=17/30. For each i, one weight-1 heavy pair (a_i,b_i)
has original presence 17/20; its weighted load 17/20 plus A leaf's
fluid load 3/20 is exactly one, and plus B leaf's fluid load 23/200
is 193/200 <=1. The center a has a weight-1/2 heavy pair to an unused
dummy vertex, of original presence 1/2; its weighted load 1/4 plus
S_A=3/4 is one. Center b likewise has a weight-1/2 guard to another
dummy, of presence 17/20; its weighted load 17/40 plus S_B=23/40
is one. All heavy pairs are disjoint; their original presences are <=1.
Caps exactly K y_e are ample to supply one unit on any free A leaf.

Consider the tempting global nested-stratum law: sample W uniform on
[0,1], partition it at T_A and T_B, and, conditional on W's stratum,
draw each pair's U_P independently uniform within that same stratum.
Each U_P is uniform marginally; the center-free bits are nested. In the
strata [0,T_A] and (T_A,T_B], every A leaf pair is occupied, since its
alpha-scaled presence is alpha*(17/20)=17/30=T_B. Its weight is one,
so every A leaf has zero residual. Center a is occupied on the lower
stratum (residual 1/2) and free on the middle stratum, but either way
the A recourse is zero. In the upper stratum every A leaf and its center
are free, so optimal A prefix recourse is one. Therefore the exact
expected A unit-profit recourse is 1-T_B=13/30, strictly below
alpha*S_A=1/2. The center's occupied residual has been accounted for:
it cannot move flow into zero-residual leaves. This defeats this fixed
stratified law's alpha support-function target. It is NOT a
counterexample to some other law or to the convex hull. In fact, the
separate fixed 3/4-independent + 1/4-common hybrid law has global LP
retention about 0.9290858368 on this same two-star instance, well above
2/3 (floating computation only).

4. WHAT TRANSFERS TO THE UNIVERSAL FIXED HYBRID PROOF

The fixed hybrid uses one global mode coin: with probability 3/4 sample
heavy pairs independently at alpha x_h; with probability 1/4 use one
common occupancy uniform, drawing categorical types independently
conditional on occupancy. This law is defined for arbitrary unequal
stars and guard links. If its one-star support inequalities were proved
for mixed weights, summing statewise star recourse would immediately
give a common-law star-forest theorem. Existing exact per-edge-cap
global LP diagnostics found no failure, but do not certify it.

The independent-mode LEAF GROUP estimate does extend analytically.
For a profit-prefix group with mass Y, largest leaf fraction t in
[1/2,1], and common pair residual R, the single-star audit gives
throughput at least c min(1,R/t), c=min(1,KY). Let x be pair presence,
mu its mean occupied weight, so x*mu<=1-tY. For a strong group
(c=1), weights >=1/2 imply occupied R<=t, whence

  E min(1,R/t) = 1 - alpha*x*(mu+t-1)/t.

The quantity x*(mu+t-1) is at most t*(1-tY): if x<=1-tY use
mu<=1 and x*(mu+t-1)<=xt; if x>=1-tY use
x*mu<=1-tY and subtract x*(1-t). Consequently

  E min(1,R/t) >= (1+2tY)/3 >= (1+Y)/3.

For a weak group, E R=1-alpha*x*mu >=(1+2tY)/3, and
min(1,R/t)>=R, so its expected throughput is at least
c*(1+Y)/3. Distinct leaf pairs are independent in independent mode.
At the center, min(R_c,L)>=R_c*min(1,L) pointwise, and
E R_c>= (1+2S)/3. Thus the independent-mode product estimate of the
audited UNIT-guard hybrid star proof carries over for external leaf
groups. A center-paired leaf can be Bernoulliized jointly with the
center for that mode; this requires a written full-prefix check if used.

The COMMON-mode strong-group estimate from the unit proof is false for
mixed center guards. A one-group legal example has S=Y=1/2, two leaves
of target 1/4 each in the same weight-1 guard pair of presence 3/4,
and a center weight-1/2 guard of presence one. Both center and leaf
rows are tight. With U uniform, the common-mode recourse is zero for
U<=1/2, then 1/2 for 1/2<U<=2/3, then one for U>2/3, yielding
5/12. The binary/unit proof would require (1+Y)/3=1/2 from its strong
group. The independent-mode recourse is 1/3, so the actual hybrid
recourse is (3/4)(1/3)+(1/4)(5/12)=17/48 > alpha*Y=1/3.
The lost common-mode activation is offset here by the independent
mode's real center residual. A mixed proof must budget those two
effects together; merely replacing each residual by a Bernoulli bit
or importing the unit common-mode bound is invalid.

A possible quantitative replacement, NOT PROVED, is the one-strong-group
bound E_common min(R_center,R_leaf/t) >= (1+Y)/3-(1-S)^2/3 for
Y<=S and t in [1/2,1]. Differential evolution over single-type
weights/presences found a worst gap ratio 1/3 at the preceding
S=Y=1/2 example; 200,000 random endpoint-categorical samples also
found no violation. These computations do not establish the bound.
The quadratic loss may be affordable because the unit one-strong-group
hybrid proof has slack (1-s)^2/6 when its only group has s=Y.

One additional diagnostic caveat: oct5_mixed_star_hybrid_probe.py uses
min(ports*R,12*Y) for a two-leaf group. With unequal leaf targets the
true capped sum is min(R,12*y_1)+min(R,12*y_2), which can be smaller.
The stronger oct5_mixed_guard_hybrid_lp_probe.py models per-edge caps
and is unaffected by this simplification. No numerical result here is
a proof of the universal fixed hybrid.

OPEN REQUIREMENTS

Prove an all-prefix common-mode correction strong enough to combine
with the independent-mode bound for a guarded mixed center, including
center-partner leaves and categorical weights. Then use star additivity
and support separation for unequal star forests. Branching fluid
supports, unrestricted heavy support, integer colors, and physical
tiny-edge palette compatibility remain beyond this note.
