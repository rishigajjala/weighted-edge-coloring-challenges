BOUNDED SOURCE LABELS FOR THE MIXED STAR-FOREST HYBRID: AUDIT
5 October 2026

SCOPE

This audits a bounded-dependence implementation of the fixed heavy law
used in oct5_mixed_star_forest_fixed_hybrid_beta_eps_transfer.txt.
Let kappa be the cap multiplier (C_e>=kappa y_e), set
delta=1/(16kappa), q=ceil(2kappa), and let a heavy source mean one
categorical guard pair. The positive fluid support is a
vertex-disjoint star forest, so a source's two endpoints touch at
most two stars. Exact physical palette realization is not proved here.

MARKING AND BASE COLORS

At each star mark its center source, if guarded. Also mark each leaf
source whose WHOLE-star fluid target mass on leaves of that source is
at least delta. The whole-star leaf masses sum to at most one, so the
number of marked sources at a star is at most
D=floor(1/delta)+1. Define a conflict edge between two sources that
touch the same star whenever at least one is marked there. For any
induced set A of sources, the number of conflict edges contributed
at a star is at most D times the number n_s(A) of its sources in A.
Each source touches at most two stars, hence

  |E(A)| <= D sum_s n_s(A) <= 2D |A|.

Every induced subgraph has average degree at most 4D, so the conflict
graph is 4D-degenerate and has a proper base coloring with at most
4D+1 colors. At a star, a locally marked source's base color is
unique among ALL sources touching that star.

SUBLABEL LOAD BALANCING

For each base color, allocate its sources among q sublabels. Impose a
star-row only at a star with no marked source of this base color.
Each coefficient in such a row is that source's whole-star leaf mass,
strictly below delta, and the row's total source mass is at most one.
The uniform fractional assignment 1/q per sublabel is feasible with
row bound 1/q. Each source×sublabel variable occurs in its source
equality and at most two star rows. Standard basic-solution iterative
rounding fixes all zero variables permanently, fixes all unit
variables, and otherwise finds an active row with at most four
fractional variables: n fractional variables, m<=n/2 source
equalities, at least n-m>=n/2 active star rows, and at most 2n row
incidences. Dropping that row raises its eventual mass by at most
4delta. Thus every unmarked star×label has whole-star leaf mass

  <=1/q+4delta <=1/(2kappa)+1/(4kappa)
                     =3/(4kappa).

At a star with a marked source of a base color, that source is the
only source of that base color there; whichever sublabel it receives
is a singleton at that star. The total number of final labels is
at most (4D+1)q=O(kappa^2).

SHARING SEEDS WITHIN LABELS

In independent mode, sample one independent pair of uniforms
(U_label,V_label) per label. Source i in that label is occupied when
U_label<=alpha x_i; conditional on occupation, map V_label through
its own categorical inverse CDF. Each physical type h retains exact
one-color marginal alpha x_ih. In common mode use one global U for
all source occupancies, but independent V_label per label for type
choices. Sources in one label may correlate, but sources in distinct
labels have independent (U,V) in independent mode and independent
type seeds conditional on global U in common mode.

Every prefix-strong leaf group has prefix mass >=1/kappa, hence its
whole-star mass >=1/kappa>delta and is marked at that star. Its
label is unique among all sources at that star. The center source is
also marked, so the all-strong proof retains exactly the required
within-star independence. A prefix-weak locally marked source may be
alone in its label, with Z_i<1. All other prefix-weak sources of one
label have total WHOLE-star mass <=3/(4kappa), so their aggregate
lower-model rank Z_label=sum_i kappa Y_i min(1,R_i/t_i) lies in
[0,3/4]. Its mean is at least kappa times its prefix mass divided
by three, by linearity of expectation. Distinct label blocks and
the center are independent in independent mode. Thus the original
weak product estimate holds with labels as blocks:

  product_label E(1-Z_label)
      <=exp(-kappa W_guarded/3).

The common-mode event U>alpha still makes every source absent,
regardless of label sharing, so the all-free weak-cap estimate is
unchanged. A center source can be a leaf source of another star, but
it touches at most two stars and the local mark/uniqueness argument
still applies independently at each.

UNGUARDED LEAVES: REQUIRED ADDENDUM

An unguarded fluid leaf is not a heavy source and has no seed. Its
whole target mass must not silently disappear from W. A prefix-strong
unguarded leaf (prefix target >=1/kappa) supplies one unit whenever
the center is free, so its star prefix already has expected rank at
least E R_center >=alpha times the total prefix mass. For all
prefix-weak unguarded leaves, let W0 be their total target mass and
use one deterministic superblock Z0=min(1,kappa W0). Its failure
factor obeys

  1-Z0 <= exp(-kappa W0/3)

(indeed it is zero when kappa W0>=1, and otherwise 1-kappa W0 is
smaller than the exponential). This block is independent of every
label and the center. It restores the exact weak product bound with
W=W_guarded+W0. Proof-only virtual guards can still be used in the
analytic all-strong comparison, but they should not be counted as
physical sources or require additional palette labels.

LIMITS

This establishes bounded LABEL count and preserves the fixed-hybrid
star recourse estimates, including shared guard pairs between stars.
It does not by itself specify an exact finite color calendar for
arbitrary rational/real type marginals. Even with O(kappa^2) labels,
the number and denominators of U/V threshold intervals can depend
on the graph and its probabilities; that calendar-size/stock issue
requires a separate construction. Nothing here handles arbitrary
branching fluid support.

More precisely, with M physical heavy identities, each label's
(U,V) outcome is constant on at most (M+1)^2 rectangles cut by its
occupancy and inverse-CDF thresholds. The joint independent-mode
support is at most (M+1)^(2L), and the common-mode support at most
(M+1)^(L+1), where L=O(kappa^2). This is polynomial in graph size
for fixed kappa, but the exponent and exact rational denominators
are not bounded independently of kappa or bit complexity.
