UNIVERSAL FIXED-HYBRID TRANSFER FOR MIXED-HEAVY STAR FORESTS
5 October 2026 -- independent audit of full-prefix assembly

THEOREM (divisible-fluid convex hull; no physical palette claim)

Let a finite loopless multigraph have heavy categorical pairs whose
simple support is a matching. At pair i, physical heavy types h have
targets x_ih>=0 with x_i=sum_h x_ih<=1 and weights w_ih in
[1/2,1]. Let the positive fluid support be a vertex-disjoint star
forest; parallel fluid identities are allowed. Let target y>=0 obey
every local weighted row

  sum_{heavy h incident v} w_h x_h + sum_{fluid e incident v} y_e <=1.

Let every fluid identity e have per-state cap C_e>=K y_e.
Fix 0<epsilon<=1/6, alpha=2/3, beta=alpha-epsilon, and
K>=64/epsilon. Then there is one joint law of feasible one-color
heavy choices and divisible fluid flows with exact coordinate means
beta x_h for every physical heavy type and beta y_e for every fluid
identity. Stars may have unequal whole-star loads, and a heavy pair
may join vertices of different stars. The theorem is an abstract
one-color fluid-hull result. It does not yet provide a finite physical
palette or handle unrestricted heavy support or arbitrary branching
fluid support.

FIXED HEAVY LAW BEFORE PROFITS

Sample one global mode coin. With probability 3/4, choose each heavy
pair independently: select type h with probability alpha x_ih,
or select none with the remaining probability. With probability 1/4,
draw one global uniform U; occupy pair i iff U<=alpha x_i and,
conditional on occupancy, select type h with probability x_ih/x_i,
independently across pairs. Each mode gives every physical heavy
identity marginal alpha x_ih. The law depends on x, not on fluid
profits, star loads, or caps. Its real residual R_i is 1-w_h if
occupied and one if absent, shared by the pair's two endpoints.

ALL-STRONG PREFIX INPUT

The exact endpoint reduction and all-strong prefix proof are recorded
in oct5_endpoint_reduction_universal_mixed_law.txt,
oct5_mixed_one_strong_group_fixed_hybrid_audit.txt, and
oct5_mixed_all_strong_star_fixed_hybrid_audit.txt. Their conclusion:
for any one star profit prefix whose positive external leaf-pair
groups all have prefix mass Y_i>=1/K, and whose center-pair partner
is not a prefix leaf, the expected optimal unit-profit prefix rank
under the fixed heavy law is at least alpha times its total target
mass. This remains valid if the star has other, omitted fluid edges:
their load only tightens center/leaf heavy allowances, improving the
lower bound. If the prefix has no positive edge, the claim is trivial.
When a center or leaf is actually unguarded, a proof-only virtual
guard pair to a dummy vertex may be added and saturated. Sampling that
virtual pair under the same mode/U can only lower the star's residual
and recourse; it does not change the actual heavy law or its marginals.
Each unguarded leaf gets a separate singleton virtual group.

WEAK GROUP FACTS

Fix any star prefix of total target mass Y<=1. The center's original
weighted heavy load is at most 1-Y, so in either mode

  E R_c=1-alpha*(center heavy weighted load)
       >=(1+2Y)/3>=1/3.                              (1)

Separate any prefix identities to the center's own heavy partner
into a bundle of target mass y0 (possibly zero). For every other
heavy leaf pair, group all its prefix leaves (one or two) and let
Y_i be their total prefix target mass. Call an ordinary group weak
when 0<Y_i<1/K and strong otherwise. Let W be the sum of ordinary
weak masses. Each weak group has a feasible rank lower model

  Z_i=K Y_i * min(1,R_i/t_i) in [0,1],              (2)

where t_i in [1/2,1] is its largest-leaf target fraction. The
single-star cap lemma proves (2) pointwise from C_e>=K y_e. In
independent mode, ordinary groups are independent of one another
and of R_c. The independent-mode leaf-group calculation in
oct5_unequal_star_common_law_audit.txt gives

  E Z_i>=K Y_i/3.

As min(1,sum_i Z_i)>=1-prod_i(1-Z_i), independence yields an
ordinary-weak-only rank lower bound

  I_weak >= E R_c * [1-exp(-K W/3)].                 (3)

The center inequality min(R_c,L)>=R_c min(1,L) justifies its factor.
All other groups and the center-partner bundle can be ignored in (3).

LARGE WEAK MASS

If W>=6/K, (3) and (1) imply

  I >= (1+2Y)/3 * (1-exp(-2)).

In common mode the event U>alpha has probability 1/3 and makes
EVERY heavy pair free. The ordinary weak groups then have aggregate
available caps at least K W>=6, so the prefix center can carry one;
thus C>=1/3. The hybrid rank is at least

  H >= (1+2Y)/4*(1-exp(-2))+1/12 >= alpha Y.       (4)

The final inequality is affine in Y and has its smallest gap at
Y=1; it follows, for example, from exp(-2)<=1/6. The argument is
unchanged if the center's own partner is also a prefix leaf.

CENTER-PARTNER BUNDLE

If K y0>=1, the bundle can carry R_c in every state because both
endpoints share that residual and its aggregate caps are at least
one. Then expected prefix rank is at least E R_c>=alpha Y by (1).
If 0< K y0<1, the same bundle can carry at least
min(R_c,K y0)>=K y0 R_c in every state. The y0=0 case is empty.

LARGE PREFIX WITH LITTLE WEAK MASS

Suppose Y>=1/8 and W<6/K. If y0=0, discard ordinary weak groups and
apply the all-strong result to the remaining ordinary strong prefix.
Its expected rank is at least alpha(Y-W)
>=alpha Y-alpha*6/K>=beta Y because K>=32/epsilon.

If 0<K y0<1, discard the center-partner bundle too. Its mass is
y0<1/K, so the remaining ordinary strong prefix has target mass
greater than Y-7/K. The all-strong result gives expected rank at
least alpha(Y-7/K)>=beta Y when
K>=56alpha/epsilon=112/(3epsilon); K>=64/epsilon suffices.
This use of all-strong transfer is valid even if no strong group
remains: in that case the bound's right-hand side is nonpositive,
because Y<W+y0<7/K; the actual rank is nonnegative.

SMALL PREFIX

Suppose Y<=1/8. A strong center-partner bundle is already handled.
If its weak bundle has y0>=Y/2, its pointwise lower bound and (1)
give expected rank at least K y0 E R_c>=K Y/6>=alpha Y for K>=4.

Otherwise the ordinary group mass is at least Y/2. If any ordinary
group is strong, use that group alone in independent mode: its
expected rank into a free center is at least 1/3, and E R_c>=1/3.
Thus I>=1/9 and the hybrid H>=3I/4>=1/12>=alpha Y.

If every ordinary group is weak, then W>=Y/2. Equation (3) gives

  H>= (3/4)I_weak
    >=(1/4)[1-exp(-K Y/6)] >=alpha Y.               (5)

The last difference is concave in Y on [0,1/8] and vanishes at
zero, so it suffices to check Y=1/8. The endpoint condition is
exp(-K/48)<=2/3, i.e. K>=48 ln(3/2)<24, implied by K>=64/epsilon.

These cases exhaust every prefix and prove expected prefix rank at
least beta Y under one and the same heavy law. In many cases the
stronger alpha Y bound holds; epsilon pays only for discarding a
small amount of weak mass.

FROM PREFIXES TO JOINT MARGINALS

For a fixed residual state of a star, greedily fill fluid identities
in descending profit order subject to individual caps, leaf residual
capacities, and the center residual. This maximizes every profit
prefix's unit rank simultaneously. Layer-cake therefore converts the
prefix bound into

  E[maximum feasible star fluid profit]
      >=beta * sum_{e in star} q_e y_e

for every nonnegative profit vector q. The fixed heavy law is common
to all stars. Because the stars are vertex-disjoint, their statewise
feasible recourse polytopes and optimum profits add even if a heavy
pair links two stars. Summing gives the global support-function
inequality. The set of conditional feasible mean fluid vectors is
compact, convex, and downward closed; separation and coordinatewise
discarding yield a conditional flow policy with exact means beta y.
The heavy identities still have means alpha x. Finally, independently
delete each selected physical heavy identity with retention beta/alpha;
this increases residual capacities and preserves the chosen fluid
flow. The resulting heavy means are exactly beta x, completing the
one-color fluid-hull theorem.

CONSTANTS AND PHYSICAL SCOPE

For the earlier parameter beta=alpha/(1+2delta), one has
epsilon=alpha-beta=4delta/[3(1+2delta)]. This theorem would require
K>=48(1+2delta)/delta, not merely K=4/delta, and its stated
epsilon<=1/6 corresponds to delta<=1/6. Larger target thinning can
be obtained from the epsilon=1/6 case by further deletion if
K>=384, but this does not recover the earlier small cap constant.
Most importantly, the theorem concerns divisible-fluid one-color
recourse. A separate finite-palette transfer and physical tiny-edge
rounding are needed before asserting an integer weighted edge
coloring bound.

The limiting factor 2/3 is sharp even within this abstract class:
take one unit-weight heavy guard pair across two leaves with target
presence 1/2, and two fluid spokes of target 1/2 from those leaves
to one unguarded center. Any common scaling gamma makes the guard
present with probability gamma/2, so expected total fluid recourse
is at most 1-gamma/2; its required total fluid mean is gamma.
Hence gamma<=2/3.
