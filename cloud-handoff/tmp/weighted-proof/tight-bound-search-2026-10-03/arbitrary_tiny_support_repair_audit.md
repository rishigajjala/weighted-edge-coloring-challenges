# Arbitrary tiny support: near-degree packing and the deletion-union obstruction

**Status (2026-10-03).** A self-contained all-tiny weighted-edge-coloring lemma holds for arbitrary multigraph support. It does not by itself extend the heavy-matching plus tiny-star `3/2` integer transfer to arbitrary tiny support: the normal-color overload estimate controls the amount a vertex *needs* removed, but not the degree of the union of edges removed to satisfy both endpoints.

## Theorem: arbitrary tiny graph packs near its weighted degree

Let `H` be any loopless weighted multigraph, with every edge weight at most `tau<1/14`. Put `W=max_v sum_{e incident v} w_e`. Then

`chi'_w(H) <= ceil(W/(1-14 tau))`.

The proof is constructive in polynomial time in the physical edge input and the chosen number of colors. Parallel edges cause no change.

More precisely, for every integer `M>=1`, the edges can be assigned to `M` colors such that the load at every vertex in every color is at most `W_v/M+14 tau`, where `W_v` is that vertex's weighted degree. To see this, make each physical edge `e=uv` a shared source with `M` states: one state `(w_e,w_e)` and `M-1` states `(0,0)`. Permuting those states is exactly choosing one color for the physical edge, consistently at both endpoints.

For `M=K` a power of two, recursively pair the states within every source, giving one state in each child half. The pair-difference matrix is two-sparse, with entries of magnitude at most `tau`; the real two-sparse signing lemma, scaled by `tau`, chooses all orientations so that every split's hub-load difference is at most `2 tau`. If a block has `k` colors, total hub load `T`, and mean `mu=T/k`, then each child total is at most `T/2+tau`. Following any branch to a single color gives load at most `mu+2 tau(1-1/k)<mu+2 tau`.

For general `M`, decompose the source states into common dyadic color blocks, using exact-quota subset rounding at each stage. From a residual list of `R` states per source, select `K`, the largest power of two at most `R`, states per source. Let `x_ei=K/R` initially and preserve each source equation `sum_i x_ei=K`. Keep active only vertex-load rows with at least five live variables. With `m` live variables, unfinished source equations number at most `m/2` because their right sides are integers, and active vertex rows number at most `2m/5` because each variable has at most two vertex incidences. A kernel direction therefore exists until all variables become zero or one. Each released row has at most four live entries of magnitude at most `tau`, so its final error from `(K/R)` times its original residual load is at most `4 tau`.

Fix a vertex and write its original per-color mean as `mu=W_v/M`. At a residual block of size `R` and load `T_R`, set `D_R=T_R-R mu`. Initially `D_M=0`. A nonfinal dyadic selection obeys `T_K=(K/R)T_R+epsilon`, `|epsilon|<=4 tau`, and leaves `R'=R-K<R/2` states with `D_R'=(R'/R)D_R-epsilon`. Thus `|D_R|<=8 tau` throughout, and every selected power-of-two block obeys `|T_K-K mu|<=12 tau`; the last block does too. Balancing inside a block of size `K` gives each color load at most `T_K/K+2 tau<=mu+12 tau/K+2 tau<=mu+14 tau`. This proves the precise statement. Choosing `M=ceil(W/(1-14 tau))` makes every load at most one; if `W=0`, there are no edges.

This is a near-degree decomposition for **arbitrary** tiny support, with an error depending on `tau` but neither graph size nor `M`. It could repair a deleted tiny-edge set if its maximum *deleted weighted degree* were bounded.

## Why the star-forest repair step does not transfer automatically

Let `N>=2`, take `tau=1/k` with integer `k>=15`, and form a center `c` with `Nk` leaves `ell_i`, each joined to `c` by a tiny edge of weight `tau`. Attach to each leaf a distinct external vertex `x_i` by a unit heavy edge. The heavy simple support is a matching, and the tiny support is itself a star. The maximum local bin count is `N`: the center has tiny weight `N`; each leaf needs two bins (unit heavy plus positive tiny).

Consider an `N`-row proposed placement that puts exactly `k` tiny spokes in each row at `c`, and puts each leaf's unit heavy edge in the same row as its own tiny spoke. Every row at `c` has load exactly one and zero overload. Each leaf has one row of load `1+tau`, hence total overload `tau`, within the half-deficit estimate. Nonetheless every tiny spoke must be deleted from this particular proposed placement to make its leaf feasible. The union of deleted tiny edges has weighted degree `N` at `c`, twice the intended `N/2` repair budget. The graph itself has a coloring with `N` rows: use `N` rows for center's spokes and place each leaf heavy edge in any different row, possible because `N>=2` and the heavy edges form a matching.

Thus the existing source-list overload theorem and the all-tiny near-degree repair theorem do **not** compose by simply deleting edges at overloaded endpoints. A valid arbitrary-tiny-support extension needs a stronger normal-color construction, or a global relocation rule, that bounds the weighted degree of the *union* of removed tiny edges by `N/2+o(N)` at every vertex. The displayed example is an obstruction to that proof step, not a lower-bound example for the proposed `3/2` chromatic coefficient.

## A second palette obstruction: globally nested heavy colors

Even a direct `M=3N/2` palette scheme cannot simply put every heavy pair's edges into the same first `p` colors, where `p` is that pair's heavy multiplicity. For an integer `N>2`, let a center `c` have `N` neighbors `u_i`; between `c,u_i` put `k=1/tau` parallel tiny edges of weight `tau`, so the tiny weighted degree at `c` is `N` and at every `u_i` is one. Join each `u_i` to a distinct external vertex `x_i` by `N-1` parallel unit heavy edges. The heavy simple support is a matching, and every vertex has a local `N`-bin packing. If all heavy pairs use the first `N-1` palette colors, every tiny edge is forbidden at `u_i` in those colors. All tiny weight `N` incident to `c` must fit into the common remaining `M-N+1` colors. This is impossible for `M<2N-1`, in particular for `M=ceil(3N/2)` when `N` is sufficiently large. Yet the graph has an `N`-color packing: assign the `N` tiny bundles at `c` distinct colors, and at each `u_i` place its `N-1` heavy edges in the other colors. Thus a successful direct extension must disperse heavy color sets across pairs; nesting them creates an artificial factor-two bottleneck.

## A fractional direct-palette identity (not a construction)

For a prospective palette of total mass `M=3N/2`, let `H_v` be the total heavy weight incident at vertex `v`, `t_v` its total tiny weight, and `rbar_v=1-H_v/M`. The local packing lower bound gives `H_v+t_v<=N`, hence

`t_v/M <= rbar_v-1/3`, with `rbar_v>=1/3`.

This leaves an absolute one-third unit of average residual capacity at every vertex even for nonunit heavies. Suppose one randomly selects at most one heavy edge per heavy pair in a palette color, independently between distinct pairs, with each physical heavy edge having probability `1/M`. Its residual `R_v=1-heavy_weight_present_at_v` has mean `rbar_v` and lies in `[0,1]`; both endpoints of a heavy pair share the same state. For a tiny edge `e=uv` between distinct heavy pairs, the tentative divisible flow

`X_e=(w_e/M) R_u R_v/(rbar_u rbar_v)`

has expectation `w_e/M`. Conditional on `R_v=r`, the expected tentative total tiny load at `v` is `r t_v/(M rbar_v) <= (2/3)r`. A group involving one **neighboring vertex** contributes at most `3/4` in any color: maximize `min(a-1/3,b-1/3)/(ab)` over `a,b in [1/3,1]`. But one neighboring **heavy pair** has two endpoints, both sharing a heavy state, and tiny edges from `v` may join both. Its total weight is bounded by `min(t_v,t_u+t_{u'})`, not `min(t_v,t_u)`. The corresponding bound is `max min(a-1/3,2(b-1/3))/(ab)=18-12 sqrt(2)≈1.0294`, attained at `a=(1+sqrt(2))/3`, `b=(2+sqrt(2))/6`. Thus one correlated neighboring-pair group can alone exceed unit capacity. The earlier `3/4` pair-group claim was false; it applies only to one neighboring vertex.

These are verified moment and support bounds only. Tentative loads may still exceed residual capacity in some heavy states; trimming would lose tiny-edge marginals, and the bounds do not establish a feasible fractional coloring. A one-third average slack is a possible input for a stronger correlated coupling or b-matching argument, not a proof of the target coefficient.

## Extremal local-LP support, with exact scope

Consider the **individual-box relaxation** in load coordinates `t_e`: nonnegative fluid edge loads, vertex inequalities `sum_{e incident v} t_e<=1`, and upper boxes `t_h<=w_h` for heavy physical edges. Fix an extreme point `t`. Let `F` contain all positive tiny edges and all heavy edges with `0<t_h<w_h`; remove every edge fixed at zero or at its heavy upper bound. The columns of the ordinary unsigned incidence matrix of `F`, restricted to tight vertex rows, must be linearly independent. Otherwise a nonzero signed perturbation on `F` would preserve every tight vertex equation and, for sufficiently small positive and negative steps, remain inside all slack inequalities and interior boxes, contradicting extremality.

For every connected component `C` of `F`, the rank of its full unsigned incidence matrix is `|V(C)|-1` if `C` is bipartite and `|V(C)|` if it is nonbipartite. Independence on a subset of rows therefore implies `|E(C)|<=|V(C)|-1` in the bipartite case and `|E(C)|<=|V(C)|` in the nonbipartite case. A connected simple-support multigraph with those edge counts is a tree or a unicyclic graph; the unicyclic graph must have an **odd** cycle, because an even cycle gives a column dependence. Thus every free-positive component is a tree or odd unicyclic component, including parallel-edge two-cycles only as forbidden even cycles. Positive heavy edges at upper box form a matching in simple support, because their weights exceed one half and the heavy simple support is a matching; at most one can be upper-bound positive in each pair.

This is a structural property of extreme points of the **individual-box relaxation**. It does **not** prove that scaling by `2/3` enters the convex hull of patterns with indivisible heavy edges. In fact, the individual-box relaxation is not the intended local mixed model when parallel heavy physical edges are allowed, even if their simple support is a matching. For two parallel heavy edges each of weight `h=0.51` between the same two vertices, `t_1=0.51` and `t_2=0.49` satisfy both vertex capacities and the individual boxes. Their physical presence variables are `x_1=1` and `x_2=0.49/0.51`, whose sum exceeds `1.96`. After `2/3` scaling their required presence marginals still sum to about `1.307>1`, impossible because a unit-capacity color can contain at most one of the two heavies. The correct local relaxation must include a **categorical pair constraint** `sum_{h in pair} x_h<=1` (or an equivalent actual one-heavy-per-bin law). These extra group rows invalidate the simple ordinary-incidence pseudoforest rank count.

The pseudoforest reduction is therefore directly relevant only under a further assumption of at most one heavy physical edge/type at each matched pair, or after a separate reduction that already enforces the categorical heavy law. It is a proof mechanism to investigate, not a reduction of the full parallel-heavy multigraph problem. Even in that restricted case, coupling fractional heavy presences across the tree/odd-cycle fluid components while retaining every tiny-edge marginal remains unproved.

There is a useful refinement when all tiny edges are truly fluid and have no per-color upper bounds. Every nontrivial tree component (at least two free edges) must have a heavy-upper-bound port at every leaf except possibly one. Indeed, the free-edge columns require at least `|V|-1` tight vertex rows, so at most one vertex in the tree is slack. A tight leaf without an upper-bound heavy edge has its sole free edge at load one, forcing its neighbor to have no other positive incident free edge and contradicting nontriviality. In particular, a component without any heavy-upper-bound port cannot be a nontrivial tree: it is either one unit-load edge or an odd cycle (whose no-port all-tight solution has half a unit on each edge). Thus the arbitrary fluid tiny graph reduces, at local-LP extreme points, to heavy-port trees and odd-unicyclic components linked by a matching of bound heavy edges. This reduction does not survive literal physical tiny-edge per-color upper boxes, whose bound-positive support can be arbitrary; a later small-edge approximation would be needed for exact stock.
