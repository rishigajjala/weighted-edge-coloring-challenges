# Paired fluid double-stars with a reservable central edge

**Status: proved, conditional extension.** The vertexwise half-deficit and exact two-thirds local-hull inclusion extend from fluid star forests to a vertex-disjoint union of stars and double-stars, provided the two centers of each double-star either both have fixed capacity one or are the two endpoints of the **same** discrete pair. All other discrete pairs may connect leaves across the two sides, across components, or to vertices outside the fluid support, with arbitrary prescribed residual laws in `[0,1]`. This is a divisible-fluid result with no fluid-edge supply cap beyond vertex capacities. It does not cover a double-star whose centers have distinct random residual sources, nor an arbitrary fluid forest.

The scalar input is the independent-base/private-sum lemma in `upper_arbitrary_paired_star_forest_half_deficit.md`: if `U` is a sum of independent `[0,1]` variables, private variables `Y_i∈[0,2]` have prescribed marginals, and `E U+Σ E Y_i≤1`, the private laws can be coupled to the base, preserving the base's full joint law, so that `E(U+ΣY_i−1)_+≤1/2`.

## Construction on one double-star

Let the centers be `u,v`, central edge `h=uv`, and let `y` be any mean-feasible nonnegative fluid flow. At every leaf `w` incident to center `a`, use the preliminary flow

`Z_aw=(y_aw/r_w)R_w` if `r_w=E R_w>0`, and `Z_aw=0` otherwise.

At a fixed leaf this means `Z_aw=y_aw`. Each preliminary leaf flow has mean `y_aw` and respects its leaf's state capacity. There is no edge supply bound to violate.

If both centers are fixed, reserve the deterministic central flow `Z_h=c=y_h`. Each center has a remaining capacity `H=1-c`.

If the centers are the endpoints of one pair, let their common residual be `X`, with `r=E X`. For `r>0`, reserve `Z_h=cX`, where `c=y_h/r≤1`; if `r=0`, all incident target flow is zero and the component is trivial. Each center has remaining state capacity `H=(1-c)X`. The center-pair residual `X` is sampled as one global source, independently of all other globally sampled pair sources. Private leaf-pair sources may subsequently be coupled to `X`.

For either center, partition its leaf edges into (i) singleton sources, namely fixed leaves and pair sources appearing only once among that center's leaves; and (ii) private leaf pairs whose two endpoints are both leaves of that center. A private pair contributes a combined preliminary load `Y_i=(a_i+b_i)R_i∈[0,2]`. Let `S` sum singleton preliminary loads. The singleton sources are mutually independent and independent of the center residual. In the fixed-center case put `U=1-H+S=c+S`. In the paired-center case put `U=1-H+S=1-(1-c)X+S`. Both are sums of independent `[0,1]` variables: `c∈[0,1]` is a constant summand in the first case, and `1-(1-c)X∈[c,1]` is a summand in the second. Mean feasibility at the center is precisely

`E U+Σ_i E Y_i = 1-E H+Σ_(leaf edges) y_e ≤1`.

Apply the scalar lemma separately to the two centers. At each one it bounds the expected branch overload by `1/2`. Trim *only its leaf-edge preliminary flows* by the common factor `min(1,H/Σ Z_aw)`, with factor one for zero load. Leave the reserved central flow untouched. The resulting flow respects every state capacity. The total branch loss at a center equals its branch overload, so its expected incident deficit is at most `1/2`. Each leaf has one fluid edge and loses no more than its center's total branch loss. The central edge has zero deficit. Thus the resulting expected flow `t` obeys `0≤t≤y` coordinatewise and `Σ_(e incident a)(y_e−t_e)≤1/2` at every vertex.

## Global coupling and coordinatewise inclusion

Sample independently every pair source that is *not* a two-leaf private pair within one center-side branch or within one ordinary-star component. This includes each double-star center pair, each ordinary-star center-to-leaf pair, and every pair joining different branches or components. A source sampled in this stage occurs at most once among the leaves of any one center-side branch, and no center source can be a leaf source of its own double-star. The scalar lemma preserves the full joint law of these sampled singleton sources within each branch. Conditional on all globally sampled sources, sample its private leaf-pair variables using the lemma's conditional kernel. The private pair sets of different branches are disjoint, so all kernels coexist, even if cross-branch pair links form cycles. For ordinary star components use the original star-forest construction and the same globally sampled shared sources. Recover discrete packet identities from their conditional laws given residuals.

For a point `(x,y)` in the full local configuration hull, the construction gives state-feasible packet/fluid patterns with discrete mean `x` and fluid mean `t≤y`, whose deficit `d=y−t` has incident sum at most `1/2` everywhere. Hence `2d` is feasible in the all-empty discrete state. Mix the packet/flow patterns with probability `2/3` and the deterministic empty-packet flow `2d` with probability `1/3`; the exact mean is `(2x/3,2y/3)`. Therefore `(2/3)P_local⊆P_global` coordinatewise for this class, and every nonnegative-profit local/global gap is at most `3/2`.

The half constant is asymptotically sharp even for a strict double-star: give one fixed center two leaves linked by a Bernoulli-`1/2` discrete pair, each target leaf flow `(1-c)/2`, and central target flow `c>0`; attach a small positive fixed-leaf flow to the other center. The two leaf flows can be served only in the pair's active state, so their combined expected service is at most `1/2`. The central edge's expected service is at most its target `c`. The first center's target incident flow is one; hence **every** admissible coupling and flow has deficit at least `1/2−c`, approaching `1/2` as `c↓0`. This witness concerns the half-deficit construction; the original star triangle gives the exact `3/2` mixed-model gap.

## Boundary and exploratory check

The proof uses a statewise reservation of the central edge that is simultaneously compatible with both center capacities and has its full target mean. Distinct arbitrary center residual laws need not permit such a reservation. For example, with one center constantly `1/2` and the other Bernoulli-`1/2`, any central state flow has mean at most `1/4`, although its mean-capacity target may be `1/2`. This blocks this reservation proof, but is **not** a counterexample to the vertexwise half-deficit statement: its central-edge loss is only `1/4`, and another construction may compensate elsewhere.

More precisely, for distinct center residual laws `X,Y`, the largest possible expected central flow over all couplings is `M=∫_0^1 min(Q_X(t),Q_Y(t))dt`, attained by a common quantile. Indeed `E min(X,Y)=∫_0^1 P(X≥s,Y≥s)ds`, and each integrand is at most the smaller of the two marginal survival probabilities; the common-quantile coupling attains every such bound simultaneously. Thus a target `y_h>M` forces central deficit at least `ℓ=y_h−M`. The branch flow must then have expected overload at most `1/2−ℓ` at *both* centers. This cannot follow from a generic “half minus mean slack” strengthening of the scalar lemma. If the remaining hub capacity is identically one and one private two-leaf load is `2B` with `P(B=1)=(1−ℓ)/2`, its mean load is `1−ℓ` but its unavoidable overload is `(1−ℓ)/2>1/2−ℓ` for `ℓ>0`. That scalar example is **not** a realizable central-mismatch counterexample: a hub with residual one can reserve its target central flow exactly. Any extension to distinct centers must use the relation between forced central loss and the residual hub-capacity laws.

As a diagnostic only, a finite-state LP was run on 4- and 5-vertex paths and 6- and 7-vertex double-stars, using randomly generated binary pair residual laws, pair matchings, and mean-feasible target flows. The LP jointly optimized the pair-state coupling and state-feasible fluid flows while minimizing the maximum vertex deficit. Four thousand random instances per topology produced no optimum above `1/2`; this is neither an exhaustive check nor evidence for arbitrary residual laws. The proved result is the reserved-center class above.

A second diagnostic used fractional two-point residual laws with **distinct random center sources** on 6- and 7-vertex double-stars, 5,000 random cases per topology, biasing the central target above `M` when possible. It also found no optimum above `1/2`. A separate search sampled 30 extreme mean-flow points for each of 300 two-point-law choices on a six-vertex double-star, again without a violation. These LP runs provide no theorem or exhaustive certificate.
