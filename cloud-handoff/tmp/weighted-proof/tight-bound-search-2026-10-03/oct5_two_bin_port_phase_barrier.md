# Two-bin port phases: an exact local surrogate and a replication diagnostic

5 October 2026. Scratch only. This does not improve either unrestricted coloring bound.

## A phase encoding that really survives repeated physical edge types

Split each physical vertex `v` into two ports of weighted degree one; partition its incident edge types into the two port bins. Suppose each type has `m` physical parallel copies. At `v` the `m` copies of its first port bin and `m` copies of its second port bin certify `bp_v<=2m`, and weighted degree `2m` proves equality. Consequently `bp(G)=2m` if all vertices have this form (private leaves may have smaller local counts). A one-color pattern has integer quantities `z_e` of each type with `sum_{e at v}w_e z_e<=1`. Repetition is therefore included in the global one-color oracle; restricting to `z_e∈{0,1}` would be unsound.

If the two port bins were the only locally saturating configurations, they would encode a binary phase at each vertex. An edge's port membership can differ at its two endpoints because its numerical weight can participate in different unit-sum complements. A cycle of these port-incidence signs may be frustrated even when the original support is bipartite. This is a plausible way to turn incompatible local configurations into a true one-color support gap. However, the local bin menu is downward closed, and near-saturating mixed configurations soften the phase constraint.

## Exact 2+3 mixed-bin surrogate

Let a local port menu consist of a two-item bin `{a,1−a}` and a disjoint three-item bin `{b,c,d}`, with `b,c,d>1/4` and `b+c+d=1`. Both bins have weight exactly one; the three-item bin is genuinely rank three and all its entries are medium. Then there is a feasible configuration different from the two port bins whose total **weight** is at least `5/6`. If the larger pair item is at least `5/6`, take it alone. Otherwise put `s=min(a,1−a)>1/6`. Some `x∈{b,c,d}` lies in `[s−1/6,s+1/6]`. Indeed, if `s>=1/3`, every triple item is `<1/2<=s+1/6`, so absence from the interval makes all three `<s−1/6<=1/3`, contradicting their sum one. If `s<1/3`, each triple item exceeds `1/4>s−1/6`, so absence makes all three `>s+1/6>1/3`, again contradicting their sum. If `x<=s`, the larger pair item together with `x` is feasible and has weight `1−s+x>=5/6`. If `x>=s`, the smaller pair item together with the other two triple items is feasible and has weight `s+1−x>=5/6`. These are mixed configurations. The bound is sharp at pair weights `(1/2,1/2)` and triple weights `(1/3,1/3,1/3)`.

This lemma blocks a tempting *local* argument that a phase mismatch in a 2+3 all-medium menu always wastes at least `1/4` of a weight-one bin. It does not provide a global packing or a `6/5` gap upper bound, and it does not apply to arbitrary separating profits `q`: the surrogate is measured in physical weight. More complicated globally inconsistent menus or high-profit edges can still matter.

## Stronger coordinatewise replacement of a repeatable rank-three bin

There is an exact all-profit obstruction to treating the original three-item bin as a unique phase after multiplicity blowup. Order its weights as `1/4<x<=y<=z<1/2`, with `x+y+z=1`. Besides the original `(1,1,1)` bin, the following *integer-multiplicity* configurations always fit:

* `P_1=(0,0,2)`, since `2z<1`;
* `P_2=(1,2,0)`, since `x+2y<=x+y+z=1`;
* `P_3=(2,0,1)`, since `2x+z<=x+y+z=1`.

Mix `P_1,P_2,P_3` with probabilities `1/3,4/9,2/9`. Every one of the three types then has expected multiplicity **exactly `8/9`**. Consequently, for *every* nonnegative profit vector `q`, at least one configuration other than `(1,1,1)` has profit at least `(8/9)(q_x+q_y+q_z)`. The construction uses at most two copies of any one type, so it is available for all physical multiplicity blowups `m>=2`; one-copy-only analysis misses it. This is a local coordinatewise alternative law, not a joint law across the two endpoints of each physical edge.

The factor `8/9` is sharp for this universal local statement. Take exact rational weights `(x,y,z)=(30,34,36)/100` and profits `(q_x,q_y,q_z)=(2,3,4)/9`. Enumerating all nonnegative integer triples with weighted sum at most one shows the original `(1,1,1)` has profit one, while the largest profit of any other bin is exactly `8/9`, attained by each of `P_1,P_2,P_3`. Thus a rank-three bin can remain strictly optimal, but its competing repeatable bins are never more than a ninth below it in any nonnegative profit direction. In particular, a proposed parity gadget that needs *every* color to use one exact 3-item phase must quantify how these alternatives affect the global coupling; uniqueness of the local full bin alone is insufficient.

## Targeted port-graph diagnostic, not a certificate

`oct4_port_phase_frustration_probe.py` constructs a 3-regular bipartite graph on port nodes, chooses positive edge weights with every port row summing exactly one, pairs ports into physical vertices, then solves the *integer* one-color maximum-weight pattern with every type allowed up to `floor(1/w_e)` copies. All physical vertices have weighted degree two, so the local half-point `x_e=1/2` belongs to every exact local hull: each vertex independently chooses one of its port bins with probability `1/2`. Thus this is a valid repeatable physical model, not merely a single-copy LP point. The objective `q_e=w_e` tests whether its all-edge-weight support ratio can exceed `4/3`.

For 12 ports per shore, 20 seeded random trials with perturbation amplitude `.20` yielded best local/global support ratio `6/5.858948818...≈1.0241` (the mixed-integer solver reported optimality). A separate polar column-generation script, `oct5_port_profit_polar_probe.py`, optimized all nonnegative profits on five 6-port and three 8-port sampled networks, with exact integer-multiplicity pricing. The best ratios were `15/14≈1.0714` for 6 ports and about `1.0556` for 8 ports; a 10-port run hit the iteration cap and yielded no conclusion. These weak ratios illustrate how integer repetitions and mixed feasible bins restore near-full value even when ideal port phases may be frustrated. The sampled graph and floating weights are only diagnostics; no exact instance or universal bound is claimed. The next meaningful test would use a deliberately structured, rationally specified port network and an exact asymptotic `bp=2m` certificate. Weight-profit random phase networks alone have not exposed the desired >`4/3` obstruction.

## A targeted rank-shifting prism also loses its phase obstruction

`oct5_role_gradient_prism_probe.py` deliberately builds two bipartite six-port cycles with small-edge weight sequence `(.27,.29,.31,.31,.29,.27)` and joins corresponding ports by a third edge completing each port's weight-one triple. This creates role-shifting edges: a common numerical small weight may be the smallest item at one port and the middle item at the other. Pairing ports into three physical vertices on each shore can make the **port-only** phase model highly inconsistent. For the displayed most-frustrated pairing, the best one-color pattern constrained to choose at most one complete port bin per physical vertex has weight `1.74`, versus local half-point objective `3`. If one ignored mixed bins, this would suggest a spurious ratio `3/1.74>1.72`.

The actual integer-multiplicity oracle has a full-weight pattern of value `3`, saturating all six physical vertices. Moreover the unperturbed decimal instance has a two-coloring of one physical copy of every edge type, so its port-only obstruction is already erased by *single-copy* cross-port bins. Perturbing edge weights by small vectors in the port-row nullspace removes most exact cross-bin coincidences while preserving every original port sum one and the role-shift order. Five such perturbations gave weight-objective ratios at most `1.00055`; their all-profit polar values were between `25/24≈1.0417` and `19/18≈1.0556`, with pricing complete. These are numerical diagnostics using floating weights, not an exact global-hull theorem. They show concretely why port-sign frustration is not a valid separating certificate until **all** mixed and repeated configurations are included.

An additional conceptual restriction is that the sharp local `8/9` example has profits proportional to rank `(2,3,4)`. A shared edge changing from smallest to middle rank cannot have both of those sharp local profit values under a single globally consistent edge-profit vector unless the neighboring local scales differ. Around a cyclic role shift, these scales must satisfy a product consistency condition. Thus a scheme that separately maximizes the loss at each port can overstate the globally realizable profit obstruction even before cross-bin repairs are considered.

## Mixed heavy-plus-medium six-cycle

`oct5_mixed_guard_triple_cycle_probe.py` tests the more relevant mixed menu: at each of six bipartite physical vertices one port is a heavy-plus-small bin `(1-s_i,s_i)`, while the other is a three-item medium bin `(s_j,t_{k-1},t_k)`. Three heavy edges form a matching. Each small edge joins an A port to a B port, and the six remaining medium edges form a B-port cycle. The cycle weights are chosen by the exact equations `t_{k-1}+t_k=1-s_j`; every original vertex consequently has weighted degree two and a certified exact-local half-point `x_e=1/2`. The script enumerates the two small-edge endpoint permutations and admissible cycle parameters, then tests the full integer-copy one-color oracle and nonnegative-profit polar by column generation. All proposed physical edges have the same weight at both ends, as required.

This is a targeted test of a guard-forced phase and rank-three core, not a random port graph. Some instances have no two-coloring when one copy of each type is present, despite `bp=2`; their **repeatable** all-profit half-point gap was only about `1.04`–`1.0833` among the inspected most-frustrated cases, while their phase-only objective was `2.255` against local objective `3`. The largest displayed polar ratio was `13/12`, for `s=(.26,.30,.34)`, left permutation `(2,1,0)`, right permutation `(1,0,2)`, and first cycle weight `.255`; it is a numerical LP/MILP result, not an exact certificate. The weight-profit oracle reached at least `2.9349` of `3` in all displayed non-two-colorable cases. Hence this smallest heavy-plus-triple cycle does not supply a >`4/3` lower construction. It also shows why a finite 3-versus-2 color obstruction cannot be scaled without a fresh one-color pattern analysis: after parallel replication, copies can be redistributed into mixed bins.
