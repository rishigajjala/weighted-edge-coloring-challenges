5 October 2026 — residual-overlap route toward full heavy+tiny bounds

STATUS. This is scratch research, not a new unrestricted coefficient.
It isolates the fractional feasibility test that remains after the
audited physical LP rounding, proves a tiny-only palette lemma, and
records a fixed-coloring failure mode. No manuscript or Lean edits.

1. EXACT FIXED-CORE ASSIGNMENT DUAL

Fix a feasible K-coloring of any core J. Let residual capacity at
vertex v and color c be R_vc, and reserve 4τ by setting
R'_vc=(R_vc-4τ)_+. Let T be physical edges of weight at most τ.
Allow tiny edge e=uv in color c only when R'_uc R'_vc>0.
The physical rounding of oct5_arbitrary_heavy_mixed_tiny_physical_palette_audit.txt
works whenever the following fractional LP is feasible:

  z_ec>=0,  Σ_{c allowed for e} z_ec=1  (every tiny physical edge e),
  Σ_{e∋v} w_e z_ec<=R'_vc        (every vertex-color row).

There are no additional stock constraints; the first equality makes
each z_ec<=1. By separation/Farkas, this LP is feasible if and only if
for every nonnegative vertex-color price vector p,

  Σ_{e=uv∈T} w_e min_{c allowed for e}(p_uc+p_vc)
      <= Σ_{v,c} p_vc R'_vc.                                (DUAL)

If an edge has no allowed color, the LP is infeasible. The product
assignment z_ec=R'_uc R'_vc/S_uv from the audit is one sufficient
certificate for this LP. Its exact row condition is

  Σ_{e=uv∈T} w_e R'_uc/S_uv <=1

for each row v,c with R'_vc>0, where
S_uv=Σ_d R'_ud R'_vd. The simpler S_uv>=max(T_u,T_v)
condition is strictly stronger. A better flow can satisfy the LP even
when the product row fails. For a universal heavy+tiny factor, the
remaining task is to choose/recolor a physical heavy palette so
that (DUAL) holds with K near the desired coefficient times bp.

2. TINY-ONLY PALETTE IS ALREADY COEFFICIENT ONE

Let Δ_T=max_vΣ_{e∋v}w_e for a loopless tiny-only multigraph.
For any τ<1/4 and K>=Δ_T/(1-4τ), the uniform fractional assignment
z_ec=1/K has load Δ_T/K<=1-4τ at every row. Applying the same
support-preserving iterative LP rounding adds at most 4τ per row,
so every physical tiny edge receives one color and

  χ'_w(T)<=ceil[Δ_T/(1-4τ)].

This is finite, graph-order uniform, allows arbitrary multiplicity,
and needs no source-preserving packet approximation. It implies that
a separate palette for a tiny subgraph costs its weighted degree
plus O(τ) proportionally. The obstacle in mixing with heavy edges
is the load of tiny edges crossing from high-heavy-load vertices
to low-heavy-load vertices: the former have small tiny degree, but
many of them can send aggregate tiny load nearly N into one low
vertex, so a small global fresh palette does not suffice.

3. WHY A FIXED PROPER HEAVY COLORING NEEDS COORDINATION

Normalize bp scale to N and consider K=3N/2 colors (ignore integer
rounding). At one vertex u, place N unit-weight heavy edges in N
distinct colors. At another v, place N heavy edges: N/2 of weight
one and N/2 of weight just above one half. Arrange v's unit edges
in all N/2 colors where u has no heavy edge, and v's near-half
edges in N/2 colors where u does have a heavy edge. In the remaining
N/2 colors u is occupied and v has no heavy edge. For the idealized
weight exactly 1/2, the product residual overlap S_uv is zero:
every color has zero residual at u or v. With weight just above 1/2,
it remains zero because u is unit-occupied in all colors where v has
positive residual, and v is unit-occupied in the u-free colors.

This is only a *fixed-coloring pattern*, not a physical lower-bound
construction: at u, unit-heavy load N leaves no local room for any
positive tiny edge if bp=N. Replacing a vanishing fraction of u's
unit heavy load by tiny capacity allows a tiny edge but only a
vanishing target there. Moreover, if the heavy stars at u and v
are independent, recoloring v aligns their free colors and repairs
the overlap immediately. The pattern shows that weighted load totals
alone cannot certify a fixed core coloring at coefficient 3/2; a
full theorem needs a favorable physical heavy coloring or a more
adaptive residual-flow assignment. It is not a counterexample to
1.5, 1.75, or any universal heavy+tiny coloring bound.

4. PRECISE LIMIT OF A 1.75 SPLIT ARGUMENT

One tempting route is to call vertices with heavy weighted load
above 3N/4 high. Each such vertex has tiny weighted load below N/4.
The tiny-only lemma would color edges induced by high vertices in a
fresh ~N/4 palette after ~3N/2 heavy colors. But tiny edges from high
vertices to a low vertex can have aggregate load nearly N at that
low vertex, because they may come from arbitrarily many high
neighbors. Thus the same fresh ~N/4 palette cannot color all edges
touching high vertices. The cross edges are exactly where the
fixed-core LP or a coordinated recoloring is needed. This is a
structural obstacle to that split proof, not a lower bound on χ'_w.

5. NEARUNIT TRIANGLE: SMALL OVERLAP BUT EXACT PRODUCT ROW SUCCEEDS

Take a heavy triangle u,v,z with m parallel copies on each side.
Give each uv copy weight 1-2δ and each uz,vz copy weight one, where
0<δ<1/4. Put a tiny physical bundle on uv whose total weight is
2δm, split into items of weight at most τ<<δ and chosen so it can
be packed into the 2δ residual of each of m uv-heavy colors.
At u and v, the heavy weighted load is 2m(1-δ), and the tiny load
is 2δm; at z the two unit-heavy bundles give bp at least 2m.
The intended local bins use one heavy per bin and fill the residual
of each uv-heavy bin, so bp=2m=N when the tiny items divide evenly.

A proper heavy coloring uses K=3m=3N/2 colors, one heavy edge in
each color. All m colors carrying uv heavy have residual 2δ at
both u and v. In colors carrying uz or vz heavy, one of u,v has
zero residual. Ignoring the 4τ reservation, the product overlap is

  S_uv=m(2δ)^2=2δ²N,

whereas T_u=T_v=δN. Thus S_uv>=max(T_u,T_v) fails by an unbounded
factor 1/(2δ) as δ→0. Nevertheless z_ec is uniform over exactly
the m uv-heavy colors. It gives tiny load (2δm)/m=2δ in each of
those colors, exactly their physical residual. The *exact product
row* succeeds at the 3N/2 palette. Reserving 4τ requires a small
additional palette margin or slightly reduced tiny target, and the
example still shows the qualitative point: a tiny product moment
does not imply difficult flow when every active color has a
proportionally small residual. This example is mixed heavy, not the
already solved unit-heavy/tiny case.
